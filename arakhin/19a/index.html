Topic

The end of פרק הֶשֵּׂג יָד and the start of the next פרק: pledging one's weight or height to הקדש

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier discussion: the ברייתא should have used the Torah's words זכר and נקבה. The גמרא then asks why an elderly woman's ערך drops to exactly a third, while an elderly man's drops lower. חזקיה answers with a folk saying: an old man at home is a burden, but an old woman at home is a treasure. After the הדרן, a new משנה teaches that one who pledges 'my weight' pays his weight in silver or gold, whichever he named. The same משנה tells how ירמטיא's mother paid her daughter's weight in gold. It also records an argument between רבי יהודה and רבי יוסי about how to measure the weight of a hand. The גמרא learns that if no metal was named, any material sold by weight in that place counts, as רחבה and רב פפא explain. It then explains the story of ירמטיא's mother: an important person's vow is judged by his status. Finally, רב יהודה splits 'my height' from 'my full height' by following רבי עקיבא, who learns meaning from extra words, and a new question about 'עומדי' continues on the next amud.
