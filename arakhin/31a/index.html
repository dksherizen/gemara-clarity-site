Topic

Redeeming fields and walled-city houses: counting the redemption year, and whether the refund counts as ריבית

Overview

The amud opens by settling two ברייתות about redeeming a field. One allows borrowing to redeem it and redeeming it in parts, and the other forbids both. They follow the רבנן and רבי שמעון. A new משנה then teaches about a house sold in a walled city. The seller may buy it back right away and for twelve months, counted from the first sale. The rights pass to heirs, and the year includes a leap month (רבי says a full solar year). After the year, the house is the buyer's forever, even if he received it as a gift. The גמרא notes that the משנה does not follow רבי, who learns from ימים that the seller must wait two days. It then works out which words in the פסוקים each side uses to teach that the year runs from the sale date, ends at the exact hour of the sale, and includes the leap month. Finally, the משנה calls the refund 'like interest,' while a ברייתא calls it real interest that the Torah permitted. רבי יוחנן says this depends on whether צד אחד ברבית is forbidden (the רבנן) or permitted (רבי יהודה).
