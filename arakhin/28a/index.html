Topic

The limits on making חרם: how much one may dedicate, what counts as one's own, and whether כהנים and לוים may dedicate

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier discussion: the original owner gets first rights to buy back his field from הקדש only when his offer plus the added fifth brings הקדש more money than the other bidder's offer. A new משנה then teaches that a person may make חרם of part of each kind of property, but not all of it. רבי אליעזר says a חרם of everything does not take effect, and רבי אלעזר בן עזריה adds a קל וחומר: a person should certainly not give away all his property to people. The גמרא learns these rules from the wording of ויקרא 27:28. It explains why each category in the פסוק is needed and learns from the word ״בהמה״ that only what a person may always sell can be made חרם. It finds a practical difference between the two opinions in the תקנה of אושא: do not give more than a fifth to צדקה. A second משנה rules that one cannot make חרם of what is not truly his, and records a dispute about whether כהנים and לוים may make חרם. The גמרא concludes that everyone agrees לוים cannot make חרם of land, and they argue only about movables. The amud ends as רבי חייא בר אבין begins teaching that movable חרם may go to any כהן the owner chooses; his proof continues on the next amud.
