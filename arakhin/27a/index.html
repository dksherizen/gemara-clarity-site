Topic

Consecrating and redeeming fields: the wording of the משנה about כהנים and לוים, and the rules for bidding on a consecrated field when there is no יובל

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier discussion. רבי יהודה and רבי שמעון learn from an extra letter in a פסוק that a field due to become ancestral is not treated like a bought field. Next the גמרא asks why the משנה says כהנים and לוים may always consecrate, since a ישראל may too. It answers that the wording simply matches the first משנה of the chapter, and that the real new point is about redeeming. The chapter אין מקדישין then ends. A new chapter teaches that when there is no יובל, the owner of a consecrated field opens the bidding, and the גמרא shows he can even be forced to. Several reasons support this: he loves the field, he has the first right to redeem, and he adds a חומש. The story of the man who bid an איסר (or an egg, according to רבי יוסי) turns out to be an argument about how small a redemption payment may be. A new משנה then rules that a bidder who backs out must cover the loss he causes הקדש, and that the owners win when their bid ties with an outsider's. The amud ends as an outsider bids twenty-one, and the ruling for that case comes on the next amud.

וְהָכָא קְרָא אַשְׁכֻּחַ וּדְרֻשׁי:

'If a field that he bought,'

'which is not his ancestral field,'
