Topic

Assessing a person's value (דמים) versus fixed ערכין: heirs, body parts, 'half' pledges, and pledges on vessels.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a chain of questions about pledging one's own market value (דמים): two separate pledges, one pledge of 'twice my value', and an assessment the court made on its own. A proof from a משנה about a person who pledged his value and died is brought and then pushed off, so that question stays unresolved. Then a new משנה teaches that ערכין are stricter than דמים in one way: heirs pay a ערך after the pledger dies, but not דמים, because the dead have no value. The משנה also covers pledging the ערך of a limb, of a vital organ, of 'half my ערך' versus 'the ערך of half of me', and of another person's value. The גמרא explains that heirs pay a ערך only when the pledger stood in judgment, while דמים still needs an assessment. It brings רב's ruling that pledging a limb's ערך obligates its value according to רבי מאיר. It gives the פסוק sources for the vital-organ rule for both ערכין and דמים, and explains רבי יוסי ברבי יהודה's 'lashes' as a rabbinic penalty. Finally, אביי and רבא explain why the חכמים void a pledge of half a כלי's ערך, and the גמרא shows why the משנה repeats its clause about another person's value.
