Topic

Does the ציץ make an impure offering acceptable when something was done on purpose? The גמרא gives four ways to settle two ברייתות that disagree.

Overview

An earlier ברייתא ruled that in some cases an impure offering is 'not accepted', but a second ברייתא says the ציץ makes impure blood, meat and fat acceptable even when done on purpose. רב יוסף answers that the lenient ברייתא is רבי יוסי, who does not penalize deliberate wrongdoing, as seen with תרומה. The גמרא objects that רבי יוסי holds the ציץ does not work for eaten parts, and tries to save the answer by reversing the names. רב ששת rejects the reversal with a long ברייתא about eating קדשים while impure: it holds that sprinkling does not work for meat that left the courtyard, which is רבי אליעזר's view, and it uses the ציץ for eaten parts. So רב חסדא answers instead that the lenient ברייתא is רבי אליעזר, who also does not penalize, even for קדשים. Finally רבינא and רב שילא each split the cases differently: one says only sprinkling on purpose ruins the offering, and the other says only making it impure on purpose does.

Opening lines

לֹא הוּרְצָה.

It is not accepted.

ורְמִינְהִי:

And they raised a contradiction:

עַל מָה הַצִּיץ מְרַצֶּה?

For what does the frontplate effect acceptance?

עַל הַדָּם וְעַל הַבָּשָׂר

For the blood and for the flesh

The steps (28)

  1. StatementEnd of the earlier teaching: the offering is not accepted
  2. ChallengeA second teaching says the frontplate works even for deliberate impurity
  3. Resolutionרב יוסף: the two teachings follow different opinions

  5. ChallengeNot penalizing is not the same as the frontplate working for eaten parts
