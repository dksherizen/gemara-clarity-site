Topic

Flawed meal-offering vows and the word "מנחה"; the sixty-עשרון limit for one כלי and why; exact measures of the חכמים; the blood of a dead mule.

Overview

The amud opens with זעירי's rule: a flawed vow for a מנחה still counts only if the person said the word "מנחה". רבא challenges this from several cases in the משנה. רב נחמן answers each time that the person did say "מנחה", and רבי שמעון's exemption is explained by רבי יוסי's view that a person's last words also count. A new משנה then teaches that a person may bring up to sixty עשרונים in one כלי. Someone who vows sixty-one splits them, sixty in one כלי and one in another. The משנה bases this on the ציבור's largest daily total of sixty-one, but רבי שמעון says the real reason is that only up to sixty can be mixed with oil. The גמרא repeats this debate between רבי יהודה בר אילעאי and רבי שמעון. רבי שמעון brings a פסוק and shows that every measure of the חכמים has a sharp cutoff, and רבי זירא explains that a מנחה must at least be fit for mixing. The amud ends with a story about measuring a dead mule's blood, a challenge that the blood of a נבילה is טהור, and רבי ביבי's silence, which he explains with רבי חנין's teaching about living without security over food.
