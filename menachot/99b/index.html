Topic

Guarding one's Torah learning, the changing of the לחם הפנים, and what 'לא ימוש' requires

Overview

The amud opens with teachings of ריש לקיש about the honor of Torah. He says stopping Torah study can sometimes be its foundation, as when משה broke the לוחות. He also says a תלמיד חכם who sinned is not shamed in public, and that forgetting even one thing from one's learning is a לאו. רבינא counts two לאוין in forgetting, and רב נחמן בר יצחק counts three. The גמרא then limits the rule: it does not cover forgetting through אונס or learning that was too hard. Next, a משנה describes how the לחם הפנים was changed each שבת. It covers the marble and gold tables, eight כהנים sliding the bread on and off, and רבי יוסי's view that a short gap is still 'תמיד'. It also gives what happens when יום כיפורים falls on or before שבת. From רבי יוסי's reading of 'תמיד', the גמרא learns that a little Torah in the morning and evening fulfills 'לא ימוש'. Different views follow on what that פסוק demands. The amud ends with חזקיה's דרשה that הקדוש ברוך הוא lures people from death to life, and this continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

שֶׁבִּיטּוּלָהּ שֶׁל תּוֹרָה זֶהוּ יִסּוּדָהּ,

that the neglect of Torah is its foundation,

דִּכְתִיב: ״אֲשֶׁר שִׁבַּרְתָּ״,

as it is written: 'which you broke.'

אָמַר לוֹ הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא לְמֹשֶׁה:

The Holy One, Blessed be He, said to Moshe:

יִישַׁר כֹּחֲךָ שֶׁשִּׁבַּרְתָּ.

Well done that you broke them.
