Topic

Questions about the עומר and the שתי הלחם, and about things that passed into the ground or through an animal: sown grain, kernels from dung, and an elephant's basket

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier argument. The word ביכורים, used for the שתי הלחם, means 'first for the מזבח,' and the מזבח already received this year's grain through the עומר. רמי בר חמא then asks whether the budding of a tree's leaves counts like grain taking root, so that the שתי הלחם would permit its fruit. This is left as תיקו. רבא בר רב חנן asks about grain that was reaped and then sown again before the עומר. Is it still like loose grain in a jug, or has it become part of the ground? He asks this about the עומר, about אונאה and about oaths, and all three stay תיקו. רמי בר חמא asks whether kernels found in animal dung, once planted again, may be used for מנחות. The answer depends on whether the problem is disgust or weakness, and this too stays תיקו. Finally, he asks about an elephant that swallowed palm leaves that were later woven into a basket. The גמרא begins to ask whether passing through the elephant counts as digestion, and that discussion continues on the next amud.

בִּכּוּרִים לְפֵירָא קָא אָמְרִינַן?

Are we saying 'first fruits' with regard to the produce?

לְמִזְבֵּחַ קָא אָמְרִינַן!

We are saying it with regard to the altar!

וְהָא אָכֵיל לֵהּ מִזְבֵּחַ

And the altar has already eaten

from the produce of this year.
