Topic

Why the Sages forbade wool ציצית on a linen garment even though the הלכה follows בית הלל

Overview

A ברייתא records that בית שמאי exempt a linen garment from ציצית and בית הלל obligate it, and the הלכה follows בית הלל. Yet in ירושלים, anyone who put wool תכלת on linen was looked at as strange, and רבי explains that the Sages forbade it because people are not experts in the law. The גמרא tries ways to teach the law instead: ten people wearing it in the market, or a public lecture. Neither works, and the ban is then explained as a גזירה because of קלא אילן, a fake blue dye that would leave a person wearing כלאים with no מצוה. The גמרא asks why fake-blue strings shouldn't at least count as white. It answers that white strings can be made of linen, following ריש לקיש's rule that a מצוה pushes aside a prohibition only when you cannot keep both. Since fake dye could be caught by testing, the גמרא switches to a new reason: dye used for testing the color (טעימה) is invalid. Sending letters to warn people is rejected as unreliable. The amud ends as רבא begins a new statement that continues on the next amud.
