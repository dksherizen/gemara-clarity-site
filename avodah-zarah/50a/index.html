Topic

Stones near a מרקוליס: the argument between רבי ישמעאל and the רבנן, and whether מרקוליס stones reused for paving roads stay forbidden

Overview

The גמרא narrows the argument in the משנה about stones lying near a מרקוליס. Stones close enough that they may have fallen off are forbidden by everyone, and 'beside' in the משנה means within the pile's ארבע אמות, so the argument is about stones farther off but still inside that area. רבי ישמעאל forbids three stones because people sometimes build a small מרקוליס next to a big one. The רבנן disagree, so for them only whether the stones are seen with the pile matters. רבא answers two contradictions: he reads a ברייתא as 'stones that were found' instead of 'that fell', and he separates stones in one area from stones in two areas divided by a raised spot. He also explains that the three-stone shape (one, one, and one on top) is only needed for the main pile. Next comes the story of מרקוליס stones from King ינאי's ruined house that were used to pave roads. רבי יוחנן points to רבי מנחם ברבי סימאי, who walked on those roads. The גמרא then explains the two sides: the strict side treats the stones as an idol's offering, which can never be cancelled, and the lenient side says stones are not like offerings in the בית המקדש. The amud ends as a ברייתא about a גוי who paved roads with such stones begins, and its ruling comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

בִּמְקוֹרָבוֹת נָמֵי,

Also with nearby stones,

דְּאִיכָּא לְמֵימַר מִינֵּיהּ נְפַל —

where one can say they fell from it —

דִּבְרֵי הַכֹּל אֲסוּרוֹת,

everyone agrees they are forbidden.

כִּי פְּלִיגִי — בִּמְרוּחָקוֹת.

Where they argue is about distant stones.
