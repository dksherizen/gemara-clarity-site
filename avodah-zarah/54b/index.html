Topic

How an act can forbid another's property or an exchange for עבודה זרה (including חליפי חליפין and שביעית), and why God does not destroy objects of idol worship

Overview

The amud opens by explaining how someone who slaughters another person's animal for עבודה זרה can forbid it, even though a person cannot forbid what is not his. The proof is the Temple כלים of אחז: because a real act of worship was done with them, they became forbidden. רבי יוחנן adds two cases where an act forbids things that bowing alone does not: digging pits in the ground, and trading animals for an idol. רבין then reports a מחלוקת about whether an exchange of an exchange (חליפי חליפין) is also forbidden. Each side explains its verse, and the debate turns on whether שני כתובין הבאין כאחד teach other cases, with שביעית as the example of status passing along a chain of purchases. A new משנה follows, telling how the elders in Rome explained why God does not destroy idols: the world needs the sun and moon, and He will not destroy His world because of fools. The גמרא adds that the world follows its normal course and sinners will be judged later. It ends with רבן גמליאל's parables answering a philosopher, and his supporting פסוק comes on the next amud.
