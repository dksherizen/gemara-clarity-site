Topic

Nullifying a גוי's idol, the brick law learned from the אשרות and the עגל, abandoned idols, and kings' platforms

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a ברייתא: a גוי's idol is not nullified when the owners leave it but plan to return, like the nations in יהושע's war. The גמרא explains why all four cases in that ברייתא had to be listed: borrowing against the idol, a rockslide covering it, robbers stealing it, and owners leaving it behind and going overseas. It then asks why the war of יהושע is mentioned at all. It answers that this hints to רב's law: a גוי who bows to a brick a Jew set up for worship makes it forbidden. The source is the burning of the אשרות in ארץ ישראל, and the reason given is that after the עגל the gentiles' worship counted as done on the Jews' behalf. A new משנה follows: an idol abandoned in peacetime is permitted, one abandoned in wartime is forbidden, and kings' platforms are permitted. רב applies the peacetime rule to בית נמרוד, and רבי יוחנן explains the reason given for the platforms. The amud ends with עולא sitting on a damaged platform. רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש permit a damaged platform, but a damaged altar stays forbidden until most of it is destroyed.
