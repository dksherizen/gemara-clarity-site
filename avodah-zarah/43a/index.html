Topic

Forbidden figures: the דרקון, cancelling a found idol, and making images of the heavenly bodies and the בית המקדש

Overview

The גמרא first shows that making a דרקון figure is not forbidden, so the דרקון part of רב ששת's ruling must be about finding one, just like our משנה. אביי accepts that the ruling mixes finding and making cases. רבא instead says the whole ruling is about finding, and its stricter middle part follows רבי יהודה, who also forbids figures of a nursing woman and סר אפיס. A ברייתא defines a דרקון as a figure with ציצין between its neck joints, and the הלכה follows רבי שמעון בן אלעזר. The story of רבי אלעזר הקפר and a found דרקון ring teaches three laws about how a גוי can cancel an idol's status. אביי explains that an owner who gives up on a lost idol does not give up on its being worshipped. The גמרא then asks how רבן גמליאל could keep pictures of the moon. אביי answers that only things that can truly be copied are forbidden, and supports this with a ברייתא about copies of the בית המקדש and the מנורה. A new challenge from "לא תעשון אתי" begins, and אביי's answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הָנֵי — אִין,

These, yes, they are forbidden to make,

צוּרַת דְּרָקוֹן — לָא!

but a dragon figure, no, it is not!

אֶלָּא פְּשִׁיטָא בְּמוֹצֵא, וּכְדִתְנַן:

Rather, it is obvious that it is about one who finds, as we learned in the משנה:

הַמּוֹצֵא כֵּלִים וַעֲלֵיהֶם צוּרַת חַמָּה.

One who finds vessels with a figure of the sun on them.
