Topic

Gentile items forbidden even for benefit, the cheese story, the source for banning idol wine, and the seal rules for vinegar and cooked wine

Overview

The amud opens with a משנה listing items of גוים that a Jew may not use, some of them forbidden even for benefit (איסור הנאה). These include wine, vinegar that was once wine, Hadrianic earthenware, and hides cut at the heart. It also gives רבי עקיבא's rule about meat going into and out of an idol's house, and a rule about doing business with people going to or from an idol festival. It then brings three disputes in which רבי מאיר forbids even benefit and חכמים are more lenient. Next, רבי ישמעאל asks רבי יהושע why gentile cheese is forbidden. רבי יהושע offers two reasons, and each one is challenged. Instead of answering the second challenge, he turns the talk to how a פסוק in שיר השירים is read. The גמרא then builds a chain of sources for the ban on benefit from idol wine: wine is learned from a זבח, a זבח from a מת, a מת from the עגלה ערופה, and the עגלה ערופה from קדשים. Finally, רב אשי explains that the vinegar clause, and a parallel teaching about cooked wine, show that a Jew's vinegar or cooked wine left with a gentile needs only one seal. His explanation for cooked wine continues on the next amud.
