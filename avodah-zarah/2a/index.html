Topic

The ban on dealings before gentile festivals, the spelling of אידיהן/עידיהן and its sources, and the opening of the aggadah about the nations at the end of days

Overview

The משנה forbids business and money dealings with גוים for three days before their festivals, because a good deal may lead the גוי to thank his עבודה זרה. רבי יהודה allows collecting a debt from them, since paying hurts the גוי. The חכמים answer that he is relieved afterward. The גמרא reports that רב ושמואל read the משנה's word for 'their festivals' differently: אידיהן with an א (calamity) or עידיהן with an ע (witnesses). Each reading has a פסוק to back it up, and each side explains why its word is better. The גמרא then challenges the פסוק used for the ע reading, since רבי יהושע בן לוי reads it as being about ישראל. רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע brings a different פסוק, where the idols themselves are the witnesses. Finally, an aggadah begins about the future: הקב״ה invites those who studied Torah to take their reward, and the nations rush in. The story continues on the next amud.
