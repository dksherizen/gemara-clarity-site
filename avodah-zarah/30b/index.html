Topic

The laws of גילוי (uncovered food and drink a snake may have poisoned), how venom behaves, and the start of a list of three kinds of forbidden wine

Overview

The גמרא finishes a ruling about soured wine: after a set time it no longer falls under גילוי or יין נסך, though the נהרדעי still worry about גילוי because a snake sometimes drinks it anyway. Next comes a list of items that are not subject to גילוי: fermenting wine, cress dishes, כותח הבבלי, steadily dripping water, and the opening of a fig. Some of these come with limits, such as the strict custom of the בני גולה, vinegar that keeps snakes away, and bite marks in the כותח. The fig ruling is matched to רבי אליעזר, who relies on 'שומר פתאים ה׳'. רב ספרא then teaches that venom sinks, spreads, or floats depending on the snake's age, and the גמרא explains that an old snake grows stronger in body but weaker in venom. Sinking venom explains why an uncovered barrel or melon stays forbidden even after nine people ate or drank safely. A ברייתא forbids using uncovered water for many other purposes. The גמרא clarifies how אחרים differ from the תנא קמא about washing, and explains why one may give such water to his own cat but not to a friend's. The amud ends as רבי יהודה בן בתירא begins listing three kinds of wine, starting with יין נסך, and the list continues on the next amud.
