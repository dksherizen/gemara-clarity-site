Topic

Whether a pouring stream (נצוק) is a חיבור, and practical rules for pouring and drinking wine with gentiles

Overview

The גמרא tries to answer a question it already raised: does a pouring stream (נצוק) connect two vessels into one unit (חיבור)? A proof from the funnel case in the משנה is blocked by רבי חייא, who places that case where the wine touched the funnel directly. A proof from pouring vessel to vessel is defended at first, then dropped, because the סיפא of the same משנה points the other way. A ברייתא about pouring from a barrel into a pit is explained by רב ששת as a case of כח גוי, a rule the רבנן limited to wine that leaves the barrel. רבא's ruling forbidding a whole barrel after a gentile touched a siphon tube is also set aside as a special case. So the question of נצוק is not settled on this amud. Along the way come practical rulings: break up the stream when pouring for gentiles, and keep a gentile from helping you pour. The amud ends with the rule that a Jew and a gentile may drink together from קנישקנין, as long as the Jew stops first.

Opening lines

אִם יֵשׁ בּוֹ עַכֶּבֶת יַיִן — אָסוּר.

If there is a remnant of wine in it, it is forbidden.

הָא עַכֶּבֶת יַיִן בְּמַאי קָא מִתַּסְרָא?

This remnant of wine, through what does it become forbidden?

לָאו בְּנִצּוֹק?

Is it not through the stream?

שְׁמַע מִינַּהּ: נִצּוֹק חִיבּוּר.

Learn from it: a stream is a connection.
