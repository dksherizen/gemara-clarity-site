Avodah Zarah 60b

עבודה זרה ס׳ ע״ב

Topic

Which kinds of contact by a גוי make Jewish wine forbidden: a split barrel, a winepress, and the משנה's cases of a loan, indirect contact, and a barrel thrown in anger.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a story about a barrel that split while a גוי hugged it closed. If it split lengthwise, the wine may only be sold to גוים. If it split widthwise, even drinking is allowed, because he was just acting like a brick. רב אשי then explains how to clean a winepress a גוי stood in. A new משנה follows with several cases. A גוי found by the vat forbids the wine only if the owner owes him money. When a גוי fell in or touched the wine indirectly, the חכמים say it may be sold and רבי שמעון permits it. A barrel thrown in anger was declared fit. In the גמרא, שמואל limits the loan case to a loan tied to that wine, and רב אשי proves this from the בית שאן משנה. רב פפא adds that a גוי who came out of the vat alive forbids the wine, because he treats his rescue like a holiday. The גמרא praises רבי שמעון's all-or-nothing view but rules against him. Finally, רב אשי compares a גוי's contact to a זב's, and רב הונא challenges this comparison from the words "in his anger."

Opening lines

דְּאִיפְּקַעָה לְאוּרְכַּהּ,

that split lengthwise,

אִידְּרִי הָהוּא גּוֹי חַבְּקַהּ,

a certain gentile jumped up and hugged it,

שַׁרְיַיהּ רַפְרָם בַּר פָּפָּא,

Rafram bar Pappa permitted it,

וְאִי תֵּימָא רַב הוּנָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב יְהוֹשֻׁעַ,

and some say it was Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua,
