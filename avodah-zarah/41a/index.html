Avodah Zarah 41a

עבודה זרה מ״א ע״א

Topic

Which statues count as עבודה זרה, what objects in their hands signify, and whether found fragments are permitted

Overview

The amud opens with רבה limiting the משנה's dispute about statues. His first version says the argument is only about village statues. The גמרא objects that village statues are obviously made for worship. So it restates his teaching: the argument is about city statues, which might be decorative, and village statues are forbidden by everyone. The גמרא then explains that a staff, bird, or orb in a statue's hand shows the idol's claimed rule over the world. A ברייתא adds a sword, a crown, and a ring to that list. For רבן שמעון בן גמליאל, even a pebble or a twig in the hand is enough. רב אשי asks about a statue holding excrement, and the question stays unresolved (תיקו). A new משנה permits found broken pieces of statues but forbids hand or foot shapes. שמואל extends the permission even to pieces of a real idol, and the גמרא begins to challenge him from the hand-and-foot ruling, a challenge that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אָמַר רַבָּה:

Rabba said:

מַחְלוֹקֶת בְּשֶׁל כְּפָרִים,

The dispute is about those of villages,

אֲבָל בְּשֶׁל כְּרַכִּים —

but regarding those of large cities,

דִּבְרֵי הַכֹּל מוּתָּרִין,

everyone agrees they are permitted.
