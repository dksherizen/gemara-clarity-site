Topic

When worship makes an animal a forbidden נעבד: forced worship, a friend's animal, and acts done on the animal itself

Overview

First, חזקיה brings a פסוק showing that idolatrous items lose their status only when they are smashed like crumbling limestone. Then a ברייתא teaches that a person can make his own item a forbidden נעבד, but not his friend's. Another ברייתא counts worship done 'באונס' (under force), and the גמרא asks whether that means grabbing a friend's animal. רמי בר חמא answers that the person was forced to bow to his own animal. רבי זירא objects that the Torah frees someone who acts under force. רבא answers that force frees a person only in private, while forced worship in public still counts. The רבנן offer רבא support from platforms used during a time of persecution, but he turns it down. חזקיה and רב אדא בר אהבה then give other answers about a friend's animal, and the conclusion is that doing an act on it, like pouring wine between its horns or cutting one סימן, makes it forbidden. Finally, the גמרא looks for the source of this rule and pushes away two suggestions, the כהנים and the מזבח stones. The search for a source continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אָמַר חִזְקִיָּה: מַאי קְרָא

חזקיה said: What is the verse?

״בְּשׂוּמוֹ כׇּל אַבְנֵי מִזְבֵּחַ

'When he makes all the stones of the altar

כְּאַבְנֵי גִר מְנֻפָּצוֹת

like limestones that are crushed to pieces,

לֹא יָקֻמוּ אֲשֵׁרִים וְחַמָּנִים״?

the אשרים and sun-images will not rise.'

The steps (18)

  1. Statementחזקיה's verse: idolatrous items lose their status only when smashed

  4. Resolutionרמי בר חמא: the person himself was forced to bow to his own animal
  5. Challengeרבי זירא: the Torah frees someone who acts under force
