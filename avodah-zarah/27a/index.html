Topic

Who may perform מילה, and being healed or given haircuts by non-Jews

Overview

The amud opens by finishing the גמרא's discussion of a ברייתא about whether a ארמאי or a כותי may circumcise a Jewish baby. One approach explains that רבי מאיר means only a recognized expert doctor. In the end, though, the גמרא reverses the names in the ברייתא, and it solves a clash between two statements of רבי יהודה by saying one of them belongs to רבי יהודה הנשיא. רב חסדא then gives the פסוקים behind רבי יהודה, who requires the right intention for מילה, and רבי יוסי, who does not. Next, רב and רבי יוחנן argue over which פסוק disqualifies a גוי from doing a מילה. After two suggested differences are knocked down by a משנה about vows, the גמרא concludes that they differ about a woman, and it defends this using the story of צפורה. A new משנה then deals with medical treatment and haircuts from non-Jews. The גמרא defines ריפוי ממון as treating one's animal and ריפוי נפשות as treating one's own body. מר עוקבא adds that simply taking a non-Jew's advice about medicine is allowed.

Opening lines

בְּרוֹפֵא מוּמְחֶה,

[We are dealing] with an expert physician,

דְּכִי אֲתָא רַב דִּימִי

as when Rav Dimi came,

אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן:

[he said that] Rabbi Yochanan says:

אִם הָיָה מוּמְחֶה לְרַבִּים — מוּתָּר.

If he was a recognized expert to the public, it is permitted.
