Topic

How an idol's forbidden status is cancelled (ביטול עבודה זרה): shared idols, damage versus insults, and selling the idol to a Jewish or gentile smith.

Overview

The amud finishes רבי הלל בריה דרבי וולס's teaching about an idol shared by a Jew and a gentile. Only the gentile can nullify his own share, and the Jew's share stays forbidden forever. A new משנה teaches that damaging an idol nullifies it, even just crushing its face. Insults like spitting or throwing dung do not nullify it. רבי and חכמים argue about whether selling or mortgaging the idol nullifies it. חזקיה uses פסוקים from ישעיהו to show that a worshipper who curses his idol in anger goes back to it. Two אמוראים disagree about whether the selling dispute concerns a gentile smith or a Jewish smith. A ברייתא of רבי is brought as a proof, but the proof is pushed off. A challenge from a ברייתא about scrap metal is answered: that seller never knowingly sold an idol. A new ברייתא about idols that were pawned, buried, stolen or abandoned begins, and its ruling comes on the next amud.
