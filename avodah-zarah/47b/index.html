Topic

Laws of buildings and stones used for עבודה זרה: the fallen wall, טומאה of its materials, and the three kinds of houses and stones

Overview

This amud opens at the end of a משנה about a wall next to a house of עבודה זרה. If the wall was shared, it is treated as half and half. The stones, wood and dust are טמא like a שרץ, but רבי עקיבא says they are טמא like a נדה, so they make a person טמא even by carrying. The גמרא asks whether pulling the wall back gives the idol extra space. It answers that the space becomes a bathroom, and after some back-and-forth about privacy it settles on a bathroom for children. It also gives a second answer: he fences the space off with thorns. Next, a new משנה lists three kinds of houses: built for עבודה זרה, plastered for it, or one an idol was only brought into. רב adds that bowing to a house also forbids it, and the משנה still counts three because building and bowing are cancelled the same way. A parallel משנה lists three kinds of stones. The גמרא finally reads רבי אמי's statement as teaching that even plaster worked into a stone can be removed to permit it.

Opening lines

נִידּוֹן מֶחֱצָה עַל מֶחֱצָה.

It is judged as half and half.

אֲבָנָיו, עֵצָיו וַעֲפָרוֹ

Its stones, its wood and its dust

מְטַמְּאִין כְּשֶׁרֶץ,

make impure like a creeping animal,

שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״שַׁקֵּץ תְּשַׁקְּצֶנּוּ״.

as it is said: 'You shall utterly detest it.'
