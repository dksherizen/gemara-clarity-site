Avodah Zarah 42a

עבודה זרה מ״ב ע״א

Topic

Whether a ספק can override a ודאי, and רבי יוחנן's challenges to ריש לקיש's ruling that an idol that broke by itself is permitted.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא: a נפל was thrown into a pit in רימון, a כהן peered in, and the חכמים ruled him טהור because martens and polecats may have dragged it away. The גמרא asks whether this means a ספק can override a ודאי. It gives two answers. First, maybe it was only something that looked like a נפל, and the כהן first checked whether it was a real baby at all. Second, since those animals are common there, they surely dragged it away. The גמרא then returns to the argument about an idol that broke by itself, which ריש לקיש permits. רבי יוחנן raises a series of challenges from sources that forbid an idol a ישראל damaged. אביי and רבא answer, and רבא explains that such cases are forbidden only by a rabbinic decree, in case the ישראל lifts the idol and makes it his own. That answer is reused for the stones of מרקוליס, the shaved idol, the ground-up idol and the beheaded dragon. Finally, the leaves of a worshipped tree stay forbidden because the tree, the main idol, is still whole.

Opening lines

שֶׁל מֵצִיק אֶחָד בְּרִימּוֹן,

of a certain oppressor in Rimon,

שֶׁהֵטִילָה נֵפֶל לְבוֹר,

who cast a stillborn into a pit,

וּבָא כֹּהֵן וְהֵצִיץ

and a priest came and peered in

לֵידַע אִם זָכָר אִם נְקֵבָה,

to know whether it was male or female,
