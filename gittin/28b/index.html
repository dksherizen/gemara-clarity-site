Gittin 28b

גיטין כ״ח ע״ב

Open Gittin 28b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Presuming a far-away or endangered person is alive: future versus past death, a חטאת sent from overseas, and people in danger or condemned to death.

Overview

The amud opens with רבא answering a contradiction. We do not worry that a far-away husband already died, but we may worry that he will die. After a challenge from the wineskin case and a failed defense, רבא settles on this: 'perhaps he died' is not a worry for anyone, while 'perhaps he will die' is a מחלוקת תנאים. The גמרא then explains how a חטאת sent from overseas can be offered without סמיכה: either it is a woman's offering (רב יוסף) or a bird חטאת (רב פפא). It also shows why the משנה had to teach the presumption of life for גט, תרומה, and חטאת העוף alike. A new משנה follows. People in a besieged town, on a storm-tossed ship, or going to trial are presumed alive. People in a conquered town, on a lost ship, or going to execution get the stringencies of both the living and the dead. רב יוסף limits the execution case to a Jewish court, and the גמרא tests this against bribery, the משנה in מכות, and a ברייתא about death reports. A second version of רב יוסף's statement begins at the end and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

שֶׁמָּא מֵת לָא חָיְישִׁינַן,

That perhaps he died, we are not concerned;

שֶׁמָּא יָמוּת חָיְישִׁינַן.

that perhaps he will die, we are concerned.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ רַב אַדָּא בַּר מַתְנָה לְרָבָא:

Rav Adda bar Mattana said to Rava:

וְהָא נוֹד דִּכְשֶׁמָּא יָמוּת הוּא,

But the wineskin is like 'perhaps he will die,'
