Topic

Whether a Jewish court's death sentence makes a man presumed dead, and when an agent bringing a גט may hand it to another agent.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing רב יוסף's statement: a man sentenced by a Jewish court is presumed executed, because once the verdict is final, the court does not look for new reasons to acquit. The גמרא tries to prove this from a משנה about a runaway convict, but a runaway may be different. A second proof comes from a ברייתא about death reports, but it is pushed off: gentile officials are not believed because they want their verdict to look carried out. A new משנה teaches that a sick agent may pass a גט to another agent, unless the husband also asked him to collect an item from the wife. רב כהנא says the agent must really be sick, and the גמרא fits this with a ברייתא about 'הולך' and 'את הולך'. אביי and רבא explain why a different משנה does not let agents appoint others, and their answers differ about a gift document. Finally, ריש לקיש and רבי יוחנן argue about what the case of the item teaches, and the גמרא begins to describe where the גט may be invalid.
