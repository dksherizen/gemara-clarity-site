Topic

An agent who misreports the wife's instructions, when a husband can retract a גט given to an agent, and whether 'here you are' counts like 'acquire'

Overview

First the גמרא shows that רב נחמן's ruling about an agent who misreported the wife's words proves nothing about whose words the husband relies on. In his case, the agent cancelled his own agency by saying he would only receive the גט. רב הונא בר חייא and then רבא challenge רב נחמן with two sources. One is a husband who says 'receive this גט for my wife.' The other is a קטנה who appoints a receiving agent. In both, the husband seems to want her divorced in any way that works. The גמרא answers that in those cases the husband knowingly meant delivery, while in רב נחמן's case he was fooled. A ברייתא about switched wording is read so that it does not clash with רב נחמן. That ברייתא shows the husband can rely on the agent's words, but רב נחמן's case differs because the husband said 'here you are, as she said.' Next comes a ברייתא with three views on when a husband can take back a גט given to the wife's receiving agent: the first opinion, רבי נתן, and רבי. Finally the גמרא asks whether, for רבי נתן, 'here you are' counts like 'acquire,' and begins quoting a משנה to answer. The answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

בִּשְׁלָמָא אִי אִיתְּמַר אִיפְּכָא:

It would be understandable if the opposite had been stated:

״הִתְקַבֵּל לִי גִּיטִּי״,

[she said:] "Receive my bill of divorce for me,"

וְ״אִשְׁתְּךָ אָמְרָה הָבֵא לִי גִּיטִּי״,

and [the agent said:] "Your wife said: Bring me my bill of divorce,"

וְהוּא אֹמֵר: ״הֵילָךְ כְּמָה שֶׁאָמְרָה״;

and he [the husband] says: "Here it is, as she said";
