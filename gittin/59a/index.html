Gittin 59a

גיטין נ״ט ע״א

Topic

רבי's unique greatness, and the rules for deals made by a deaf-mute and by young children

Overview

רב reports that when רבי's court voted, they asked him first. The גמרא asks why, since a משנה says that outside capital cases the vote starts with the greatest judge. It answers that רבי's court always started with the junior judges. The same teacher praises רבי: from משה until רבי, no one person was supreme in both Torah and worldly greatness. The גמרא tests this against יהושע, שאול, דוד, שלמה, חזקיה and עזרא. Each time it answers either that someone else was their equal, or that the claim means being unmatched for all one's years. רב אחא בריה דרבא says the same about the period from רבי to רב אשי. A new משנה then rules that a deaf-mute can buy and sell movable items by gestures (בן בתירא: even by lip movements), and that young children's deals in movable items count. The גמרא discusses whether this reaches גיטין, at what age children qualify, why the rule exists, how big a price mistake is allowed, and whether a child's gift counts. Between these it explains the words מלתחה and מלמלא. The amud ends with a new משנה about rules made מפני דרכי שלום, such as the order of the Torah reading.

Opening lines

אֲנָא הֲוַאי בְּמִנְיָינָא דְּבֵי רַבִּי,

I was present at the counting of the vote in the house of Rabbi,

וּמִינַּאי דִּידִי מְנוֹ בְּרֵישָׁא.

and they began counting with me myself first.

וְהָאֲנַן תְּנַן:

But didn't we learn in a Mishnah:

דִּינֵי מָמוֹנוֹת וְהַטְּהָרוֹת וְהַטְּמָאוֹת –

In monetary cases, and cases of ritual purity and impurity,
