Gittin 32b

גיטין ל״ב ע״ב

Topic

Cancelling a גט: what the word בטל means, which phrases cancel a גט, whether a cancelled גט can be reused, and how many people must be present.

Overview

The גמרא explains that the word בטל can mean 'already void' or 'let it become void,' and each case uses the reading that actually works there: one for a גט, another for a gift. אביי adds a tradition that a שליח for a gift is like a שליח for a גט, so 'take this' (הולך) does not mean 'acquire this' (זכי). רב נחמן בר יצחק wonders whether the bare word 'void' cancels a גט, and the question ends in תיקו. רב ששת, or a ברייתא, then lists phrases that cancel a גט, like 'let it be pottery,' and phrases that do nothing, like 'it is pottery.' 'It is hereby pottery' does cancel it, just like 'it is hereby הקדש.' רב נחמן and רב ששת argue whether a cancelled גט can be used again, and the הלכה follows רב נחמן that it can. The גמרא defends this against רבי יוחנן's ruling: words can cancel words, but cancelling the שליח does not destroy the גט itself. Finally, the two argue whether cancelling a גט needs two or three people. רב נחמן begins a proof from the פרוזבול that two can be called a בית דין, and that proof continues on the next amud.
