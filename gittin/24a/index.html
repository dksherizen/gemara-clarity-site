Topic

Why a woman bringing her own גט must declare 'בפני נכתב', how her agency is set up, and the opening משנה on writing a גט לשמה

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא that supports אביי. A woman who brings her own גט must say 'בפני נכתב ובפני נחתם', just like the other women who may bring it, and that declaration is only needed outside ארץ ישראל. רב אשי adds that the wording of the משנה points the same way. The גמרא then explains that רב יוסף really does split the משנה: the first and last clauses are outside ארץ ישראל and the middle clause is inside it, because the middle clause says only 'the writing proves'. Next the גמרא asks why a woman bringing her own גט needs to declare anything, since a גט divorces her the moment it reaches her hand. Several setups are tried and rejected: a condition about a specific court, putting the גט on the ground and picking it up, and having her switch from delivery agent to receiving agent. The answers that survive keep her as the husband's delivery agent until she arrives. Then she either appoints a new agent or has the בית דין appoint one, and she receives the גט from that agent. The chapter הַמֵּבִיא גֵּט then ends. The next chapter opens with a משנה teaching that a גט must be written for the specific woman being divorced, even when the names match exactly.

Opening lines

וּמִמָּקוֹם שֶׁבָּאתָ,

And from the place that you came,

מָה לְהַלָּן צְרִיכוֹת שֶׁיֹּאמְרוּ:

just as there, they must say:

'It was written in our presence and it was signed in our presence,'

אַף הִיא צְרִיכָה שֶׁתֹּאמַר:

so too she must say:
