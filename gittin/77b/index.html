Topic

How a woman can acquire her גט in her own חצר even though her husband has rights in her property: the principle of declining a תקנת חכמים, רבא's idea that the גט and her חצר come 'together', and רבינא's challenge to רבא's comparison with her hand.

Overview

The גמרא is asking how a wife can acquire her גט in her own חצר, since her husband has rights in her property. First it explains that a person may decline a תקנת חכמים made for his benefit, like רב הונא's rule that a wife may refuse support and keep her earnings. רבא gives another answer: the גט and her courtyard come to her at the same moment (באין כאחד). רבינא questions the comparison רבא drew to a wife's hand, and רב אשי explains that רבא was really thinking of a slave's hand. A story follows about a dying man. רבא told him to give his wife the place where the גט lay. When רב עיליש challenged this, רבא defended it, at last with the same idea of באין כאחד. Next, עולא and רבי אושעיא argue over whether the woman must be near her courtyard. They explain differently what it means for a חצר to be 'like her hand.' Finally, רב יוסף rules on a גט that landed on a board in a courtyard lent to her. The board's size and height decide whether it is a separate area, and a further limit continues on the next amud.
