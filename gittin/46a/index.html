Topic

The ban on remarrying a wife divorced for a bad name or a vow: its reason, when it applies, and when it does not

Overview

The amud asks why a man who divorced his wife because of a bad name (שם רע) or a vow (נדר) may not remarry her. In one version of רב נחמן's teaching, the reason is קלקולא: the fear that the husband will later attack the גט, so he should not state his reason. In another version, it is a penalty that warns Jewish women not to be careless with עריות and נדרים, so he must state it. The גמרא brings a ברייתא supporting each version: רבי מאיר for the first and רבי אלעזר ברבי יוסי for the second. Next it explains רבי יהודה's view that only a vow known to רבים blocks remarriage, which he learns from the oath to the גבעונים. The רבנן reject that proof. רב נחמן and רבי יצחק then argue whether רבים means three or ten people. The גמרא then explains the dispute between רבי מאיר and רבי אלעזר over vows that need a חכם, which depends on whether a husband minds his wife being embarrassed in בית דין. Finally, it fills in a missing line in the משנה: if the husband made the vow, he may remarry her, as the story in צידן shows.

Opening lines

״מִשּׁוּם נֶדֶר אֲנִי מוֹצִיאֵךְ״ –

"Because of a vow I am sending you out."

קָסָבַר: טַעְמָא מַאי –

He holds: what is the reason?

מִשּׁוּם קִלְקוּלָא;

Because of the harm.

אִי אָמַר לַהּ הָכִי –

If he said this to her,
