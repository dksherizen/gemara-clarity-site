Gittin 44a

גיטין מ״ד ע״א

Topic

The penalty on a Jew whose slave ends up with a גוי: forced loss, partial sales, the size of the fine, and whether it passes to an heir

Overview

This amud is about the חכמים's penalty on a Jew whose slave ends up in a גוי's hands. First the גמרא gives another explanation of the earlier case of a slave used as security for a loan. Then a ברייתא teaches that a slave collected by a גוי for a debt, or taken by a סיקריקון, does not go free. The גמרא defends this ruling against a ברייתא about grain seized by the king's officers and against רב's ruling about selling a slave to a gentile official. רבי ירמיה then asks about temporary sales, partial sales, and different kinds of buyers; only the גר תושב question is answered. רבי אמי rules that a master may take money for a slave who ran off to a foreign army, because he is only rescuing what he can. רבי יהושע בן לוי fines one who sells his slave to a גוי, and the גמרא brings two versions of how large the fine is compared to ריש לקיש's fine for selling an animal. The amud ends with רבי ירמיה asking whether the son is fined after his father dies. That question continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאִי בָּעֵית אֵימָא:

And if you want, say:

בְּשֶׁלָּוָה עַל מְנָת לְמַשְׁכְּנוֹ,

where he borrowed on the condition that they would collect it,

וְלֹא מִשְׁכְּנוֹ.

but they did not yet collect it.

תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן:

The Sages taught:
