Topic

Laws of orphans' property and their stewards (sales, removal, and oaths), and a new משנה on causing hidden damage to another's food or wine.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a reason from the previous page: if money alone finalized a sale, a paid seller could just say 'your wheat burned in the attic.' The גמרא then discusses buyers who paid orphans for produce, and רב שישא בריה דרב אידי explains why a special rule for orphans would actually hurt them. רב אשי reports a sale of an orphan's land without a public announcement, which is allowed for head tax, food, and burial. A story follows about עמרם צבעא, a steward whom the relatives accused before רב נחמן. The הלכה is that a steward is removed only if witnesses show he is causing loss. Next the גמרא explains the משנה's dispute about which steward must swear. The first opinion says the steward appointed by the father, אבא שאול says the one appointed by בית דין, and רבי אליעזר בן יעקב says both. A new משנה then teaches about one who makes another's food טמא, mixes in תרומה, or pours wine to an idol. רב and שמואל argue about what 'pouring' means, and רב's full reason continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

דְּאָתוּ לְמֵימַר לְהוּ:

For they will come to say to them:

נִשְׂרְפוּ חִיטֵּיכֶם בַּעֲלִיָּיה.

Your wheat was burned in the upper story.

יָהֲבִי לְהוּ זוּזֵי לְיַתְמֵי אַפֵּירֵי;

If they gave money to the orphans for produce;

אִיַּיקַּר –

if it went up in price –
