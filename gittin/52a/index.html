Gittin 52a

גיטין נ״ב ע״א

Topic

The powers and limits of an אפוטרופוס caring for orphans, and the rule that orphans' property is like הקדש in sales.

Overview

The משנה teaches that a person caring for orphans, whether a homeowner they live with or an אפוטרופוס their father appointed, must tithe their produce. It also brings a dispute about which kind of אפוטרופוס swears when he hands the property back. The גמרא asks how a steward can tithe produce that is not his, and רב חסדא answers that he may tithe food the orphans will eat now but not produce being stored. A long ברייתא then lists a steward's limits. He may spend on מצוה items with a fixed cost but not on open-ended ones. He may not go to court in a way that risks a loss, make risky land trades, or free the orphans' slaves. Stories follow: רבי מאיר stops a steward from trading land for slaves and ignores a dream, he makes peace between two people who always fought, and רבי יהושע בן לוי allows trading land for plowing oxen. רב נחמן rules that an old woman the orphans lived with could sell their ox. He also teaches שמואל's rule that orphans' property, like הקדש, is acquired only with money. The גמרא works out how this rule applies when prices rise or fall. רב שישא insists it must never be used in a way that hurts the orphans, and his last objection continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳ יְתוֹמִין שֶׁסָּמְכוּ

MISHNA: Orphans who relied

אֵצֶל בַּעַל הַבַּיִת,

on a homeowner,

אוֹ שֶׁמִּינָּה לָהֶן אֲבִיהֶן אַפּוֹטְרוֹפּוֹס –

or whose father appointed a steward for them,

חַיָּיב לְעַשֵּׂר פֵּירוֹתֵיהֶם.

he is obligated to tithe their produce.
