Topic

Vows made על דעת רבים, why witnesses sign a גט מפני תיקון העולם, and how הלל's פרוסבול works

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion about making a כהן take a vow that cannot easily be undone. The גמרא settles on a vow made על דעת רבים, which אמימר rules cannot be undone. It then adds a limit: such a vow can be undone for a דבר מצוה, as in the story of the children's teacher whom רבינא brought back. Next, the גמרא asks how the משנה can call witnesses signing a גט a rabbinic fix (תיקון העולם) when signing seems to come from the Torah. רבה answers that the משנה follows רבי אלעזר, for whom the signatures are only a rabbinic backup. רב יוסף answers that the fix was making witnesses write out their full names, as רבן גמליאל taught, and the גמרא explains why great rabbis could still sign with a well-known mark. Finally, the גמרא turns to הלל's פרוסבול and asks how a rabbinic enactment can stop שביעית from cancelling debts. אביי answers that nowadays, according to רבי, cancelling debts is only rabbinic, because it depends on the release of land.

Opening lines

דְּמַדְּרִינַן לֵיהּ בְּרַבִּים.

That we administer the vow to him in public.

הָנִיחָא לְמַאן דְּאָמַר

This works out well according to the one who says

נֶדֶר שֶׁהוּדַּר בְּרַבִּים – אֵין לוֹ הֲפָרָה;

a vow made in public has no undoing;

אֶלָּא לְמַאן דְּאָמַר יֵשׁ לוֹ הֲפָרָה,

but according to the one who says it does have undoing,
