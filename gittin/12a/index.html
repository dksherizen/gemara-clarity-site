Gittin 12a

גיטין י״ב ע״א

Topic

Whether a master may tell his slave 'Work for me, and I will not feed you,' after setting aside the proof from the פאה dispute

Overview

The amud opens by knocking down a proof. The גמרא had tried to settle whether someone who grabs property for a creditor acquires it, by linking it to the dispute between רבי אליעזר and the רבנן about gathering פאה for a poor friend. The גמרא shows that each side may rely on its own special reason: a מיגו for רבי אליעזר, and a פסוק for the רבנן. It then explains how רבי אליעזר reads that פסוק. Next, the גמרא infers from the משנה that a master may tell his slave, 'Work for me, and I will not feed you.' It pushes this aside by saying the master may have let the slave keep his earnings for his food. The same answer is defended against a ברייתא about a slave and a wife exiled to the ערי מקלט, and against a dispute between רבן שמעון בן גמליאל and חכמים about a slave in years of famine. Finally the גמרא brings a proof from רב about a slave whose work was given to the הקדש. It rejects that proof too, and its challenge to that rejection continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

דִּלְמָא לָא הִיא;

Perhaps it is not so;

עַד כָּאן לָא קָאָמַר רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר הָתָם,

Rabbi Eliezer only said his ruling there

אֶלָּא דְּמִיגּוֹ דְּאִי בָּעֵי

because, since if he wanted,

מַפְקַר לְהוּ לְנִכְסֵיהּ,

he could make his property ownerless,
