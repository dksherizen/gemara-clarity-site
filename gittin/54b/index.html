Topic

Fines for deliberate acts in ביטול, and when a person is believed that he ruined something (פיגול, טהרות, a ספר תורה).

Overview

This amud opens by finishing a ברייתא about ערלה nuts that fell into other nuts and broke. The גמרא explains why רבי יהודה fines even an accidental break: otherwise people would break the nuts on purpose and fake an accident. It then sets רבי יוסי against himself, and answers that nobody risks his whole vineyard for one sapling, so that rare case needed no fine. A new משנה teaches that כהנים who make a קרבן פיגול on purpose must pay. A ברייתא follows about when a coworker is believed that he ruined טהרות or זבחים, explained differently by אביי and רבא. רבי אסי says the תורה believes such a person. The proof brought from the כהן גדול on יום הכפורים falls with 'קשיא'. The amud ends with two stories of a סופר who admits a hidden flaw in a ספר תורה he sold. רבי אמי says the סופר loses his pay but the scroll stays valid. רבי אבהו says the סופר's word makes the scroll unusable too.

Opening lines

נָפְלוּ וְנִתְפַּצְּעוּ;

They fell in and were broken;

אֶחָד שׁוֹגֵג, וְאֶחָד מֵזִיד –

whether by accident or on purpose,

לֹא יַעֲלוּ,

they are not nullified,

דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי מֵאִיר וְרַבִּי יְהוּדָה.

these are the words of Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda.
