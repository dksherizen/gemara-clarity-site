Topic

כותי witnesses on a גט, and documents made in non-Jewish courts

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion about a גט signed by a כותי witness. The גמרא explains that such a גט is valid because the Jewish witness signed after the כותי. On a גט the witnesses must sign together, so the Jew knowingly accepted him. רב אשי says this signing rule is a גזירה because of a husband who tells 'all of you' to sign. Next the גמרא studies רבי אלעזר's rule that only one כותי witness is allowed. It answers a challenge from the story of רבן גמליאל in two ways: אביי reads the story as 'its witness', and רבא says רבן גמליאל disagrees. A new משנה then teaches that documents from non-Jewish courts are valid, except a גט and a document freeing a slave, while רבי שמעון accepts even those. The גמרא asks how a gift document from such a court can work. It answers with שמואל's rule, דינא דמלכותא דינא, or by reading the exception as 'documents like a גט'. Finally it explains רבי שמעון through רבי אלעזר's view that the witnesses to the handing over make the divorce. After a challenge from רבי אבא, the גמרא starts narrowing the case, and the answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

דְּאִי לָאו דְּכוּתִי חָבֵר הֲוָה,

For if it were not that the Samaritan was a ḥaver,
