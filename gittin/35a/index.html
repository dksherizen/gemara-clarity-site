Topic

Why courts stopped making an אלמנה swear for her כתובה, and how far that rule reaches

Overview

This amud discusses the משנה's rule that courts stopped making an אלמנה take a שבועה before collecting her כתובה from the יתומים. The גמרא first suggests the reason is the story of a widow who swore, truthfully as far as she knew, about a deposited gold dinar, and lost a child. Then it asks why a גרושה still swears, and answers that an אלמנה, who works hard for the orphans, may talk herself into swearing falsely. Next come two versions of what רב and שמואל held: does the rule apply only in בית דין, or even outside it? In the נהרדעא version, רב never lets a widow swear, which is why he never collects a כתובה for her. Stories follow about widows before רב הונא and רבה בר רב הונא, including the rule that a widow who swears on her own (קופצת) may collect. רב יהודה then gives a way to let widows collect: a נדר in court and a שבועה outside it. Finally, the גמרא returns to שמואל's statement that a גרושה is made to swear. It challenges it with a letter from ארץ ישראל about a divorcée who collected with only a נדר, and that discussion continues on the next amud.

Because of desirability

it teaches us [that this is not so].
