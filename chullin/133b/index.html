Topic

Partnerships with a גוי or כהן and the gifts of זרוע, לחיים וקבה: marking, partial partnerships, and following the obligation

Overview

An animal owned in partnership with a גוי or a כהן is exempt from the gifts of זרוע, לחיים and קבה, but in some cases the Jewish partner must mark the animal so onlookers do not think he is skipping the gifts. The גמרא explains why a גוי partner needs no mark while a כהן partner does: people would not assume a Jew trusts a גוי with his money box, and a גוי partner openly argues about prices, while a modest כהן would not. Next, the גמרא asks why פסולי המוקדשין need no mark, since they are sold like ordinary meat. רב אדא בר אהבה answers that the ruling is about the kind sold only from home. רב הונא and חייא בר רב then argue about a partner in only one part of the animal. רב הונא says this exempts only that part's gift, and חייא בר רב is refuted from ברייתות. רב חסדא explains what misled him: a ברייתא listing the 24 מתנות כהונה counts זרוע, לחיים and קבה as one item, but that is only because they are similar. The גמרא then rules that when a ישראל owns only the head, we follow the part that carries the obligation (בתר חיובא אזלינן). Finally it quotes the משנה's case of a כהן who sells an animal 'except for the gifts.'
