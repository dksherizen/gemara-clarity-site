Topic

Signs of kosher eggs: how far they can be trusted, and what the 'mixed egg is a שרץ egg' rule teaches

Overview

The amud opens with the last words of an earlier ברייתא listing foods we may get from a non-Jew: 'fish embryos.' The גמרא then quotes a ברייתא giving signs of a kosher egg: one round end and one pointed end, with the white around the yolk. If eggs have their own signs, why rely on the seller? The answers that the eggs were cut, or beaten in a bowl, are rejected, because beaten eggs may not be bought from a non-Jew at all. רבי זירא then says the egg signs are not reliable by Torah law, and proves it from רב אסי's eight birds of doubtful status. So the ברייתא is re-read: bad signs alone prove an egg is non-kosher, but good signs count only when the seller names a kosher bird, since some crow eggs look like pigeon eggs. Next the גמרא asks why the ברייתא says a mixed egg is a שרץ egg. רב עוקבא בר חמא says it matters for טומאה, but רבינא objects that it may be a snake's egg, so רבא says it matters for מלקות if someone eats a formed embryo. The גמרא challenges רבא, since a formed embryo in a kosher egg should also bring מלקות, and starts quoting a ברייתא that continues on the next amud.
