Topic

Whether a bird's שחיטה is from the Torah, and whether cutting only the windpipe is enough for a bird.

Overview

The גמרא finishes testing the claim of רבי יצחק בן פנחס that a bird has no Torah requirement of שחיטה. A proof from a ברייתא about covering blood is pushed off by saying the case is a חיה. A משנה about מליקה done with a knife does seem to require Torah שחיטה for birds. The גמרא answers that רבי יצחק בן פנחס follows רבי אלעזר הקפר, who learns from a פסוק that a צבי and an איל need שחיטה but a bird needs it only מדברי סופרים. רבי disagrees and learns the rules of שחיטה, including cutting most of one סימן in a bird, from 'כאשר צויתך'. Then רב נחמן and רב אדא בר אהבה argue whether that one סימן can be either pipe or must be the ושט. Several sources are brought on each side and pushed off, until a ברייתא on מליקה of a חטאת העוף refutes רב אדא בר אהבה. The גמרא then wonders whether מליקה is different from regular שחיטה. It begins a story about a duck in רבא's house to settle the question, and the story continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַאי לָאו בְּעוֹף,

דְּקָא בָעֵי לֵיהּ לִדְמֵיהּ לְיָנִיכָא?

where he needs its blood for a moth?

No, it is about a wild animal,

דְּקָא בָעֵי לֵיהּ לִדְמֵיהּ לְלַכָּא.

where he needs its blood for dye.
