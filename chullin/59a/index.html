Chullin 59a

חולין נ״ט ע״א

Open Chullin 59a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Dangerous foods, the snake-bitten deer, and the signs of kosher animals, birds, grasshoppers and fish

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a plant definition and bringing health warnings. רב יהודה and רבי אבהו warn about eating חלתית on an empty stomach, and רב יוסף warns about a heavy mix of foods eaten in summer. Next comes a story about a deer brought to the ריש גלותא. רב checked it and found it kosher, but שמואל worried it had been bitten by a snake. An oven test proved him right. A new משנה then teaches that the Torah gives the kosher signs for בהמה and חיה, while חכמים gave signs for birds and grasshoppers, and fish need fins and scales. The גמרא explains a ברייתא about teeth: an animal with no upper front teeth is kosher. רב חסדא gives three backup tests for an animal whose signs are damaged: teeth, hooves, and flesh. For each test he names the one exception to watch for: the young camel, the חזיר, and the ערוד. Finally the גמרא asks why a חיה needs its own signs, and רבי זירא's answer begins at the end of the amud and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

עִיקָּרָא דְּמָרִירְתָּא.

It is the root of a bitter plant.

אָמַר רַב יְהוּדָה:

Rav Yehuda says:

הַאי מַאן דַּאֲכַל

This one who eats

תְּלָתָא תִּקְלֵי חִלְתִּית

three shekels of asafoetida
