Topic

The cooked foreleg of the נזיר's ram as a source for ביטול: the meaning of 'cooked', sixty versus one hundred, and the word 'זהו'

Overview

The פסוק in במדבר 6:19 says the כהן takes the 'cooked foreleg' (זרוע בשלה) of the נזיר's ram. The foreleg goes only to a כהן, while the rest of the ram is eaten by the נזיר even if he is not a כהן. A ברייתא gives two readings of 'cooked': the תנא קמא says the foreleg is whole, and רבי שמעון בן יוחאי says it is cooked together with the ram. The גמרא offers two ways to explain their argument, and shows that under one of them everyone can learn the rule of ביטול from this case, while under the other only רבי שמעון בן יוחאי can. It then explains that the opinions of 'sixty' and 'one hundred' both come from the foreleg: one measures meat and bones, the other meat alone. The גמרא challenges this from a ברייתא that calls the foreleg 'זהו', 'this one', which seems to make it a one-time exception. אביי answers that 'זהו' is for רבי יהודה, who holds that a same-kind mixture is not nullified, based on the blood of the פר and שעיר. The foreleg is a חידוש that teaches no general rule, though it can still teach the stricter measures of sixty or one hundred. רבא answers instead that 'זהו' marks an exception to the rule of טעם כעיקר in קדשים.
