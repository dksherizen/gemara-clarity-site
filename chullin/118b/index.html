Topic

The sources and limits of יד (handle) and שומר (covering) for food טומאה: their פסוקים, הכשר, and minimum sizes

Overview

Background: a יד is a handle-like part of food, such as a stalk, and a שומר is a protective covering, such as a shell or peel. The גמרא first asks where we learn that a יד brings טומאה into food. It settles on the פסוק about food (ויקרא 11:38), and explains that the extra פסוק about the שומר of a נבלה teaches its own topic, even though a קל וחומר could teach it. When a פסוק can teach something new, though, we read it that way. רב חביבא offers another answer: the שומר of a נבלה acts like a handle, so its extra teaching goes to the law of יד. Next, רב יהודה בר ישמעאל challenges learning שומר from 'on any sowing seed', using a pomegranate and parts of meat that are never sown. The גמרא answers that three 'sowing' words cover seeds, trees, and meat, eggs and fish. Then רב and רבי יוחנן argue whether a handle also counts for הכשר, and the גמרא explains their argument from a פסוק or from logic; a ברייתא supports רבי יוחנן. Finally they argue whether a handle or covering counts for food smaller than a כזית or a כפול. A ברייתא about two bones with half-olives of flesh challenges both views, and the discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאֵימָא: אִם אֵינוֹ עִנְיָן

But say: if it is not needed for the matter

לְשׁוֹמֵר דִּנְבֵלָה,

of the covering of a carcass,

תְּנֵהוּ עִנְיָן לְשׁוֹמֵר דְּעָלְמָא,

apply it to the matter of coverings in general,

שׁוֹמֵר לְהַכְנִיס וְשׁוֹמֵר לְצָרֵף,

a covering brings in, and a covering joins together,
