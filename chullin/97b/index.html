Chullin 97b

חולין צ״ז ע״ב

Open Chullin 97b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Cancelling forbidden food in sixty: tasting, salted meat with the גיד הנשה, how to measure, and the cases of the גיד, כחל and egg

Overview

This amud finishes the rules for forbidden food that gets mixed into permitted food. When the two foods taste different, we rely on tasting: a Jew tastes if the mixture is permitted to him, and a קפילא (non-Jewish cook) tastes if it might be forbidden. When tasting is impossible, we need sixty times as much permitted food. Next comes a real case: thighs were salted with the גיד הנשה still inside. רבינא forbade them, while רב אחא בר רב אשי and רב אשי permitted them. רבינא's view is left with a קשיא, because שמואל's rule מליח כרותח means salted food is like roasted food, and roasted meat only needs peeling. The גמרא then teaches what counts toward the sixty (רבי חנינא) and that every forbidden food is measured as if it were as strong as onion or leek (רבי יוחנן). רב נחמן rules on the גיד, the כחל and a non-kosher egg, and the גמרא explains that the כחל itself stays forbidden like a piece of נבלה. Finally, רב אידי בר אבין asks whether an egg gives off taste at all, and אביי's answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וַאֲמוּר רַבָּנַן בְּשִׁשִּׁים.

And the Sages said: in sixty.

הִלְכָּךְ, מִין בְּשֶׁאֵינוֹ מִינוֹ

Therefore, a type mixed with what is not its type:

דְּהֶיתֵּרָא – בְּטַעְמָא,

where it is permitted, we go by tasting,

דְּאִיסּוּרָא – בְּקַפִּילָא.

where it is forbidden, we go by a non-Jewish cook.
