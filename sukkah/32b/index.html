Topic

Iron Mountain palms, minimum lengths of the ארבעה מינים, and the identity and עבות requirement of the הדס

Overview

The amud opens with a second version of אביי's teaching about the לולב from צִינֵּי הַר הַבַּרְזֶל. A palm whose leaves reach one another is כשר, and one with bare gaps is פסול. An aggadic tradition places these palms at the entrance to גיהנם. The גמרא then sets the minimum lengths: שמואל says three טפחים for the הדס and ערבה and four for the לולב, and רבי יוחנן adds that the spine itself must rise one טפח above the הדס. רבי טרפון's 'אמה of five טפחים' is explained two ways, by רב דימי and by רבין. The rule that a rounded number is assumed only לחומרא shows that שמואל follows רבין's smaller measure. A new משנה lists what makes a הדס פסול, and the גמרא proves from the פסוק ״עֲנַף עֵץ עָבוֹת״ that the plant is the הדס, ruling out the olive tree, the דולבא and the הירדוף. It then defines עבות as three leaves per base, against רב כהנא's lenient two-and-one. The amud ends with אביי beginning to answer how a הדס that lost most of its leaves can still be עבות, and his answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאִיכָּא דְּרָמֵי לַיהּ מִירְמֵא:

And there are those who raise it as a contradiction:

תְּנַן צִינֵּי הַר הַבַּרְזֶל — כָּשֵׁר,

We learned in the Mishnah: palms of the Iron Mountain are fit,

וְהָתַנְיָא: פְּסוּלָה!

But isn't it taught in a baraita: it is unfit!

אָמַר אַבָּיֵי, לָא קַשְׁיָא:

Abaye said: It is not difficult:
