Nazir 26a

נזיר כ״ו ע״א

Open Nazir 26a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

When a נזיר's unassigned money (מעות סתומין) goes to נדבה, and what counts as assigned money (מפורשין)

Overview

The amud opens at the end of a challenge. The rule that a dead person's unassigned money (מעות סתומין) goes to נדבה seemed special to a נזיר, yet a ברייתא applies it to people who owe bird-pairs (קינין). The גמרא answers that the משנה taught נזיר, and cases like קינין share the rule because they are similar. The limit is meant to exclude a different ברייתא. There, someone owes a חטאת for a sin and also vowed an unrelated עולה, and his unassigned money must be thrown into ים המלח. Next, רב אשי explains when money counts as assigned (מפורשין): even if the owner named the חטאת, עולה, and שלמים together in one sentence. A second version of רב אשי goes further: even saying 'for my obligation' is enough. Finally, רבא limits the נדבה rule. If the חטאת money has already been separated out, the remaining money counts as assigned.

Opening lines

שֶׁהִפְרִישׁוּ מָעוֹת לְקִינֵּיהֶם,

who separated money for their bird-pairs,

רָצָה לְהָבִיא בָּהֶן חַטַּאת בְּהֵמָה — יָבִיא,

if he wants to bring an animal sin-offering with it, he may bring it,

עוֹלַת בְּהֵמָה — יָבִיא.

an animal burnt-offering, he may bring it.

מֵת וְהָיוּ לוֹ מָעוֹת סְתוּמִין

If he died and had unassigned money,

The steps (6)

  1. ChallengeA ברייתא applies the rule of unassigned money to people who owe bird-pairs
  2. ResolutionThe משנה taught נזיר, and bird-pairs are similar to it
  3. StatementThe excluded case: an unrelated חטאת and עולה paid from 'my obligation' money
  4. Statementרב אשי: naming all three offerings together still counts as assigned
  5. StatementA second version of רב אשי: even 'for my obligation' counts as assigned
  6. Statementרבא: once the חטאת money is separated, the rest counts as assigned
