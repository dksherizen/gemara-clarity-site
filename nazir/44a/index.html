Topic

A כהן and an incomplete body (מת חסר), and the משנה comparing the three prohibitions of a נזיר, tested by a series of קל וחומר arguments

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא that seems to go against רב, who said a כהן does not become טמא for a relative whose body is incomplete. The ברייתא lets a כהן become טמא even for a spine, a skull, or most of the bones. The גמרא answers that this ברייתא follows רבי יהודה. רב follows a different תנא, who tells how the חכמים ruled that 'לאביו' applies only when the father's body is whole. Then a new משנה lists the three things forbidden to a נזיר: טומאה, shaving, and grape products. It explains that each one is stricter than the others in a different way: what cancels days, what has exceptions, and what needs a קרבן. The גמרא then tests every comparison with a קל וחומר. Each time, a פסוק or a practical reason blocks it. For example, shaving cancels days only because the נזיר's hair must grow back, so wine, which leaves the hair in place, cancels nothing.

Opening lines

יָכוֹל לֹא יִטַּמֵּא

One might think he may not become impure

וּלְרוֹב בִּנְיָינוֹ וּלְרוֹב מִנְיָינוֹ

of other relatives.
