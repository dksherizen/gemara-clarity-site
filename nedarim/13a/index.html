Topic

Is the dispute about vowing with בשר בכור the same as the question of whether a person means the animal before or after זריקת דמים? The גמרא says no: the dispute is really about whether a נדר can take hold through something forbidden by the Torah (דבר האסור) rather than by a vow (דבר הנדור).

Overview

The amud opens by asking whether the dispute about someone who compares his meat to בשר בכור depends on which stage of the animal he means, before or after זריקת דמים. The גמרא answers no: everyone agrees he means the stage before the sprinkling. The real argument is whether a vow can be attached to a דבר האסור, something the Torah forbids, or only to a דבר הנדור, something forbidden through a vow. The one who permits learns from 'כי ידר' that only a דבר הנדור works, and uses 'לה׳' to include חטאת and אשם, which a person makes holy by declaring them. The one who forbids points to a ברייתא in the name of רבי that there is a מצוה to declare a בכור holy. The other side answers that a בכור is holy even without that declaration. Next, a ברייתא lists Temple items like the lamb, the pens, the altar and ירושלים, and the גמרא identifies it as רבי מאיר. It then reconciles the ברייתא's second half with his view by splitting 'not for a lamb' from 'for a lamb'. A new משנה follows about vows that use the names of offerings. The גמרא says its unattributed author is רבי מאיר, then challenges this from a ברייתא that permits 'הא קרבן', which only swears by the life of an offering. The answer to that challenge comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

דְּמַחֵית בְּשַׂר בְּכוֹר

where he places meat of a firstborn animal

וּמַחֵית בָּשָׂר דְּהַאיְךְ גַּבֵּיהּ,

and places other meat next to it,

וְאָמַר ״זֶה כָּזֶה״.

and he said: 'This is like this.'

וְתַנָּאֵי הִיא.
