Topic

What does ״מוּדְּרַנִי הֵימָךְ״ forbid: one side, both sides, or no food at all? (Reworking the views of רבי יוסי ברבי חנינא and שמואל)

Overview

This amud asks what happens when someone says ״מוּדְּרַנִי הֵימָךְ״ ("I am avowed from you"). First the גמרא says that this plain phrase forbids both people, and it backs this up with רבי יוסי ברבי חנינא. A משנה about ״הריני עליך חרם״ seems to forbid only one side, and the גמרא answers twice that the speaker there spelled out that the other side stays permitted. But the end of that משנה implies that a plain vow forbids only one side. So the גמרא re-states רבי יוסי ברבי חנינא: ״מודר אני לך״ forbids both people, while ״מודרני הימך״ forbids only the speaker. This clashes with how שמואל's words on our משנה were explained, so the גמרא re-states שמואל as being about eating versus all benefit. That reading does not fit שמואל's wording ״עד שיאמר״. In the final version, the plain phrases do not forbid food at all, because each one naturally means a different kind of separation: no talking, no business, or not standing within four אמות. Only adding "that I eat" or "that I taste" makes it a food vow.

Opening lines

אֲבָל אָמַר ״מוּדְּרַנִי הֵימָךְ״ לְחוֹדֵיהּ —

But if he said "I am avowed from you" by itself,

שְׁנֵיהֶן אֲסוּרִין.

both of them are forbidden.

כִּי הָא דְּאָמַר רַבִּי יוֹסֵי בְּרַבִּי חֲנִינָא:

This is like what רבי יוסי ברבי חנינא said:

״מוּדְּרַנִי הֵימָךְ״ — שְׁנֵיהֶן אֲסוּרִין.

"I am avowed from you": both of them are forbidden.
