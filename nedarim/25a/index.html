Topic

Whose understanding governs an oath, משה's oath wording, and the exaggerated vow about a snake like an olive-press beam

Overview

The amud opens with רב אשי's answer that an oath follows the plain, common meaning of its words, so we never imagine a person secretly meant ants. The גמרא challenges this from the court's warning before an oath: 'we make you swear by our understanding, not by a condition in your heart.' It answers that the warning blocks a trick like the cane of רבא, where a borrower hid coins in a cane and handed it to the lender while swearing he had paid. A second challenge comes from משה, who made ישראל swear 'by my understanding and God's.' The גמרא answers that this blocks a real second meaning of a word: an idol can truly be called אלוה. It then asks why משה didn't pick a clearer oath wording, and tests several, from מצוות to תורה כולה. Each can be read narrowly until '613 מצוות' is suggested. The final answer is that משה simply chose a wording that was easy on the people. The amud ends with the משנה's exaggerated vow about a snake 'like the beam of an olive press,' which means a snake notched on its back. That wording also teaches that a real olive-press beam must be notched, which matters for sales.

Opening lines

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: כִּי מִשְׁתְּבַע

He said to him: When he swears,

אַדַּעְתָּא דִידַן מִשְׁתְּבַע,

he swears based on our understanding,

וַאֲנַן לָא מַסְּקִינַן נַפְשִׁין אַשּׁוּמְשְׁמָנֵי.

and we do not bring ourselves to think of ants.

וְעַל דַּעְתָּא דְנַפְשֵׁיהּ

And according to his own understanding,
