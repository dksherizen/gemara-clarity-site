Topic

Leftover figs after the harvest, רבי טרפון's regret, and when one may use the honor of תורה

Overview

A ברייתא teaches that once most of the fig-cutting knives are put away, the leftover figs are treated as ownerless: taking them is not גזל, and they need no מעשר. The גמרא tells three stories about this. רבי eats the figs, but רבי יוסי בר רבי יהודה refuses, because he thinks the owner's invitation was only polite. רבי חמא בר רבי חנינא proves to his attendant that eating is allowed. Then an owner nearly drowns רבי טרפון, who saves himself by saying his name, and he regrets all his life that he used the crown of תורה. רבי יוחנן teaches with a קל וחומר from בלשצר that whoever uses the crown of תורה is uprooted from the world. The גמרא explains that the owner took רבי טרפון for a thief, and that רבי טרפון regretted it because he could have paid the man off. Related teachings follow: learn תורה out of love and not for honor, and do not use it as a crown or as a digging tool. Finally רבא rules that a person may make himself known where he is a stranger, and that a תלמיד חכם may ask to be heard first, just as a כהן takes first. The source for the כהן, וקדשתו, continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תָּנָא:

It was taught:

הוּקְפְּלוּ רוֹב הַמַּקְצוּעוֹת —

If most of the knives have been folded up and put away,

מוּתָּרוֹת מִשּׁוּם גֵּזֶל,

they are permitted with regard to stealing,

וּפְטוּרוֹת מִן הַמַּעַשְׂרוֹת.

and they are exempt from tithes.
