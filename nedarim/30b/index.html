Topic

How the words of a נדר are understood: seafarers, 'those who see the sun,' 'the black-headed ones,' and ילודים and נולדים

Overview

The גמרא finishes explaining who counts as יורדי הים (seafarers) and who counts as יושבי היבשה (land-dwellers). In a second version, everyone counts as a land-dweller, because even sailors come back to land. Next comes a series of new משניות about the words people use in a נדר. A vow from 'those who see the sun' includes the blind, because it covers everyone the sun shines on. A vow from 'the black-headed ones' includes bald and gray-haired men but not women or children, because the name belongs to men. A vow from ילודים does not include נולדים, but a vow from נולדים includes ילודים. רבי מאיר disagrees, and the גמרא explains his opinion by saying the משנה is missing words. רב פפא and אביי then show from פסוקים that נולדים can mean either 'already born' or 'will be born,' so for vows we follow everyday speech. Finally, the חכמים's phrase 'whose nature is to be born' leaves out fish and birds.

Opening lines

הַהוֹלְכִים מֵעַכּוֹ לְיָפוֹ,

[not] those who travel from Akko to Jaffa,

דְּהָלֵין יוֹשְׁבֵי הַיַּבָּשָׁה נִינְהוּ,

for these are land-dwellers,

אֶלָּא מִמִּי שֶׁדַּרְכָּן לְפָרֵשׁ.

rather, [it means] from those whose way is to set sail.

וּמַאן דְּמַתְנֵי אַסֵּיפָא,

And the one who teaches it on the latter clause
