Topic

Redeeming a Jew sold to a non-Jew: the lower price, and whether a slave or a walled-city house can be redeemed in parts

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a proof that a Jew who sold himself to a non-Jew should be treated strictly, since his sins brought him so low. That proof is pushed back, because the Torah commands that he be redeemed. רב נחמן בר יצחק then shows from two פסוקים, and from the extra word 'בשנים', that he always pays the lower price: the original price if his value rose, his current value if it fell. Next, רב הונא בר חיננא asks רב ששת whether such a slave can be redeemed in parts. רב ששת says no: like a thief who is sold whole, he is redeemed whole. אביי shows that partial redemption can help or hurt, depending on whether the slave's value rises or falls. רב הונא בר חיננא then asks the same question about a house in a walled city. רב ששת answers from רבי שמעון's reasoning that it may be redeemed with borrowed money and in parts. A ברייתא is brought that seems to say the opposite, and the גמרא begins to answer with 'לא קשיא'. The answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

זֶה הַנִּמְכָּר לַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה עַצְמָהּ.

This is the one who is sold to idol worship itself.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: הָתָם, הָא אַהְדְּרֵיהּ קְרָא,

He said to him: There, the verse has brought him back,

דְּתָנֵי דְּבֵי רַבִּי יִשְׁמָעֵאל:

as the school of Rabbi Yishmael taught:

הוֹאִיל וְהָלַךְ זֶה
