Topic

Testing רב אסי's rule that money in the תורה means Tyrian silver, rewording it to cover fixed amounts only, and a new source for בית שמאי's דינר minimum for קידושין.

Overview

Earlier on the דף, רב אסי taught that whenever the תורה says כסף (money), it means a full silver coin, a כסף צורי. The גמרא first tests this rule with three cases: the oath over a claim, מעשר שני, and הקדש. Each time it answers that the case has a special source of its own: a comparison to 'vessels', the extra word 'the' in the פסוק, or a גזירה שוה from מעשר. A fourth challenge comes from קידושין. There בית הלל allow just a פרוטה, so רב אסי would seem to agree only with בית שמאי. The גמרא therefore rewords רב אסי's rule: only a fixed amount in the תורה is Tyrian money, while amounts set by the חכמים are cheaper local money. The new point turns out to be the rabbinic half: the חכמים' fine of a סלע for striking someone is only half a זוז. Then ריש לקיש offers a different source for בית שמאי's דינר minimum, from חזקיה's teaching that an אמה עבריה can deduct from her redemption price. The גמרא pushes back on that proof, and the discussion continues on the next amud.
