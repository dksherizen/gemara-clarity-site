Kiddushin 49b

קידושין מ״ט ע״ב

Topic

קידושין on conditions about the man himself, the ten portions of traits, and why unspoken intentions do not count

Overview

The amud opens by explaining what a man must know when he betroths a woman on condition that he knows תורה, and the exact wording of his claim changes the standard. A ברייתא then goes through conditions a man might set about himself: a תלמיד, a חכם, a גבור, or an עשיר. For each one it sets a realistic minimum instead of demanding greatness. If he says he is a צדיק or a רשע, the קידושין can take effect even if he seems to be the opposite, because one thought can change a person in a moment. The גמרא then brings an אגדה about ten portions of each trait coming down to the world. It stops to work out why arrogance belongs to עילם and not to בבל. A new משנה rules that when a man betroths on a spoken condition about himself that turns out false, she is not betrothed. This holds even if she says she didn't care, and the same is true when she misled him. The גמרא then tells of a man who sold his property planning to move to ארץ ישראל. רבא rules that unspoken intentions do not count, and the search for רבא's source continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַאי תּוֹרָה –

What is 'Torah'?

מִדְרַשׁ תּוֹרָה.

The interpretation of the Torah.

וְהָנֵי מִילֵּי דַּאֲמַר לַהּ: ״תָּנֵינָא״,

And this applies when he said to her, 'I have learned,'

אֲבָל אֲמַר לַהּ: ״תַּנָּא אֲנָא״,

but if he said to her, 'I am a תנא,'
