Kiddushin 46b

קידושין מ״ו ע״ב

Topic

רב and שמואל on money given to betroth one's sister, and רבינא's challenge from חלה separated from flour.

Overview

The amud opens with a rule from the earlier discussion: when קידושין does not take effect, the money normally goes back. Then רב and שמואל argue about a man who gives קידושין money to his own sister. Both agree he knows it cannot work. רב says he meant it as a פקדון, so it is returned, and שמואל says he meant it as a gift, so she keeps it. רבינא challenges שמואל from a משנה: חלה separated from flour is not חלה and must be returned to the owner, so why isn't it a gift? The גמרא answers that the flour is returned for a practical reason: someone might think the dough was already fixed and come to eat טבל. It defends this answer by explaining that people know the rule but misunderstand its reason, and that people only listen to a second separation when there are two separate containers. Then it shows that the bitter cucumber case is different, because there the תרומה is real by Torah law, as רבי אלעאי learns from a פסוק. The amud ends as רבא begins a new statement, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וּשְׁמַע מִינַּהּ:

And conclude from it:

מָעוֹת בְּעָלְמָא – חוֹזְרִים.

generally, money is returned.

אִיתְּמַר: הַמְקַדֵּשׁ אֲחוֹתוֹ,

It was stated: one who betroths his sister,

רַב אָמַר: מָעוֹת חוֹזְרִים.

Rav says: the money is returned.
