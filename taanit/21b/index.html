Topic

When to fast for plagues and other dangers, and stories of humility and hidden merit

Overview

This amud opens with the end of a ברייתא: three deaths on one day, or spread over four days, are not a דֶּבֶר. In דרוקרת three people died on one day and רב נחמן בר רב חסדא declared a fast. רב נחמן בר יצחק pointed out that this fits only רבי מאיר's minority view about an ox's gorings. That exchange leads to a warm back-and-forth about who should move to whom, with רבי יוסי's teaching that a person honors his place, proved from הר סיני and the אהל מועד. Two stories follow, about a plague in סורא and a fire in דרוקרת. In each, a neighborhood was protected not by a great rabbi but by an ordinary person's kindness. Next come cases where אמוראים fasted over locusts, a pig plague, a distant plague in בי חוזאי, and a plague in ארץ ישראל. The גמרא explains why each danger could reach them. Finally, the story of אבא אומנא the bloodletter shows how his modesty, his care for the poor and his generosity to scholars earned him daily greetings from Heaven. The story of אביי's test of him continues on the next amud.
