Topic

נקדימון's miracles, the sun breaking through for משה, and רב יהודה's harsh פסוקים read as blessings (the reed and the cedar)

Overview

The amud finishes the story of נקדימון בן גוריון. He prays in the בית המקדש and rain fills the twelve cisterns he borrowed for the עולי רגלים. When the official claims the sun had already set, נקדימון prays again and the sun breaks through, so his real name בוני became נקדימון. A ברייתא lists three people for whom the sun broke through: משה, יהושע and נקדימון. Three אמוראים then show where we learn it about משה. The גמרא returns to the משנה about a city without rain, and רב says both cities are cursed. This opens a series of רב יהודה's readings that turn harsh פסוקים into ברכות: ירושלים as a נדה, as a widow, Israel as lowly, and Israel as a reed. The reed that bends and survives is contrasted with בלעם's stiff cedar. A ברייתא teaches that a person should be soft like a reed. A story about רבי אלעזר ברבי שמעון, proud of his learning, begins and continues on the next amud.
