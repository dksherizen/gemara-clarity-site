Topic

Mourning practices of תשעה באב (work, eating, turning over the bed) and why ט״ו באב was a day of joy

Overview

The amud opens by finishing the story of how רבי יהודה בר אילעי ate his last meal before תשעה באב: he sat in the lowliest spot in the house, like a mourner. Next comes a משנה on doing work on תשעה באב. It depends on local custom, but תלמידי חכמים never work, and רבן שמעון בן גמליאל says everyone should act like a תלמיד חכם. Several ברייתות stress how serious the day is. Eating on it is compared to eating on יום הכפורים, and work done that day brings no blessing. Only those who mourn for ירושלים will see its future joy. The גמרא then discusses רבי יהודה's view that the bed must be turned over on תשעה באב. After a back-and-forth about who is able to do it and about the other beds in the house, רבא rules that the חכמים rejected רבי יהודה completely. Finally, the גמרא asks why ט״ו באב was one of the happiest days for ישראל. It brings several answers: the tribes were allowed to marry each other, בנימין was let back in, the deaths in the מדבר ended, and the road guards of ירבעם were removed. עולא's account continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

בֵּין תַּנּוּר לְכִירַיִים וְאוֹכֵל,

between the oven and the stove, and he would eat,

וְשׁוֹתֶה עָלֶיהָ קִיתוֹן שֶׁל מַיִם,

and he would drink a jug of water with it,

וְדוֹמֶה כְּמִי שֶׁמֵּתוֹ מוּטָּל לְפָנָיו.

and he looked like one whose dead relative is lying before him.

תְּנַן הָתָם:

We learned in a mishna there:
