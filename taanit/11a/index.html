Topic

Sharing in communal suffering, divine judgment, and whether fasting is a sin or holy

Overview

The amud opens in the middle of a discussion about why a traveler should eat little on the road. The גמרא shows when the 'stomach' reason and the 'food supply' reason give different results, and it tells us what רב פפא did and why. Next come teachings about famine years. רב says that someone who holds back from eating in a famine is saved from an unusual death. ריש לקיש says couples may not have relations in famine years, and a ברייתא adds that those with no children yet may. ברייתות then teach that a person must share the צבור's suffering, as משה did when he sat on a stone, and must never separate himself or party privately. They also describe who testifies against a person who sinned in secret, and they explain the fairness of ה׳'s judgment from 'אל אמונה ואין עול צדיק וישר הוא'. Finally the גמרא returns to fasting. שמואל says one who fasts is called a חוטא, and רבי אלעזר says he is called קדוש; both learn this from the נזיר. The גמרא then begins to question whether רבי אלעזר really holds this, and that discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

דְּיָתֵיב בְּאַרְבָּא.

Where he is sitting in a boat.

אִי נָמֵי, דְּקָאָזֵיל מֵאַוּוֹנָא לְאַוּוֹנָא.

Or else, where he travels from station to station.

רַב פָּפָּא — כׇּל פַּרְסָה וּפַרְסָה

Rav Pappa, for each and every parasang,

אָכֵיל חֲדָא רִיפְתָּא.

would eat one loaf of bread.
