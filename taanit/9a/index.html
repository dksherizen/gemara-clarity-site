Topic

מעשר and wealth, stories of ריש לקיש's son, and whether sustenance comes for one person

Overview

The amud opens with the דרשה that ״עשר תעשר״ means: give מעשר so that you become wealthy. In a story, רבי יוחנן teaches this to ריש לקיש's young son and tells him to go test it. When the boy objects that we may not test ה׳, רבי יוחנן answers from a פסוק in מלאכי that מעשר is the one exception. In a second story, the boy finds a hint in the תורה, from יוסף's brothers, for a פסוק in משלי, and his mother pulls him away from רבי יוחנן. The גמרא then brings רבי יוחנן's teaching that rain can come for one person, but sustenance comes only for the many. A ברייתא about the well, the clouds and the מן, given in the merit of מרים, אהרן and משה, seems to show sustenance coming for one person. The גמרא answers that משה asked for the many, so he counts like the many. The amud ends by starting a story about רבא's students and רב פפא.

Opening lines

״עַשֵּׂר תְּעַשֵּׂר״ —

"A tithe shall you tithe"

עַשֵּׂר בִּשְׁבִיל שֶׁתִּתְעַשֵּׁר.

Tithe so that you will become wealthy.

אַשְׁכְּחֵיהּ רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן לְיָנוֹקָא דְּרֵישׁ לָקִישׁ,

Rabbi Yochanan found the young son of Reish Lakish,

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: אִימָּא לִי פְּסוּקָיךְ!

He said to him: Tell me your verse!
