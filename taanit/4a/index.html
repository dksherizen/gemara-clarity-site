Topic

The drizzle and the growing scholar; a scholar's temper; improper requests answered by ה׳; who taught when we ask for rain

Overview

The amud opens by finishing the גמרא's list of kinds of rain with the gentle drizzle, which helps even a seed buried under a clod of earth. רבא uses that image for a young Torah scholar: once he starts to grow, he keeps growing. Sayings follow about the 'fire' and 'iron' of a תלמיד חכם, and רבינא balances them: a person must still train himself to be gentle. Next, רבי יונתן lists three people who made risky requests. אליעזר and שאול were answered well, but יפתח was answered badly, and two פסוקים in ירמיהו are read as hints to his story. רבי ברכיה adds that כנסת ישראל also asked improperly, comparing ה׳ to rain and to a seal, and ה׳ answered with something better and constant: dew, and engraving Israel on His palms. Finally the גמרא returns to the משנה and asks whose opinion says we ask for rain only near the rainy season. רבא says רבי יהושע, and אביי says it could even be רבי אליעזר, since asking and mentioning are separate. A second version of this discussion begins and continues on the next amud.
