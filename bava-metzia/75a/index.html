Topic

Loans of produce and other cases of רבית: wheat loans, הלל's strict view, gifts between תלמידי חכמים, and trading farm work.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא about lending wheat. רב ששת explains that it is talking about a loan where no price was set: if wheat gets cheaper the lender takes wheat back, and if it gets more expensive the borrower pays the old value. A new משנה forbids borrowing wheat to repay at harvest, because a price rise would be רבית. It allows a very short loan, like 'until I find the key', but הלל forbids even that, and even lending a loaf of bread without setting its value. The גמרא brings רב הונא and רבי יצחק on how owning some grain makes such a loan allowed, and רבי חייא's teaching supports רבי יצחק. שמואל rules like הלל, but the גמרא says the הלכה is not like him, and the חכמים let neighbors borrow and repay casually. שמואל then teaches about friends who are strict with each other when sharing meals, and says תלמידי חכמים may give each other extra on a loan as a gift. רב's idea of lending to one's family with רבית as a lesson is rejected. A new משנה closes the amud: neighbors may swap the same kind of farm work, but not different kinds.
