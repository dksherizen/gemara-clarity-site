Topic

Advance payments, discounts and sharecropping: when a deal is fair business and when it looks like ריבית

Overview

This amud deals with deals that can look like ריבית, meaning payment for waiting for money. It first finishes a ברייתא: a deal is allowed if the seller carries the risk, repaying produce in another town is allowed only if the borrower owns produce there, and donkey drivers may charge the cheaper town's price. רב פפא and רב אחא בריה דרב איקא give two business reasons for the drivers' leniency, and the reasons give different results for a new merchant. רב bought wheat this way but took less than the full amount, because an important person is stricter with himself. רבי יוחנן tells stories showing that such deals were not allowed with linen garments or metal scraps. רב and שמואל argue about buying an orchard's produce in advance at a discount, though רב agrees it is allowed with oxen, since their risk of loss is high. שמואל and רבא give practical advice on how to set up such deals so they are permitted. Finally, רבא defends his own deal with his sharecroppers against the charge that he takes ריבית.
