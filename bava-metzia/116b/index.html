Topic

Two-story houses: dividing the materials when a house owned by two people collapses, and the rights of an upstairs renter whose floor breaks.

Overview

The amud opens with a משנה about a two-story house owned by two people that collapses. The owners split the fallen materials, the court looks at which stones were likely to break, and a man who recognizes his own stones takes them as part of his share. The גמרא first narrows the even split to cases where nobody can know how the house fell or whose stones they are. It then explains that the partner who recognizes his stones wins only because the other partner says 'I don't know.' The גמרא shows this fits רב נחמן, because the case involves an oath that partner cannot take. רבא and אביי then argue over whether the recognized stones count against the broken stones or the whole ones. A second משנה rules that a renter whose upper story breaks may live downstairs until the owner repairs it, and רבי יוסי adds how two owners share the cost of the ceiling. The גמרא discusses how big the break must be, where רב and שמואל disagree, and what wording of the rental gives the renter a claim on the house below. רב אשי's answer is backed by a case about a vine sold 'on top of this peach tree.' The amud ends as רבי אבא בר ממל begins a new question.
