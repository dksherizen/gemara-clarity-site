Bava Metzia 22b

בבא מציעא כ״ב ע״ב

Topic

יאוש שלא מדעת is settled like אביי; wind-fallen dates; and whether a trampled סימן or the place of loss counts as a סימן.

Overview

The גמרא finishes its long test of the argument between אביי and רבא about יאוש שלא מדעת (giving up hope before the owner even knows the item is lost). A proof from the laws of הכשר is rejected. The פסוק of כי יותן, as רב פפא explains it, requires the wetting to happen with the owner's knowledge, and that is a special rule for הכשר alone. Then a פסוק about an item swept away by a river shows that an item lost only to its owner must be returned even when it has no סימן. That refutes רבא, and the הלכה follows אביי, one of the six cases of יע"ל קג"ם. רב אחא בריה דרבא then asks how we may eat dates blown down by the wind. רב אשי answers that the owner gives up on them in advance, because creatures eat them. Those dates are forbidden, though, when the land is known to belong to orphans or the trees are fenced in. Next the גמרא turns to bundles found in a public area. רבה and רבא argue whether a סימן likely to be trampled counts, and whether the place where an item was lost counts as a סימן. Each of them reads a ברייתא about bundles and sheaves according to his own view, and רבה's explanation continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֵינָן בְּכִי יוּתַּן.

They are not under the law of 'when [water] is placed.'

טַעְמָא מַאי?

What is the reason?

לָאו מִשּׁוּם דְּלָא אָמְרִינַן

Is it not because we do not say

כֵּיוָן דְּאִיגַּלַּאי מִילְּתָא

since the matter has been revealed
