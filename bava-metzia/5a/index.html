Topic

Two rules of רבי חייא about the oath of מודה במקצת: whether 'הילך' is exempt, and whether witnesses proving part of a claim create an oath, ending with the case of the shepherd.

Overview

The גמרא continues the argument between רבי חייא and רב ששת about a defendant who says 'הילך', here it is. It asks רב ששת why the Torah needs to exclude land from the oath, since land is always 'הילך'. It gives two answers: the land was damaged by digging, or the land itself was denied. Next it tries to prove רבי חייא's view from רמי בר חמא's teaching about the four שומרים, but pushes the proof away with a case of a cow that died through negligence. Then a ברייתא from the father of רבי אפטוריקי challenges רבי חייא's other rule, that witnesses proving part of a claim create an oath. The גמרא answers that רבי חייא is a תנא and reads the words 'הוא' and 'זה' in the פסוק differently. Finally comes the story of a shepherd who denied receiving animals, and witnesses said he ate two. רבי זירא ties the owner's oath to רבי חייא's rule. The גמרא asks why רב נחמן's שבועת היסת would not be enough, and begins answering that רב נחמן's oath is only a rabbinic תקנה. That answer continues on the next amud.
