Topic

How a שומר who chooses to pay earns the thief's double payment (כפל): רבא's mechanism, רבי יוחנן on 'saying I will pay', and רב פפא on different guards

Overview

The משנה taught that a שומר who pays the owner for a stolen animal, instead of swearing, gets the thief's double payment (כפל). The גמרא asks how the owner can give away something that may never exist. רבא answers that the owner transfers the cow itself to the שומר, either from the time of the deposit or, in a second version, right before the theft. רבי זירא objects to the first version because of the fleece and offspring, and the גמרא explains the practical differences between רבא's two versions. Next, רבי יוחנן rules that the משנה's word 'paid' only means the שומר said 'I will pay', and a ברייתא about a renter supports him. רב פפא explains which guards earn the כפל by admitting they must pay, and the two versions of his statement disagree about a borrower. רב זביד, quoting אביי, says a borrower must actually pay, and a ברייתא using the word 'went ahead' (קידם) seems to support him. The reciters confirm that the two ברייתות were taught together, so the difference in wording counts. Finally the גמרא begins to discuss a שומר who changes his mind about paying, and this continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מִי יֵימַר דְּמִגַּנְבָא,

Who can say that it will be stolen?

וְאִם תִּמְצֵי לוֹמַר דְּמִגַּנְבָא –

And even if you say that it will be stolen,

מִי יֵימַר דְּמִשְׁתְּכַח גַּנָּב,

who can say that the thief will be found?

וְאִי מִשְׁתְּכַח גַּנָּב –

And even if the thief is found,
