Topic

Does סידור (leaving a person his basic needs), known from ערכין, apply to a debtor and to a pledge to הקדש, and why is collateral not returned for הקדש?

Overview

The גמרא first finishes the earlier topic: the creditor must return the debtor's collateral because the פסוק calls it 'צדקה' for him. Then it asks a new question. With ערכין, a poor person who cannot pay is left his basic needs (סידור). Does a regular debtor get this too, through the shared word 'מך' (poor)? רבין's letter reports a debate about a pledge to בדק הבית. בר פדא and אילפא say there is no סידור for הקדש, using a קל וחומר that assumes a debtor gets none. רבי יוחנן says הקדש does get סידור, because the פסוק links all vows to ערכין. The גמרא then tests two more קל וחומר arguments and blocks each one with a word in a פסוק. 'הוא' keeps סידור away from a debtor. 'צדקה' shows that collateral is not returned for הקדש, after the 'blessing' reason is rejected. Finally, אליהו tells רבה בר אבוה that a debtor does get סידור, learned from ערכין through the word 'poor'.
