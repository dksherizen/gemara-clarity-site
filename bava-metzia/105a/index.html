Topic

Joint ventures and partners, a tenant's duties to the field, and measuring a 'pile' and inferior olives

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a rule: two ventures written in one שטר are counted together, which works against the manager. רבא then rules that a manager who quietly covered a loss cannot later ask the investor to share it. He also rules that when two partners run a venture together, one cannot force a split, even if he offers to keep sharing losses. Next, a משנה rules that a tenant farmer must weed the field. The גמרא explains that weed seeds already in the soil will keep growing after he leaves. A second משנה rules that a tenant must keep working a weak field if it yields enough for a pile. The גמרא then defines how big a pile is: a shovel buried in the grain, or a set amount of two or three סאה. The amud ends by explaining פריצי זיתים, 'wicked' olives that give little oil, and by measuring how little oil that means.

Opening lines

תְּרֵי עִיסְקֵי וְחַד שְׁטָרָא –

Two joint ventures and one document

פְּסֵידָא דְּלֹוֶה.

the loss is the borrower's.

וְאָמַר רָבָא:

And Rava said:

הַאי מַאן דְּקַבֵּיל עִיסְקָא מִן חַבְרֵיהּ

This one who receives a joint venture from his fellow
