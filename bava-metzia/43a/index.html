Topic

Deposits with a שולחני, בעל הבית, or חנווני: when the keeper may use the money and when he pays for loss or אונס. Then how much a שומר pays after misusing a deposit.

Overview

This amud opens with the last line of the previous case. A new משנה then teaches that a שולחני may use deposited money only if it is untied, and that if he may use it, he must pay if it is lost. A בעל הבית may never use it, and רבי מאיר and רבי יהודה argue whether a חנווני is like a בעל הבית or like a שולחני. The גמרא explains that tied money must also be sealed, or according to רב מארי tied with an unusual knot. In another version, רב מארי asks whether an unusual knot is enough and the question stays unresolved. רב הונא says the שולחני pays even for אונס, and רב נחמן disagrees. רב נחמן explains to רבא that the chance to profit makes the שולחני a שומר שכר. רב נחמן also challenges רב הונא from a משנה about a גזבר, and רב הונא answers that the wording 'he spent it' only matches the first part of that משנה. A second משנה brings three opinions on how much a שומר pays after misusing a deposit. The גמרא brings רבה's rule about a stolen barrel of wine, then shows that reading בית הלל's 'time of removal' as the time the item is destroyed does not work.

Opening lines

וּמְשַׁלֵּם לֵיהּ דְּמֵי כִּיסֵי.

And he pays him the value of the hops.

מַתְנִי׳ הַמַּפְקִיד מָעוֹת אֵצֶל שׁוּלְחָנִי,

MISHNA: One who deposits money with a money changer,

אִם צְרוּרִין – לֹא יִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ בָּהֶן,

if it is tied up, he may not use it,

לְפִיכָךְ אִם אָבְדוּ אֵינוֹ חַיָּיב בְּאַחְרָיוּתָן.

therefore, if it is lost, he is not responsible for it.
