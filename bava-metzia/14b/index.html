Topic

A field sold by someone who did not own it: when the buyer can back out, whether he recovers the שבח, and רבא's challenge to שמואל from the משנה about collecting שבח and produce

Overview

The עמוד opens by finishing a ruling about a buyer of a contested field. Before he takes חזקה (by walking its borders) he may back out, but after that he may not. A second version says this holds even if the sale came with אחריות. Next, רב and שמואל argue about a field that turns out not to belong to the seller: רב says the buyer gets back his מעות and the שבח, while שמואל says only the מעות. רב הונא is unsure whether an explicit promise of שבח would help in שמואל's view, but רב נחמן reports that שמואל denies the שבח even then, because it looks like ריבית. רבא challenges this from a משנה in גיטין that lets שבח be collected from free property. The גמרא defends שמואל by reading that clause as a creditor case, and works through two ברייתות about שבח and produce. רבא explains the produce ברייתא as a robber who ate the produce and ruined the field. רבה בר רב הונא then begins another explanation, which continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

יָכוֹל לַחֲזוֹר בּוֹ,

he can back out of it.

מִשֶּׁהֶחְזִיק בָּהּ – אֵינוֹ יָכוֹל לַחֲזוֹר בּוֹ.

Once he took possession of it, he cannot back out of it.

דְּאָמַר לֵיהּ:

Because the seller can say to him:

חַיְיתָא דְקִטְרֵי סְבַרְתְּ וְקַבֵּלְתְּ.

You knowingly accepted a tied-up sack.
