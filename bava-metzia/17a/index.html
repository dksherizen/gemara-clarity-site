Bava Metzia 17a

בבא מציעא י״ז ע״א

Topic

When a debtor's claim "פרעתי" is believed, when he becomes a כפרן, and when a found שטר חוב is returned.

Overview

The amud opens with a debate about a borrower who says "פרעתי" (I paid) after בית דין ruled against him. One view splits the court's wording "צא תן לו" from "חייב אתה ליתן לו". רב זביד says he is believed after either wording. For רב זביד, the wording matters only when witnesses disprove his first "פרעתי" and he says it again: after "צא תן לו" he becomes a כפרן for that money, but after "חייב אתה ליתן לו" he was only stalling. רבי יוחנן adds two more cases of כפרן. The first is someone who denies a loan completely and then claims he paid, backed up by the story of שבתאי and the woolen cloak. The second is someone who falsely claims he took an oath that בית דין required of him. The גמרא then turns to a found שטר חוב with a הנפק dated that same day, which רבי יוחנן says is returned. The גמרא defends this ruling with two explanations: same-day repayment is uncommon, and רב כהנא's case where the borrower admits the debt. The amud ends as רבי יוחנן begins a new teaching about claiming payment after a מעשה בית דין, and the ruling comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאָמַר ״פָּרַעְתִּי״ – נֶאֱמָן.

and he said "I paid" — he is believed.

בָּא מַלְוֶה לִכְתּוֹב –

If the lender comes to have a document written —

אֵין כּוֹתְבִין וְנוֹתְנִין לוֹ.

they do not write it and give it to him.

״חַיָּיב אַתָּה לִיתֵּן לוֹ״

If they said, "You are obligated to give him"

The steps (26)

  5. StatementAfter 'You are obligated to pay' he is not branded a denier
