Topic

Rejecting the comparison to the גט case, reading הטעו as 'backed out,' and the ברייתא's rules for paying workers when a job falls through

Overview

The גמרא first rejects the attempt to compare workers hired through a middleman to רב נחמן's ruling about a שליח carrying a גט. In that case the שליח cancelled his own agency completely, so it shows nothing about whose words the husband relies on. Then the גמרא offers a new reading of the משנה: the word הטעו (deceived) can mean חזרו (backed out of the deal). It proves this from a long ברייתא about hired workers. If either side backs out before work begins, there is only a complaint (תרעומת). If the workers came and found no work, they get the reduced pay of an idle worker. If they quit partway, the court values their work so that they do not gain from quitting, and רבי דוסא calculates this differently. When quitting would cause a loss and no replacements are available, the employer may hire others at their expense or trick them into finishing. Finally, רב and the גמרא discuss whether idle workers get any pay at all. The answer depends on whether the employer checked his field the night before, a distinction that רבא also uses and that continues on the next amud.
