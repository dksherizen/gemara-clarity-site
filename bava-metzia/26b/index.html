Bava Metzia 26b

בבא מציעא כ״ו ע״ב

Open Bava Metzia 26b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

When an owner gives up hope (יאוש) on a lost coin: coins dropped among a group or into sand, a finder's sins, and items found in a store, near a money changer, or inside produce

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא about a סלע seen falling from a group of people. If it fell from one of two, the finder must return it, because the owner can blame the other person and does not give up hope (יאוש). If it fell from one of three, the owner gives up hope, so the finder keeps it. רבא limits this case: if the coin is worth a פרוטה for each of the three, they may be partners who do not give up hope, so the finder must return it. A second version of רבא extends this even to two פרוטות. רבא then lists which commandments a finder breaks, depending on when he took the coin and what he meant to do with it. He also rules that coins that fall into sand belong to the finder, even if the owner is seen sifting for them. A new משנה follows. It decides who owns items found in a store or near a שולחני, based on where they lie, and it rules on coins found inside produce someone bought or received. The גמרא brings רבי אלעזר's ruling that even coins on the money changer's table go to the finder, tests it against the משנה, and has רבא explain its source. ריש לקיש then begins to limit the produce ruling, and his statement continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

שֶׁנָּפַל מִשְּׁנַיִם

that fell from two people

חַיָּיב לְהַחְזִיר.

he is obligated to return it.

מַאי טַעְמָא?

What is the reason?

הָהוּא דִּנְפַל מִינֵּיהּ לָא מִיָּאַשׁ,

The one from whom it fell does not give up hope.

The steps (25)

  1. StatementA coin that fell from one of two people must be returned
  2. ResponseWhy the owner in the case of two does not give up hope
