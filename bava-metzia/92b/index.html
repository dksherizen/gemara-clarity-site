Topic

Does a פועל eat from his own wages (משלו) or as a gift from Heaven (משל שמים)? Two ברייתות are tested against both sides.

Overview

The גמרא is testing whether a worker who eats from the crop he works on eats 'from his own' (משלו, the food is part of his pay) or 'from Heaven' (משל שמים, a special gift the Torah gives him). First it finishes a ברייתא about tithes. That ברייתא seems to challenge משלו, but רבינא answers that the son's case only 'looks like a sale.' Next, a משנה about נטע רבעי seems to challenge משל שמים, and the גמרא answers that the hiring only 'looks like a mistaken deal.' The סיפא of that משנה, about opened wine barrels, seems to break this answer. The גמרא rescues it with two versions of when wine becomes obligated in מעשר, plus local customs that made the workers' mistake reasonable. Then a משנה about who may give up the right to eat is tested, and it is set against רבי הושעיא's ברייתא. The גמרא explains that the two do not argue: the difference is whether the man feeds his household, and a father may never make his young children suffer. The amud ends as the גמרא begins to challenge its reading of the משנה, and that challenge continues on the next amud.
