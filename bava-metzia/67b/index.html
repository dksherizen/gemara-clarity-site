Topic

The laws of a mortgaged field (מַשְׁכַּנְתָּא): eating its produce with a deduction or through קיצותא, how local custom decides whose property it is, and when the lender must leave.

Overview

This amud opens with the last word of an earlier story: רב אשי treated the orphans as adults. Then רבא בריה דרב יוסף, in the name of רבא, rules that where the borrower may pay back the loan at any time and remove the lender, the lender may eat the field's produce only with a deduction from the loan. A צורבא מדרבנן must use a fixed arrangement called קיצותא instead. רב אחא and רבינא argue whether קיצותא is allowed, and the גמרא gives two versions of what קיצותא means. Even the strictest view allows the סורא mortgage, where the land returns to the owner with nothing owed after a set number of years. Next, local custom decides whether the mortgaged field counts as the lender's property, which affects his creditors, his בכור's double share, and שביעית. The גמרא then discusses harvested dates, a promise by the lender to leave (the הלכה requires a קנין), and how the borrower's words end the lender's right to eat. Finally, some אמוראים refused to eat even with a deduction while רבינא did, and מר זוטרא begins to explain רבינא's view by comparing it to a שדה אחוזה, a comparison that continues on the next amud.
