Topic

Collateral and שמיטה, down payments, and whether backing out of a spoken promise counts as מחוסרי אמנה (breaking faith)

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion of a ברייתא about a loan backed by a משכון (collateral) worth only half the loan. In it, רבן שמעון בן גמליאל and רבי יהודה הנשיא argue about whether שמיטה cancels such a loan. The גמרא first tries to tie their argument to the dispute over whether a ערבון (down payment) acquires כנגד כולו (the whole deal) or כנגדו (only its own value). It then pushes the link away by saying that, for רבי יהודה הנשיא, the collateral may be only a reminder. Next comes the story of רב כהנא, who was paid in advance for flax. רב ruled that he must deliver only what the money covered, because backing out of mere words is not מחוסרי אמנה (breaking faith). The גמרא presents this as a dispute: רב says spoken promises carry no such problem, and רבי יוחנן says they do. Challenges follow from 'הין צדק', from the curse of 'מי שפרע', and from the story of רבי יוחנן בן מתיא and the hired workers. Then רבי יוחנן's own statements about promised gifts and about a לוי's מעשר are examined. The result is that a small gift, which people rely on, may not be taken back. The amud ends with רבא's ruling on sesame sellers who backed out of a deal, and with the start of a story about an especially honest rabbi, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

But the collateral that he holds, why does he need it?

Rather, do we not conclude from it:

that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says?
