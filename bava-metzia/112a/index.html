Topic

The meaning of the פסוק ״וְאֵלָיו הוּא נֹשֵׂא אֶת נַפְשׁוֹ״ (דברים 24:15) about paying a שכיר on time, and the מחלוקת between רב הונא and רב חסדא over whose soul is taken when wages are held back.

Overview

The גמרא explains how the other Sage uses the פסוק ״וְאֵלָיו הוּא נֹשֵׂא אֶת נַפְשׁוֹ״: a worker risks his life for his wages, so you must not hold them back. A second reading says withholding wages is like taking a soul. רב הונא and רב חסדא argue whether this means the robber's soul or the victim's soul, and each explains the other's פסוק from משלי. The גמרא then returns to the משנה. The employer violates the prohibition only if the worker asked for his pay, and a ברייתא learns from ״אִתְּךָ״ that he must also have the money and must not have sent the worker to a storekeeper or money changer. רב ששת and רבה argue whether, after such a transfer, the worker can still go back to the employer to collect. Next, רב ששת rules that an employer who hires a contractor does violate בל תלין. This connects to the question of whether a craftsman owns the improvement he adds to an item. A ברייתא about a garment given to a craftsman is offered as support for רב ששת. רב מרי pushes it aside by reading it as the cleaning of a coarse garment. When that is challenged, the גמרא defends him by reading it as a worker paid for each tread, who is a regular hired worker.
