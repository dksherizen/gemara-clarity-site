Bava Metzia 4a

בבא מציעא ד׳ ע״א

Topic

רבי חייא's two rulings on oaths: the הצד השוה source for witnesses on part of a loan, and the debate with רב ששת over whether one who says הילך must swear.

Overview

This amud picks up in the middle of the גמרא's search for a source for רבי חייא's rule: if two witnesses prove a borrower owes part of a loan, he must swear about the rest. After a comparison to a single witness (עד אחד) fails, רב פפא suggests learning from גלגול שבועה. The גמרא then goes back and forth between a person's own admission (פיו) and עד אחד, with each one covering the other's weakness. In the end it combines them into a הצד השוה: both come through a claim and a denial, and so do witnesses. Two challenges to this, that he is a proven denier and that witnesses are subject to הזמה, are both answered. Next the גמרא says our משנה of two people holding a garment does not really match the witnesses case. Instead it supports a different ruling of רבי חייא: one who admits part of a claim and hands it over (הילך) must still swear. רב ששת disagrees and holds that הילך is exempt, calling the משנה's oath only rabbinic. רבי חייא answers that the חכמים model their oaths on Torah oaths. The amud ends as the גמרא begins quoting a ברייתא against רבי חייא, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תֹּאמַר בְּעֵדִים,

Would you say the same about witnesses,

שֶׁעַל מַה שֶּׁכָּפַר הוּא נִשְׁבָּע.

where he swears about what he denied?

אֶלָּא אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא:

Rather, Rav Pappa said:

אָתֵי מִגִּלְגּוּל שְׁבוּעָה דְּעֵד אֶחָד.

It is derived from the rolling-over of an oath caused by one witness.
