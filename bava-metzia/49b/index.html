Topic

Backing out of sales before משיכה, and how אונאה (overcharging) is measured: one-sixth of the item or of the money.

Overview

The גמרא finishes a story about a man who left his money in someone's house, and רבא rules that saying 'here is the house' does not make a person a watchman at all. It then explains רבי שמעון's view in the משנה. A ברייתא and רבא show when the seller is locked in even without משיכה. רב חסדא then rules that a buyer who paid but did not pull the goods may also back out. A new משנה sets the line for אונאה at one-sixth, with only a short time to complain. It also tells how the merchants of לוד turned down רבי טרפון's one-third rule. רב and שמואל argue whether the sixth is measured against the item or also against the money paid. Proofs from the משנה all end up fitting רב, and a ברייתא that supports שמואל begins, continuing on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֲמַרִי לֵיהּ: לָא.

I said to him: No.

אֲמַר לִי: לֶיהְווֹ הָנָךְ זוּזֵי

He said to me: Let these coins be

בְּפִקָּדוֹן גַּבָּךְ,

as a deposit with you,

דְּהָא חֲשַׁכָה לִי.

for it has grown dark for me.
