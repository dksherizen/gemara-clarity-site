Topic

End of the משנה (stones of a fallen wall; paying a פועל with hay and straw) and the opening of the גמרא: the מחלוקת between רב and שמואל on how big a break in the roof of the בית הבד must be, and why that מחלוקת had to be stated in two places.

Overview

The amud opens at the end of a משנה. If a wall falls into a neighbor's garden, its owner cannot force the stones on the neighbor, and an employer cannot force a פועל to take the hay or straw he worked on as pay. But once the other side accepts, the deal cannot be undone. The גמרא then explains the earlier case of a בית הבד whose roof broke: רב says most of it must break, שמואל says four טפחים is enough, and their מחלוקת had to be taught both here and for a house. רבי יוחנן says the court's standard time to remove a danger is thirty days. The גמרא shows that the garden owner gets the stones only if he cleared them, because a field acquires only when the giver truly means to give. It explains why both cases of the משנה were needed. It then settles a contradiction between the משנה and a ברייתא about paying in straw: after רבא knocks down two answers, רב נחמן distinguishes lifting from merely watching. Finally רבה and רבא give different readings of a משנה about guards of שמיטה grain: is the argument about whether watching acquires ownerless property, about trusting an unpaid guard, or about scaring off violent people?
