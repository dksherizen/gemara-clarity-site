Bava Metzia 109a

בבא מציעא ק״ט ע״א

Learn Bava Metzia 109a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Bava Metzia 109a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

A sharecropper (מְקַבֵּל) who takes a field for a few years versus seven years: planting פשתן, cutting קורות שקמה, and whether he is paid for the שבח (growth) of the sycamore when he leaves.

Overview

The משנה teaches that someone who takes a field for only a few years may not plant flax, which weakens the soil, and may not cut the sycamore branches used for beams. With a seven-year deal he may do both. אביי says the short-term worker is still paid for the sycamore's growth (שבח), and רבא says he is not. The גמרא challenges רבא from ברייתות about a court appraisal. In the end it shows that only a buyer at the יובל keeps the growth, because he was a true owner and a special פסוק gives it to him. Then come real cases: רב פפא is paid for palm trees that sprouted in his hay field only as wood, and רב ביבי gets nothing because he lost nothing. רב יוסף tries to remove his dead planter's five heirs, but the ruling that heirs leave with no payment is rejected. A planter who promised to leave if he caused a loss leads to a three-way argument, and רבא calls the promise an אסמכתא that is not binding. The amud ends with רבא approving רבינא's removal of his planter without warning, and it begins quoting רבא's rule about such workers, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳ הַמְקַבֵּל שָׂדֶה מֵחֲבֵירוֹ

MISHNA: One who receives a field from his fellow to work it

לְשָׁנִים מוּעָטוֹת –

for a few years,

לֹא יִזְרָעֶנָּה פִּשְׁתָּן,

may not plant it with flax,

וְאֵין לוֹ בְּקוֹרוֹת שִׁקְמָה.

and he has no rights to the sycamore beams.
