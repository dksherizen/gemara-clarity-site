Topic

Announcing and returning lost items: how the announcement developed over time, proving ownership with סימנים and witnesses against swindlers, and caring for or selling found animals.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a question about festival announcements: what if someone mixes up the festivals? The answer is that the third festival is still coming. A ברייתא then tells the history of announcing lost items. First it was three festivals in ירושלים plus seven days. After the חורבן it moved to בתי כנסיות and בתי מדרשות. When harsh rulers who took lost items for the king spread, the finder only had to tell his neighbors. A new משנה rules that a claimant needs סימנים, and a known רמאי gets nothing even with סימנים. רב יהודה and רב נחמן argue about whether the finder may name the item in his announcement. A ברייתא and a story about רב פפא's father show the later rule that a claimant must bring witnesses that he is not a רמאי. A further משנה teaches that a found animal that pays for its own food is kept, and one that only eats is sold. The גמרא sets time limits for different animals and settles two ברייתות that seem to contradict each other. The amud ends as the גמרא begins to pin down the dispute between רבי טרפון and רבי עקיבא about the sale money, which continues on the next amud.
