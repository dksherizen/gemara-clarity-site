Topic

מכת מדינה in a farm rental: when a tenant may subtract from his rent, how luck and merit affect the claim, and related rules about counting years

Overview

The משנה taught that a tenant whose rented field was struck by blight may subtract from his rent only if it was a מכת מדינה, a disaster that hit most of the valley. The גמרא first asks 'what if' questions about different crops and different kinds of blight, and these stay unresolved (תיקו). It then rules that a tenant who planted barley instead of the wheat he was told to plant cannot subtract. It also rules that the owner's bad luck does not count as a regional disaster, because bad luck always leaves a little. And if all of the tenant's own fields were struck, the owner can blame the tenant's bad luck. Next, the גמרא raises an objection from a משנה in ערכין about years of blight. רב נחמן בר יצחק answers that a sold field follows a special פסוק about 'years of crops,' and this leads to a back-and-forth about שביעית. Finally, שמואל rules that a tenant who never planted cannot subtract, since the owner's merit might have saved the field. רב ששת challenges this from a shepherd who left his flock, and that discussion continues on the next amud.
