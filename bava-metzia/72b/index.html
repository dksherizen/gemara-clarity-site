Topic

Documents that 'should never have been written' (לא ניתן ליכתב), and fixing prices and borrowing produce by the market rate without רבית

Overview

The amud opens at the end of an argument about a sale document. Someone argues that a document the seller admits to should collect from נכסים משועבדים, just like a loan שטר the borrower admits writing. רבא answers that a document only has that power if it records something that was allowed to be written (ניתן ליכתב). רבינא tests this twice: once from רבי יוחנן's rule on a backdated document (שטר מוקדם), and once from a ברייתא about a robber who sold a stolen field. מרימר and the גמרא answer that the backdated document is still good from its true date. They also answer that the robber will make peace with the owner and make his sale document good. A new משנה then teaches that you may not fix a price now for produce delivered later until the market price is out, with exceptions for goods that are almost ready and for manure. The גמרא limits רבי יוחנן's ban to unsteady small-town markets. A ברייתא and רב נחמן discuss separate prices for new and old grain and for gleaners' wheat. רב הונא first forbade borrowing produce based on the market rate, then allowed it after hearing רבי אלעזר. The amud ends by setting up a new ברייתא about buying a traveler's package at the price of the town he is heading to, and its ruling comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

מוֹדֶה בִּשְׁטָר שֶׁכְּתָבוֹ –

One who admits about a document that he wrote it –

אֵינוֹ צָרִיךְ לְקַיְּימוֹ,

the holder does not need to prove it valid in court,

וְגוֹבֶה מִנְּכָסִים מְשׁוּעְבָּדִים.

and he collects from liened properties that were sold.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ רָבָא: מִי דָּמֵי?

Rava said to him: Are these comparable?
