Topic

Coins in bought produce, the garment as the model for announcing lost items, why the Torah names each item, the פרוטה minimum, and whether סימנים are from the Torah.

Overview

This עמוד opens by finishing a rule from the previous עמוד. Someone who finds coins in produce he bought may keep them only if he bought from a merchant, not from a private owner. רב נחמן questions a ברייתא that says this, and it is kept by reading it as a case where the owner's כנעני slaves did the threshing. A new משנה then teaches that the Torah singled out the שמלה (garment) to show that any lost item with סימנים and with owners who would claim it must be announced. רבא asks why the Torah lists an ox, a donkey, a sheep and a garment. He explains the donkey (signs on its saddle are enough) but admits that the word שה has no explanation. Next, a ברייתא teaches that an item worth less than a שוה פרוטה need not be returned. The תנא קמא and רבי יהודה learn this from different words in the פסוק. אביי says they differ only about the source. רבא finds a practical difference: an item whose value went up, down and up again. Finally the גמרא asks whether returning items based on סימנים is from the Torah or from the Rabbis, and what difference that makes. The answer continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

בְּלוֹקֵחַ מִן הַתַּגָּר,

[This was taught only] in the case of one who buys from a merchant,

אֲבָל בְּלוֹקֵחַ מִבַּעַל הַבַּיִת –

but in the case of one who buys from a private owner,

חַיָּיב לְהַחְזִיר.

he is obligated to return [the coins].

וְכֵן תָּנֵי תַּנָּא

And likewise the tanna taught
