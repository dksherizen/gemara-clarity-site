Bava Metzia 100b

בבא מציעא ק׳ ע״ב

Learn Bava Metzia 100b step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof colour-coded.

Open Bava Metzia 100b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Why the seller swears in a disputed slave or field sale (רבי הושעיא's זוקקין vs. רב ששת's רבי מאיר reading), סומכוס on dividing money in doubt, and the new משנה on olive trees sold for wood.

Overview

The משנה rules that in a disputed sale of a slave or a field, the seller takes an oath, and the גמרא works hard to explain why. A garment explanation runs into the rule that admitting a different item is not a partial admission. רב פפא answers that the garment is made of attached pieces. רבי הושעיא explains the case as a slave claimed with his clothing, or a field with its sheaves, so the oath on the movables binds an oath on the slave or field. רב ששת objects that this binding (זוקקין) is already taught elsewhere. He says instead that the משנה follows רבי מאיר, for whom a slave is like movables, and he defends this with רבן גמליאל, רבא's case of a damaged slave or field, and רבה בר אבוה's reversed version of a משנה. But a משנה about loaded vines shows that רבי מאיר agrees land gets no oath, so the גמרא returns to רבי הושעיא and explains why זוקקין needed to be taught here. Next, a ברייתא about a cow or maidservant that gave birth is tied to סומכוס, who splits money in doubt, and סומכוס is shown to agree that a Torah oath comes before splitting. Finally, a new משנה about olive trees sold for wood rules by how much oil the olives yield, and the גמרא explains that this applies when the seller gave no time for cutting.
