Topic

The ruling on an engaged woman's extra כתובה money, when the closeness of marriage begins (חופה or ביאה), and רבי יהודה's view on a bride's receipt and on giving up part of the כתובה.

Overview

The גמרא concludes that both sides of the argument about רבי אלעזר בן עזריה rely on reading the husband's intent. One side says the extra כתובה money was pledged for the marriage itself, so an engaged woman does not collect it. The other side says it was pledged out of a closeness that already existed, so she does. After a list of conflicting rulings, including רב נחמן's curse on judges who follow רבי אלעזר בן עזריה, the הלכה is fixed like רבי אלעזר בן עזריה. רבין then asks whether the closeness of the חופה alone earns the extra money, or only the closeness of ביאה. A ברייתא about 'the first night' turns out to fit both views. רב אשי asks about a bride who became a נידה under the חופה, and his question ends in תיקו. The גמרא next asks how רבי יהודה can let a bride write a receipt when elsewhere he rejects receipts; רבי ירמיה and אביי each give an answer. Finally, the גמרא asks why a spoken waiver of the כתובה does not work for רבי יהודה. It answers that the חכמים strengthened the rabbinic כתובה, and then challenges that answer from the husband's rabbinic right to פירות.

Opening lines

אֶלָּא: תַּרְוַיְיהוּ אָזְלִי בָּתַר אוּמְדָּנָא.

Rather: both of them follow the assessment of intent.

מַאן דְּאָמַר הֲלָכָה — שַׁפִּיר,

The one who says the halakha follows him — that is fine,

מַאן דְּאָמַר אֵין הֲלָכָה —

The one who says the halakha does not follow him —

הָכָא נָמֵי אוּמְדַּן דַּעְתָּא הוּא.

here too it is an assessment of intent.
