Topic

Captive women and claimed כהנים: when one person is believed about herself or about another

Overview

The משנה teaches that two captive women are not believed when each says she herself is pure, but they are believed when each testifies for the other. A ברייתא lists four things a captive woman might say about herself and her friend. The גמרא finds that some of its clauses need עדים of captivity while the middle clause needs none. אביי accepts that the ברייתא switches cases this way. רב פפא instead says every clause has עדים of captivity plus a single עד who contradicts her. He shows why each case is needed: we do not suspect she hurt herself to hurt her friend, and she is believed about her friend even when she is clearing herself. A new משנה applies the same idea to two men who claim to be כהנים, and רבי יהודה, רבי אלעזר and רבי שמעון בן הסגן argue about whether one witness is enough. The גמרא then asks why the פרק needs all its similar cases, and begins showing that each one teaches something new. That explanation continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳

MISHNA:

שְׁתֵּי נָשִׁים שֶׁנִּשְׁבּוּ,

Two women who were taken captive,

זֹאת אוֹמֶרֶת ״נִשְׁבֵּיתִי וּטְהוֹרָה אֲנִי״,

this one says: "I was taken captive, but I am pure,"

וְזֹאת אוֹמֶרֶת ״נִשְׁבֵּיתִי וּטְהוֹרָה אֲנִי״ —

and that one says: "I was taken captive, but I am pure" —
