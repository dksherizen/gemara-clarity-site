Topic

The husband's rights to produce of his wife's מלוג property: offspring, items that wear out, inherited old slaves and trees, and his spending on her property

Overview

The amud opens with the end of רבי ינאי's ruling: a thief who steals the offspring of a wife's מלוג animal pays the כפל to the wife. The גמרא answers that the husband was given only פירא, not פירא דפירא, so everyone can agree. It then asks why the רבנן give a maidservant's child to the wife but an animal's offspring to the husband. The answer is that a dead animal still leaves its hide. שמואל rules like חנניה, and רב נחמן adds that a divorced wife may buy back her maidservant's children because of שבח בית אביה. רב נחמן also rules that items brought in for their produce, such as a goat, a hen, or a cloak, may be used until they are gone, following the חכמים about mines, and רבא explains how רבי שמעון differs from the תנא קמא. A new משנה deals with old slaves and old trees the wife inherits, and the גמרא reverses רב's limit on that dispute after רב יוסף's challenge. A final משנה covers a husband's spending on his wife's property, and רבי אסי says even one dried fig counts as 'a little'. The amud ends as a new statement begins, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְלַד בֶּהֱמַת מְלוֹג —

[One who steals] the offspring of a usufruct animal

מְשַׁלֵּם תַּשְׁלוּמֵי כֶפֶל לָאִשָּׁה.

pays the double payment to the woman.

כְּמַאן?

In accordance with whom?

לָא כְּרַבָּנַן וְלָא כַּחֲנַנְיָה!

Not like the Rabbis and not like Ḥananya!
