Topic

When a person is liable for both money and מלקות: does he pay or get lashes? (עולא vs. רבי יוחנן)

Overview

The גמרא is settling a clash: our משנה says a man who violates his sister pays a קנס, but the משנה in מכות says he gets מלקות. עולא holds that when both money and lashes apply, a person pays and is not flogged, and he learns this from the word 'תחת' in the rape פסוק and in 'עין תחת עין'. רבי יוחנן answers differently: one משנה is where he was warned (התראה) and gets lashes, the other is where he was not warned and pays. So רבי יוחנן holds that with a warning, lashes win over money, and he learns it from 'כדי רשעתו' placed next to 'ארבעים יכנו'. The גמרא challenges him from חובל בחבירו, since his own ruling shows that a warned injurer who causes real damage pays. The answer, using רבי אילעא's principle, is that the Torah explicitly singled out the injurer for payment, just as it did for עדים זוממין. The גמרא traces רבי אילעא's statement to a מחלוקת of רבי מאיר and חכמים about false witnesses, and shows the extra words 'יד ביד' and 'כן ינתן בו' both point to money. Finally, רבי יוחנן rejects עולא's rule, because it would cancel the lashes for ערות אחותך.

Opening lines

אֶלָּא עוּלָּא ״תַּחַת״ ״תַּחַת״ גָּמַר.

Rather, עולא learns it from 'for' (תחת) and 'for' (תחת).

כְּתִיב הָכָא: ״תַּחַת אֲשֶׁר עִנָּהּ״,

It is written here: 'because he afflicted her,'

וּכְתִיב הָתָם: ״עַיִן תַּחַת עַיִן״.

and it is written there: 'an eye for an eye.'

מָה הָתָם — מָמוֹנָא מְשַׁלֵּם, מִילְקָא לָא לָקֵי;

Just as there, he pays money and is not flogged;
