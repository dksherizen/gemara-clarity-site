Topic

The ברכת אבלים and the ten-man quorum, the story of ריש לקיש's comfort blessings, and the customs of the house of mourning (ten cups, רבן גמליאל's simple burials).

Overview

The גמרא first settles where רבי יוחנן's rule applies: mourners are not counted in a quorum of ten. It moves the rule from the שורה, the line of comforters, to the רחבה, where the ברכת אבלים is said. It then explains that this blessing can be said all seven days when פנים חדשות, new visitors, arrive. To show this, it tells how ריש לקיש came on the second day to comfort רב חייא בר אבא, whose child had died. ריש לקיש's spokesman spoke about the child, and the גמרא explains that his harsh-sounding words were really an honor. The spokesman then said four blessings: praise of הקב"ה, comfort for the mourners, a blessing for the comforters, and a prayer for all ישראל. Along the way, אביי teaches not to speak of future deaths, because one should never 'open his mouth to the שטן.' Finally the גמרא lists the ten cups of wine set up for the house of mourning, and explains the cup once added for רבן גמליאל, who made burials simple and cheap. A new statement of רבי אלעזר begins at the end and continues on the next amud.
