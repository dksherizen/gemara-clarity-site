Topic

צדקה and the swindlers; whether a poor person must sell his belongings; how to set an orphan daughter's dowry

Overview

The amud opens by finishing the story of רבי חנינא and a 'poor' man who turned out to live in luxury. רבי חנינא responds with רבי אלעזר's teaching that we should be grateful to swindlers, since they give us some excuse for not helping every poor person. The גמרא adds that ignoring צדקה is like עבודה זרה, and that anyone who fakes need will end up truly needy. It then asks whether a poor person must sell his belongings before taking צדקה, and gives two different answers, from רב זביד and רבא בריה דרבה and from רב פפא. A new משנה teaches that an orphan girl given too small a dowry may claim the proper amount when she grows up. רבי יהודה and חכמים argue about how to measure that amount. The גמרא shows that שמואל, who estimates by the father, follows רבי יהודה, and that this is the הלכה. It then explains that when the father's intent cannot be assessed, the daughter gets רבי's fixed tenth, and it shows how that tenth is calculated so the son is not left with nothing.

Opening lines

בִּטְלֵי כֶסֶף אוֹ בִּטְלֵי זָהָב?

On silver tablecloths or on gold tablecloths?

אָמַר: הַיְינוּ דְּאָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר:

He said: This is what Rabbi Elazar said:

בּוֹאוּ וְנַחֲזִיק טוֹבָה לָרַמָּאִין,

Come and let us be grateful to the swindlers,

שֶׁאִלְמָלֵא הֵן, הָיִינוּ חוֹטְאִין בְּכׇל יוֹם,

for were it not for them, we would be sinning every day,
