Topic

What an adult may testify about from his childhood (כהונה, rabbinic laws, family lineage), and where such testimony is not accepted.

Overview

The גמרא continues through the משנה's list of things an adult may testify about from what he saw as a child. It answers a challenge about a slave who reads Torah: only a child sent to school like a son is a sign. It limits the immersion testimony to תרומה דרבנן. It explains that receiving תרומה at the threshing floor counts because the משנה follows רבי יהודה, and it ties his dispute with רבי יוסי to local custom. It reworks רבי אלעזר ברבי יוסי's story so that a צדיק never actually caused a slave to become a כהן. Childhood memory is accepted for בית הפרס and תחומין because they are only דרבנן, but never to take property away from its owner. A ברייתא adds more such cases and shows that רבי יוחנן בן ברוקא disputes only whether a convert or freed slave is believed. The amud ends by explaining the קצצה ceremony and closing the chapter האשה שנתארמלה.

אַפּוֹטְרוֹפּוֹס, אוֹ שֶׁהִנִּיחַ תְּפִילִּין בִּפְנֵי רַבּוֹ,

a steward, or who put on tefillin in front of his master,

or who read three verses in the synagogue —
