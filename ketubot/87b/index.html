Topic

The oaths a woman takes to collect her כתובה: Torah or rabbinic, and when they apply

Overview

The amud opens with the end of the משנה. רבי שמעון says the heirs can make the widow swear only when she is claiming her כתובה. The גמרא then asks what kind of oath a פוגמת takes, meaning a woman who admits part of her כתובה was already paid. רמי בר חמא suggests it is the Torah's oath of מודה במקצת. רבא rejects this and says it is a rabbinic oath, meant to make her remember exactly what she received. The גמרא then asks three questions about this oath. Does she swear if the partial payment had witnesses? A proof is brought and pushed off. Does listing tiny payments make her believable? That one ends in תיקו. Does a פוחתת, who says the total is smaller but that she received nothing, swear? A ברייתא says she collects without an oath, as long as she claims a private trust agreement. Finally, רמי בר חמא suggests that the oath against one witness who says the כתובה was paid is a Torah oath. רבא again rejects this and calls it a rabbinic oath, meant to put the husband's mind at ease. The amud ends as רב פפא begins a statement that continues on the next amud.

רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן אוֹמֵר:
