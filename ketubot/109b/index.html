Topic

Losing a claim by making a field a boundary marker, the lost path to a field, and a palm tree left to a daughter

Overview

This amud opens by finishing a point about documents: judges may sign one without reading it, because they only confirm the witnesses' signatures. Next comes the משנה's case of someone who described a field as another person's by using it as a boundary marker (סימן). אביי says he loses his claim only if he wrote this in someone else's document. If he wrote it in his own purchase deed, he can say the seller would not have sold otherwise, and he could not make a secret protest (מודעא), since secrets spread. In a real case, אביי first rules that the man lost his claim. The orphans' steward then wins back the field with two claims from רבי יוחנן: 'I marked only one furrow' and 'I bought it back.' A new משנה follows about a man whose path to his field was lost. אדמון lets him take the shortest route, while חכמים say he must buy a path, and רב and רבא explain when they disagree. The amud ends with two cases of a father who left 'a palm tree' to his daughter. In one, the heirs must give her a tree and divide the estate again. In the other, two half-trees count as 'a palm tree.'
