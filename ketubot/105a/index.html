Topic

The end of the משנה on the wife's oath; the Jerusalem decree-judges; and how judges may and may not take money from the people in a case

Overview

The amud opens with the end of the משנה. חנן says a wife whose husband went overseas swears only at the end, when she collects her כתובה. The בני כהנים גדולים, joined by רבי דוסא בן הרכינס, say she swears at the beginning too, and רבן יוחנן בן זכאי sides with חנן. The גמרא then asks about the Jerusalem judges. Some sources list them differently, and one calls them judges of 'thefts' instead of 'decrees.' The גמרא answers that they made decrees about theft cases, and that ירושלים had hundreds of courts but only a few decree-makers, who were paid from Temple funds. Next comes the judge קרנא, who took a small coin from both sides. Step by step, the גמרא shows this was neither שוחד nor pay for judging. It was pay for obvious lost work time, like רב הונא asking for a worker to replace him. The amud ends with teachings on how bribes blind judges, even great sages, and that explanation continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תִּשָּׁבַע בַּסּוֹף,

She takes an oath at the end,

וְלֹא תִּשָּׁבַע בַּתְּחִלָּה.

and she does not take an oath at the beginning.

נֶחְלְקוּ עָלָיו בְּנֵי כֹּהֲנִים גְּדוֹלִים,

The sons of the High Priests disagreed with him,

וְאָמְרוּ: תִּשָּׁבַע בַּתְּחִלָּה וּבַסּוֹף.

and said: She takes an oath at the beginning and at the end.
