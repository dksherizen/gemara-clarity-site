Topic

Why a thief who slaughters on שבת, for עבודה זרה, or a שור הנסקל may be exempt or liable, including רבי יוחנן הסנדלר's ban on מעשה שבת and the defense of רבי מאיר

Overview

The גמרא explains why a thief who slaughtered a stolen animal on שבת, for עבודה זרה, or as a שור הנסקל could be exempt. The exemption follows רבי שמעון: a שחיטה that does not make the meat permitted is not called שחיטה. That fits עבודה זרה and שור הנסקל, but a משנה says a שחיטה done on שבת is valid. The גמרא answers that this view follows רבי יוחנן הסנדלר, who forbids food made by breaking שבת on purpose to everyone, forever. His source is רבי חייא's teaching on 'כי קודש היא לכם'. 'קודש' forbids eating, 'לכם' still allows benefit, and 'מחלליה מות יומת' limits the rule to someone who did it on purpose. רב אחא and רבינא argue whether this ban is from the Torah or only from the חכמים. According to the rabbinic view, the חכמים exempt the thief only in the other cases. The גמרא then defends רבי מאיר, who makes the thief pay. For עבודה זרה, רבא says the man worships only at the end of the slaughter. For the שור הנסקל, רבה sets the case in a שומר's house and relies on the rules of רבי יעקב and רבי שמעון. Finally, רבה begins a new reading in which the thief slaughtered by himself, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן הִיא, דְּאָמַר:

It is Rabbi Shimon, who said:

שְׁחִיטָה שֶׁאֵינָהּ רְאוּיָה —

a slaughter that is not fit

לֹא שְׁמָהּ שְׁחִיטָה.

is not called a slaughter.

הָתִינַח עֲבוֹדָה זָרָה וְשׁוֹר הַנִּסְקָל,

This works well for idolatry and the ox that is stoned,
