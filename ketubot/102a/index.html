Topic

Does writing "I owe you" in a document, without a formal act of acquisition (קנין), create a real obligation? The גמרא keeps testing the view of ריש לקיש (no obligation) against the view of רבי יוחנן (yes, obligated).

Overview

The גמרא continues the argument between רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש about whether writing "I owe you" in a document creates a real debt without a קנין. First it pushes away a proof for רבי יוחנן. That case was about engagement agreements, which are binding by words alone, as רב גידל taught in the name of רב. Next it challenges ריש לקיש from a father who writes to a כהן, "I owe you five סלעים," for פדיון הבן. The answer is that the Torah already obligates the father, and the note only picks which כהן gets the money. The גמרא then asks why the son is not redeemed, and answers with עולא: by Torah law he is redeemed once the money is paid, but the חכמים decreed otherwise so people won't think one can redeem with documents. Finally רבא links the argument to one between רבי ישמעאל and בן ננס about a ערב who signs after the document was already signed. The גמרא begins to question that link, noting that according to בן ננס everyone agrees. That discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

לָא, בִּשְׁטָרֵי פְסִיקָתָא,

No, it is about documents of stipulation,

וְכִדְרַב גִּידֵּל.

and it follows רב גידל.

דְּאָמַר רַב גִּידֵּל אָמַר רַב:

As רב גידל said that רב said:

כַּמָּה אַתָּה נוֹתֵן לְבִנְךָ?

How much are you giving your son?

The steps (14)

  1. RejectionThe earlier case is about engagement agreements
  2. Statementרב גידל: engagement promises are binding by words alone
  3. ChallengeNew challenge: a written promise to a כהן for פדיון הבן
  4. ResolutionThere the father already owes the money from the Torah
  5. ChallengeThen why did he write a note at all?
  6. ResolutionHe wrote it to choose a particular כהן
  7. ChallengeThen why isn't the son redeemed?
  8. Resolutionעולא: a rabbinic decree about documents
  9. Statementרבא: this is an older argument of תנאים
  10. Statementרבי ישמעאל and בן ננס argue
  11. Questionרבי ישמעאל asks for the reason
  12. Responseבן ננס: the man in the market
  13. StatementSuggesting the two arguments match
  14. RejectionAccording to בן ננס, there is no argument
