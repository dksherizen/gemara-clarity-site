Eruvin 30a

עירובין ל׳ ע״א

Learn Eruvin 30a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Eruvin 30a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Which foods and measures are valid for an עירוב: majority practice versus individual measures, raw foods, the meaning of מזון, vowed-off loaves, and wine for a נזיר.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing אביי's proof: in the laws of טומאה, the measure that suits rich people's clothing does not set the rule for the poor, so one group's custom (like the Persians' roasted meat) does not set the rule for עירוב. The גמרא rejects a suggestion to be strict in עירוב anyway, because רבי שמעון בן אלעזר measures an עירוב leniently for the sick, the old and the glutton. It then contrasts that ruling with his view that עוג מלך הבשן needs a doorway as big as himself, and explains that there the body simply has no other way out. Next come rulings that raw meat and raw eggs are valid for an עירוב, with a dispute over whether one egg or two is needed. The גמרא discusses the משנה about vowing off מזון, and רב הונא saves רב and שמואל by separating 'anything that nourishes' (זן) from real מזון. A long discussion follows about a loaf someone swore not to eat versus a loaf he forbade with 'עלי'. It ends with two traditions of רבי אליעזר's view. Finally, the גמרא shows that our משנה, which allows wine for a נזיר's עירוב, follows בית הלל. בית הלל's argument against בית שמאי continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֲבָל בִּגְדֵי עֲשִׁירִים לַעֲנִיִּים — לָא.

But the clothing of the rich, for the poor — no.

וְכִי תֵּימָא:

And if you say:

הָכָא לְחוּמְרָא וְהָכָא לְחוּמְרָא,

here it is a stringency and there it is a stringency,

וְהָתַנְיָא,

but wasn't it taught in a ברייתא,
