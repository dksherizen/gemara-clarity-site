Topic

An עירוב that is hard to reach: in a pit, on top of a reed, or in a locked cupboard

Overview

The גמרא finishes asking where an עירוב placed in a deep pit is valid. It settles on a pit in a כרמלית, following רבי's rule that a rabbinic prohibition (שבות) does not apply during בין השמשות. A new משנה teaches that an עירוב on top of a detached reed or pole stuck in the ground is valid, even a hundred אמות high. רב אדא בר מתנא asks how the earlier clause can follow רבי while this one seems to follow the רבנן. רב חסדא accepts the split. רבינא says the whole משנה is רבי, and the reed case has its own worry that he might snap the reed off. A story about רב נחמן letting students sit on pressed-down marsh reeds on שבת leads to a distinction between hard reeds, which count as trees, and soft reeds, which count as vegetables. A last משנה rules that an עירוב locked in a cupboard with a lost key is still valid. The גמרא explains this as a cupboard of loosely stacked bricks, following רבי מאיר, and then limits it to יום טוב. That limit is challenged from רבי אליעזר's distinction between a key lost in the city and one lost in the field, and the discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

פְּשִׁיטָא! רְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד עוֹלָה עַד לָרָקִיעַ,

It is obvious! The private domain rises up to the sky,

וְכִי הֵיכִי דְּסָלְקָא לְעֵיל,

and just as it goes up above,

הָכִי נָמֵי דְּנָחֲתָא לְתַחַת.

so too it goes down below.

וְאֶלָּא דְּקָאֵי בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים.

Rather, it is a case where the pit is located in the public domain.
