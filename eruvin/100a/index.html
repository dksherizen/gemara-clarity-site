Topic

Using trees and their roots on שבת, and whether someone up a tree may climb down

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier point: carrying under a tree with low branches is limited to בית סאתים, because the space is a דירה שתשמישה לאויר. The גמרא then turns to the משנה's rule that roots three טפחים above the ground may not be sat on. רבה and רב ששת argue about a root that rises above three טפחים and then comes back down near the ground. רבה permits it because anything that low counts as ground, and רב ששת forbids it because it grows from a forbidden source. The גמרא narrows the dispute to roots running sideways and extends it to trees in a ditch, in a corner, and to אביי's palm tree growing through his roof. An attempted proof from the משנה against רבה is rejected, and a ברייתא teaches that on שבת one may not climb, hang from, or lean on a tree or an animal. Two ברייתות disagree about whether someone up a tree may climb down, and the גמרא gives three ways to settle this. רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע then links that disagreement to the argument of רבי אליעזר and רבי יהושע about mixed blood of קרבנות, which turns on whether acting (קום עשה) or not acting (שב ואל תעשה) is better. The גמרא begins to reject that link, and the discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מִשּׁוּם דְּהָוֵי דִּירָה שֶׁתַּשְׁמִישָׁהּ לַאֲוִיר,

because it is a dwelling whose use is for the open air,

וְכׇל דִּירָה שֶׁתַּשְׁמִישָׁהּ לַאֲוִיר —

and any dwelling whose use is for the open air —

אֵין מְטַלְטְלִין בָּהּ יָתֵר מִבֵּית סָאתַיִם.

one may not carry in it more than an area of two beit se'a.

שׇׁרָשָׁיו גְּבוֹהִין מִן הָאָרֶץ וְכוּ׳.

"Its roots are high above the ground," etc.
