Topic

Moving water and שביתה; why רבי יהושע בן לוי ruled both like רבי יוחנן בן נורי and like the lenient view in עירוב; whether one-against-many matters in rabbinic law

Overview

The גמרא finishes its discussion of rain that falls on יום טוב. It rejects the idea that the water did not yet exist, because then it would be נולד and forbidden. Instead it says that water in clouds, and in the ocean, is always moving, so it never acquires שביתה. A ברייתא supports this: flowing rivers and springs are like the feet of whoever draws from them. Then רבי יהושע בן לוי rules that the הלכה follows רבי יוחנן בן נורי, and he also says that the הלכה follows the lenient view in עירוב. The גמרא explains why both rulings were needed: the general rule limits us to his leniencies, and the specific ruling shows we follow a lone lenient view even against the majority. רבא asks why the number of sages should matter in a rabbinic law. Two challengers prove that it does matter: רבי relied on the lone רבי אלעזר only בשעת הדחק, and רבי יוחנן followed the lone רבי עקיבא in mourning only because of a special rule to be lenient in אבל.
