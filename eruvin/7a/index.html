Topic

Following the stringencies of two opinions, and how to fix an alley that is open at both ends

Overview

The גמרא finishes explaining a ברייתא that says the הלכה follows בית הלל, yet lets a person act like בית שמאי. It then rules out taking the leniencies of both sides, or the stringencies of both sides. The alleyway in נהרדעא, which was treated with the stringencies of both רב and שמואל, still seems to break that rule. רב נחמן בר יצחק answers that everything there followed רב, who held 'הלכה ואין מורין כן'. When another version of רב's words undoes that answer, רב שיזבי limits the rule: we avoid two stringencies only when they contradict each other, like the spine in טומאה and in טריפה. A challenge from רבי עקיבא's two tithes is answered: he was strict both ways because he was in doubt. Then the גמרא returns to the מבוי מפולש. רב defines when the dispute applies (main street or plaza on both sides) and when a lighter fix is enough (a valley on one or both sides). A final ruling says an alley ending in an enclosed yard needs nothing at that end, and אביי identifies that ruling as שמואל's.
