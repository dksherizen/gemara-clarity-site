Topic

Who is exempt from ראייה, and the חכמים who cried at פסוקים about closeness to ה׳ and Divine judgment

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a point about the מפונק, a pampered person who cannot walk barefoot and so is exempt from ראייה, proven from ישעיהו. A ברייתא then says the ערל and the טמא are exempt too. The גמרא finds the טמא's source in the link between 'coming' and 'bringing.' It explains the ערל by saying the ברייתא follows רבי עקיבא, who treats a ערל like a טמא. Another ברייתא repeats the טמא's exemption and adds that a person blind in one eye is exempt, learned from reading the פסוק as both 'will see' and 'will be seen.' From there the גמרא turns to מוסר: רב הונא, רבי אלעזר, רבי אמי, רבי אסי and רב יוסף each cried at certain פסוקים, out of fear of being pushed away, fear of Divine judgment, and the uncertainty of 'perhaps.' רב יוסף's question, whether someone can die before his time, is answered with the story of the מלאך המוות's messenger who took the wrong מרים. That story continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מְפַנְּקִי, דִּכְתִיב:

Delicate people, as it is written:

״כִּי תָבֹאוּ לֵרָאוֹת פָּנָי

"When you come to appear before Me,

מִי בִקֵּשׁ זֹאת מִיֶּדְכֶם

who asked this of your hand,

רְמוֹס חֲצֵרָי״.

to trample My courts?"
