Sanhedrin 74b

סנהדרין ע״ד ע״ב

Open Sanhedrin 74b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

When one must give up his life in public (פרהסיא), why אסתר did not, and whether a בן נח is commanded in קידוש השם

Overview

This amud finishes the rule that during a decree against Judaism, one must give up his life rather than change even a sandal strap. The גמרא then asks how many people make an act public (פרהסיא). רבי יוחנן says ten, and a ברייתא uses a גזירה שוה on the word 'תוך' to show that all ten must be Jews. The גמרא challenges this from אסתר, whose relationship with the king was public, yet she did not give up her life. אביי answers that she was completely passive, like the ground. רבא answers that when a non-Jew forces a sin for his own benefit, it is different. רבא proves this from the coal shovels Jews handed to the Persians, and the גמרא shows that this matches רבא's own ruling about cutting grass on שבת. Finally, the חכמים ask whether a בן נח must give up his life for קידוש השם. אביי's proof from the seven מצוות is rejected, and the school of רב brings the story of נעמן and אלישע, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֲפִילּוּ לְשַׁנּוֹיֵי עַרְקְתָא דִּמְסָאנָא.

Even to change the strap of a sandal.

וְכַמָּה פַּרְהֶסְיָא?

And how many make it public?

אָמַר רַבִּי יַעֲקֹב אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן:

Rabbi Yaakov said that Rabbi Yochanan said:

אֵין פַּרְהֶסְיָא פְּחוּתָה מֵעֲשָׂרָה בְּנֵי אָדָם.

Public is not fewer than ten people.
