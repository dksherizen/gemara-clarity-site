Sanhedrin 23a

סנהדרין כ״ג ע״א

Topic

How judges are chosen for דיני ממונות, and when a litigant may reject the other side's judges or witnesses.

Overview

The משנה teaches that a money case is judged by three judges. רבי מאיר and חכמים argue about who picks the third judge, and whether a litigant can reject the other side's judges or witnesses. The גמרא first tries reading 'one' as a whole court, and deals with a borrower's right to refuse a court. Then it drops that reading: each side picks one judge and a third is added, so that a true judgment comes out. Next it explains the argument over the third judge. Everyone needs the judges to agree, and they argue only about whether the litigants must agree too. Along the way it supports רב יהודה's rule that witnesses sign only when they know who signs with them. Finally, רבי יוחנן limits rejecting judges to lay courts, and the גמרא defends this against several challenges. This leads to ריש לקיש's reading 'his witness' and the question of backing out of accepting one witness like two, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳ דִּינֵי מָמוֹנוֹת בִּשְׁלֹשָׁה.

MISHNA: Monetary cases are judged by three.

זֶה בּוֹרֵר לוֹ אֶחָד,

This litigant chooses one for himself,

וְזֶה בּוֹרֵר לוֹ אֶחָד,

and that litigant chooses one for himself,

וּשְׁנֵיהֶן בּוֹרְרִין לָהֶן עוֹד אֶחָד,

and the two of them choose one more for themselves;
