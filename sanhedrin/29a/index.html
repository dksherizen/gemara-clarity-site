Topic

Biased witnesses and judges, how the court questions עדים and reaches a verdict, and when an admission of debt is binding, including the rule for a מסית.

Overview

This amud finishes the story of מר עוקבא. He refused to judge his former in-laws, not because they were still relatives, but because they would not obey the ruling. The גמרא then explains the משנה's 'friend' and 'enemy' who cannot testify. A groomsman counts as a friend for a limited time. The enemy rule comes from a פסוק, the friend rule from סברא, and the רבנן use the same פסוק for biased judges and for scholars who hate each other. A new משנה describes how עדים are questioned one at a time, how the judges vote and announce the verdict, and that a judge may not reveal he was outvoted. The גמרא discusses what words are used to scare the עדים, and that an admission of debt counts only when it is made deliberately before witnesses. A borrower who says 'I was teasing you' is exempt, though the court will not raise that claim for him. In capital cases the court argues for the defendant, except for a מסית, as learned from a פסוק and from the snake in גן עדן. This leads to the lesson that whoever adds ends up subtracting. אביי then begins to limit the 'teasing' rule, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

קְרוֹבִים וְנִתְרַחֲקוּ הֲווֹ.

They had been relatives and became unrelated.

אֲתוֹ לְקַמֵּיהּ לְדִינָא.

They came before him for a court case.

אֲמַר לְהוּ: פְּסִילְנָא לְכוּ לְדִינָא.

He said to them: I am disqualified from judging you.

אֲמַרוּ לֵיהּ: מַאי דַּעְתָּיךְ?

They said to him: What is your reasoning?
