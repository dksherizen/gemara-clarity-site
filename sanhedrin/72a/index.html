Sanhedrin 72a

Topic

Being judged by one's end: the בן סורר ומורה and the burglar (בא במחתרת), and whether a burglar must pay for or return what he took

Overview

The גמרא opens with a ברייתא from רבי יוסי הגלילי. He explains that a בן סורר ומורה is not punished for one meal; the Torah sees that his path will lead to robbery, so it is better that he die innocent. A new משנה teaches that a burglar who tunnels in (הבא במחתרת) is also judged by his likely end, and that he pays for a barrel he breaks only if killing him would be forbidden. רבא explains that a burglar expects the owner to defend his money and is ready to kill him, so the owner may kill him first. רב rules that such a burglar keeps the vessels he took, because he 'acquired them with his blood.' רבא first limits this to broken items, then admits רב meant even items that still exist, and explains why he himself disagrees: paying for accidents is not ownership, just like a borrower. The גמרא challenges רב from the משנה's wording, which stays a קשיא. It challenges רבא from a ברייתא about stealing a purse on שבת, and answers it. A story shows רבא refusing to take back his own stolen rams out of respect for רב's ruling. Finally, two ברייתות read 'if the sun has risen upon him' in opposite ways for a homeowner who is unsure of the burglar's intent; the answer begins at the end of the amud and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

גְּמָ׳ תַּנְיָא,

GEMARA: It is taught in a ברייתא:

רַבִּי יוֹסֵי הַגְּלִילִי אוֹמֵר:

Rabbi Yosei HaGelili says:

וְכִי מִפְּנֵי שֶׁאָכַל זֶה

Is it really because this one ate

תַּרְטֵימָר בָּשָׂר

a tarteimar of meat
