Topic

Proper conduct of judges and leaders (דברים 1:17), a court summons with three, three experts for fines, and why a מוציא שם רע case needs twenty-three

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a story: רב refuses to judge his host, who had done him favors, and sends רב כהנא, who warns the host that his tie to רב will not help him. The גמרא then explains דברים 1:17 phrase by phrase. A small-money case must not be pushed behind a big one. A judge must not hold back his opinion out of fear. Crooked judges make הקב״ה return the money to its owners. On whether משה was punished for saying 'bring the hard case to me,' רב נחמן בר יצחק points out that the פסוק says 'I will hear it,' and a ברייתא explains that the law of inheritance came through בנות צלפחד because they earned it. Two pairs of פסוקים teach that the people must respect the judge while the judge carries the people like a nurse carries a baby, and that a generation has one leader. Turning back to הלכה, the גמרא shows that 'זימון with three' means a court summons, which must come in the name of all three judges except on the set court day. It proves that דיני קנסות need three expert judges, so one expert is not enough. Finally, עולא and רבא give two explanations of why the חכמים require twenty-three judges for a מוציא שם רע case.

Opening lines

פְּסִילְנָא לָךְ לְדִינָא.

I am disqualified from judging for you.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ רַב לְרַב כָּהֲנָא:

Rav said to Rav Kahana:

פּוֹק דַּיְּינֵיהּ.

Go out and judge it.

חַזְיֵיהּ דַּהֲוָה קָא גָּאֵיס בֵּיהּ.

He saw that he was acting haughtily about it.
