Topic

The number of judges for money cases: the sources for three, whether a court must be odd-numbered, and רבי's five

Overview

The amud opens with the end of an answer: the משנה lists חצי נזק, and then full נזק, because of how it was already listing other payments. The גמרא then asks where we learn that money cases need three judges. רבי יאשיה counts three mentions of the word אלהים in the פסוקים. רבי יונתן does not count the first mention, so he counts two and adds a third because a בית דין cannot be even-numbered. The גמרא shows that both agree first mentions are not normally counted. רבי יאשיה counts this one only because the תורה chose the word אלהים over 'the judge'. The גמרא then explains that רבי יאשיה, like and even beyond רבי יהודה, allows an even-numbered court. He applies the odd-number rule only to capital cases, but still learns majority rule for money cases through a קל וחומר. Finally, a ברייתא brings רבי's view that money cases need five judges. רבי אבהו knocks out the first explanation for this, and the גמרא gives a new source: 2 + 2 from the פסוקים, plus 1 so the court is not even.
