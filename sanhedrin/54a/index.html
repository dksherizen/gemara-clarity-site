Topic

A ברייתא explaining the פסוק about one who lies with his father's wife (ויקרא 20:11): who is liable, which women are included, which death penalty applies, and where the actual prohibition comes from.

Overview

The amud opens with a long ברייתא that supports רבא's explanation of רבי יהודה. Reading the פסוקים about אשת אב, the ברייתא works out who is liable, that the punishment is סקילה, where the warning comes from, and how a mother who is not the father's wife is included. It ends by teaching that a man with his own mother is liable only as 'mother', not also as אשת אב. The גמרא then shows that the רבנן read 'ערות אביך' literally, as a ban on one's father that adds a second count to the general ban on a male. This matches רב יהודה, and רבא explains that the 'two counts' mean two קרבנות for a Jew who sinned by accident. Two views argue whether this fits רבי יהודה, and that argument depends on whether we punish based on a קל וחומר. Next the גמרא lists which words each side uses for the father's wife after the father's death, for her punishment, and for a mother who is not the father's wife. Along the way, אביי shows that רבי יהודה disagrees with the משנה about counting אשת איש. אביי also explains that כלתו and אשת בנו are one prohibition, not two. A new משנה then rules that relations with a male or with an animal are punished by סקילה and explains why the animal is killed too. The גמרא learns the law of a male from ויקרא 20:13 and its סקילה from the words 'דמיהם בם'.
