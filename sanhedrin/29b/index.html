Sanhedrin 29b

סנהדרין כ״ט ע״ב

Open Sanhedrin 29b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

When an admission of debt is binding, and when it may be written into a document

Overview

This amud is about when an admission of a debt really counts. It starts with a man who admitted a debt casually and later said 'it never happened.' One view calls him a כפרן, but רבא's version says people simply forget casual remarks. Next come stories of lenders who hid witnesses to catch an admission. From them we learn that an admission counts unless the borrower openly says 'no' to being witnessed. Then come cases about people who talk about debts so they won't look rich, including a deathbed admission on which רבי ישמעאל ברבי יוסי and רבי חייא ruled differently. The גמרא then asks when witnesses may write an admission into a document: with a קנין, before three people acting as a בית דין, and for land as opposed to movables. It ends with the wording of admission documents. The amud closes as a new case about a document called 'a record of the proceedings' begins; that case continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

״לֹא הָיוּ דְבָרִים מֵעוֹלָם״ –

'These things never happened' —

הוּחְזַק כַּפְרָן.

he is established as a denier.

אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב אַחָא בַּר אַדָּא,

Rav Pappa, son of Rav Aḥa bar Adda, said:

הָכִי אָמְרִינַן מִשְּׁמֵיהּ דְּרָבָא:

This is what we say in the name of Rava:
