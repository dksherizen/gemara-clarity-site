Sanhedrin 32a

סנהדרין ל״ב ע״א

Learn Sanhedrin 32a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Sanhedrin 32a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

The differences between money cases and capital cases in the משנה, and the גמרא's question of whether money cases truly require careful questioning of witnesses.

Overview

The משנה opens by saying that money cases (דיני ממונות) and capital cases (דיני נפשות) share one rule: the witnesses must be carefully questioned (דרישה וחקירה), because the פסוק says 'you shall have one law.' It then lists the differences between the two kinds of cases. These cover the number of judges, how the discussion opens, the majority needed, and reopening a case. They also cover who may argue and switching sides, timing rules (including no capital trials on ערב שבת or ערב יום טוב), which judge speaks first, and who is fit to judge. In each difference, capital cases lean toward saving the accused. The גמרא then asks whether money cases really need careful questioning. It points to a ברייתא that keeps a loan document valid even when its witnesses seem to have been elsewhere on its date. After explaining why it chose that ברייתא over a משנה about postdated documents, the גמרא brings the first of several answers (memory aid חרפ״ש). רבי חנינא says the Torah requires questioning in all cases, but the חכמים dropped it in money cases so lenders will not stop lending. The amud ends as the גמרא begins to challenge this answer, and the challenge continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳ אֶחָד דִּינֵי מָמוֹנוֹת

MISHNA: Both cases of monetary law

וְאֶחָד דִּינֵי נְפָשׁוֹת

and cases of capital law

בִּדְרִישָׁה וּבַחֲקִירָה,

are equal with regard to inquiry and interrogation of witnesses,

שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״מִשְׁפַּט אֶחָד יִהְיֶה לָכֶם״.

as it is stated: “You shall have one manner of law.”

The steps (19)

  1. StatementMoney cases and capital cases both require careful questioning of witnesses
  2. StatementThe differences begin: money cases need three judges, capital cases need twenty-three
  3. StatementHow deliberation may open: either way in money cases, only for acquittal in capital cases
  4. StatementMajority needed: one vote either way in money cases, but two votes to convict in capital cases
  5. StatementReopening a case: either way in money cases, only for acquittal in capital cases
  6. StatementWho may offer an argument
  7. StatementSwitching sides after giving an argument
  8. StatementFinishing the case at night
  9. StatementWaiting a day before a guilty verdict
  10. StatementNo capital trials before שבת or יום טוב
  11. StatementWhich judge speaks first
  12. StatementWho is fit to judge capital cases
  13. ChallengeDo money cases really need careful questioning?
  14. ChallengeSpelling out the contradiction
  15. ChallengeWhy not ask from the משנה about postdated documents?
  16. ResolutionThe ברייתא makes an even stronger point
  17. ChallengeThe difficulty still stands, plus a memory aid
  18. Resolutionרבי חנינא: Torah law requires it, but the חכמים eased it for lenders
