Topic

Humility, reward and punishment, the limits of יום הכפורים, and appeasing someone you have hurt

Overview

This amud opens with how great leaders kept themselves humble: רבא when he went out to judge, and רב זוטרא while he was carried in honor. The גמרא then explains the verse 'שאת פני רשע לא טוב'. It is bad for the wicked, like אחאב, to be spared in this world, and good for the righteous, like משה, to be punished here. The merit of צדיקים protects their descendants, while the guilt of רשעים harms theirs. One who brings the public to merit is protected from sin, but one who leads the public to sin can almost never do תשובה. Returning to the משנה, the גמרא explains why 'אחטא ואשוב' is said twice: a repeated sin comes to feel permitted. It also shows that even רבי agrees יום הכפורים does not atone for someone who sinned while relying on it. Sins against another person need the victim to be appeased, and the amud gives the rules: even for words, up to three times, and at the grave if he has died. Stories about רבי ירמיה, רבי זירא and רב show this in practice, and a new story about רב reading before רבי begins and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

בִּצְבוּ נַפְשֵׁיהּ לִקְטָלָא נָפֵיק,

Of his own will he goes out to death,

וּצְבוּ בֵּיתֵיהּ לֵית הוּא עָבֵיד,

and he does not do the will (needs) of his household,

וְרֵיקָן לְבֵיתֵיהּ אָזֵיל,

and he goes back to his house empty-handed,

וּלְוַאי שֶׁתְּהֵא בִּיאָה כִּיצִיאָה.

and if only the coming in would be like the going out.
