Topic

חצי שיעור: oaths, the word אסור, and the מחלוקת of רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש (Yoma 74a)

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a challenge to ריש לקיש. He rules that an oath not to eat takes effect even on a half-measure, following רבי עקיבא. That suggests he holds חצי שיעור is not forbidden at all, not even מדרבנן. The גמרא considers saying that an oath takes effect whenever something is permitted מן התורה. It then tests this against the dice-player, whom only the רבנן disqualify, yet a שבועת העדות does not apply to him. The answer is that testimony is different, because the verse ״אם לא יגיד״ requires someone able to testify. Next the גמרא asks whether the word אסור can describe acts that carry כרת. It answers in two ways: אסור means חצי שיעור, or it means the four lighter עינויים learned from שבתון. Then, with גופא, the גמרא lays out the מחלוקת itself. רבי יוחנן says חצי שיעור is forbidden מן התורה because it can combine. ריש לקיש says the Torah only forbids an act of 'eating'. רבי יוחנן challenges him from ״כל חלב״, and ריש לקיש answers that the פסוק is only an אסמכתא. A supporting proof from the כוי is then pushed back, and the reason continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאַלִּיבָּא דְּרַבִּי עֲקִיבָא דְּאָמַר:

And it is according to Rabbi Akiva, who said:

אָדָם אוֹסֵר עַצְמוֹ בְּכׇל שֶׁהוּא!

A person can forbid himself from any amount at all!

וְכִי תֵּימָא:

And if you say:

כֵּיוָן דְּאִית לֵיהּ הֶיתֵּר מִן הַתּוֹרָה,

since it has permission from the Torah,
