Topic

A scholar's response to insult, the finger-count of the פייס and the meaning of פקיע, and the ramp murder that preceded the פייס

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a statement: a תלמיד חכם who does not 'avenge and hold a grudge like a snake' is not a real scholar. The גמרא challenges this from 'לא תקום ולא תטור' and goes through several rounds. It concludes that the scholar may keep a personal hurt in his heart, but he must forgive once the offender comes to appease him. Next the גמרא turns to the משנה about the פייס (the lottery of fingers). A healthy כהן puts out one finger. A sick כהן may put out two, but they count as only one. Along the way it explains the word פקיע: a split-ended whip used to punish cheating כהנים, which is why אביי changed his mind about its meaning. Then a ברייתא tells of a כהן murdered during the race up the ramp, followed by רבי צדוק's tearful speech and the father's shocking concern for the knife's טהרה. The גמרא works out that the murder came first but was seen as a freak crime, while the broken leg showed the race itself was dangerous and led to the פייס. Finally, the גמרא asks whether ירושלים can bring an עגלה ערופה at all. It begins quoting a ברייתא of ten special laws of ירושלים, which continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

שֶׁאֵינוֹ נוֹקֵם וְנוֹטֵר כְּנָחָשׁ —

who does not avenge and hold a grudge like a snake —

אֵינוֹ תַּלְמִיד חָכָם.

is not a Torah scholar.

וְהָכְתִיב: ״לֹא תִקּוֹם וְלֹא תִטּוֹר״?

But isn't it written: 'You shall not take revenge and you shall not hold a grudge'?

הָהוּא, בְּמָמוֹן הוּא דִּכְתִיב,

That one is written regarding money matters,
