Topic

Placing the קטורת inside, the מעלה עשן requirement, and leaving a holy place or one's רב without turning one's back

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier point: the כהן גדול piles the קטורת starting from the far side so he will not burn himself. A ברייתא then teaches from 'לִפְנֵי ה׳' that the קטורת is put on the coals inside קודש הקדשים. This rejects the צדוקים, who wanted it lit outside because of the פסוק 'כִּי בֶּעָנָן אֵרָאֶה'. The חכמים explain that this פסוק requires a מעלה עשן, a smoke-raising herb, and that leaving it or any spice out brings מיתה. The גמרא asks why that is not already a death penalty for an empty entry, and רב ששת and רב אשי each describe a case that explains it. The גמרא then asks why a second פסוק, 'וְכִסָּה', is needed, and gives several answers: root and leaf, יום הכפורים and the whole year, מצוה and לעכב, and עונש and אזהרה. A ברייתא of רבי אליעזר explains that the warning came before נדב ואביהוא died, and that they actually died for ruling on a הלכה in front of משה. Finally, the גמרא learns from שלמה at גבעון that one leaves a holy place without turning one's back, and applies this to כהנים, לוים, ישראל and a תלמיד leaving his רב, with stories about רבי אלעזר and רבא.

Opening lines

שֶׁלֹּא תַּתְחִיל מִפָּנֶיךָ,

so that you do not begin in front of you,

שֶׁמָּא תִּכָּוֶה.

lest you be burned.

תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן:

The Rabbis taught:

״וְנָתַן אֶת הַקְּטוֹרֶת עַל הָאֵשׁ לִפְנֵי ה׳״ —

"And he shall put the incense upon the fire before ה׳" —
