Topic

The laws of קריעה (tearing for a death): mending, size, location, adding onto a tear, special cases, and the mourner's meal

Overview

This amud is mostly about קריעה, tearing one's garment for a death. It first explains which sewing counts as real mending (only the fine 'Alexandrian' kind) and which stitched spots on a garment a valid tear may be made over. It then covers whether a torn garment may be turned over and mended, and teaches that a buyer is bound by the same ban on mending as the seller. Next it records how long the first tear and an added tear must be, and עולא rules on that. It explains which part of a shared tear may be mended when a father and a son die one after the other. רבי יהודה בן בתירה requires a separate tear for parents, and שמואל rules like him, because קריעה is separate from אבילות. The amud then teaches how far down a tear may go and where to tear once the garment is full. A disagreement follows over whether a second death needs a new tear within seven days or within thirty days, and רבי זירא challenges both views. After more rules about borrowed cloaks, sick relatives, children and in-laws, the amud turns to the mourner's meal on upright or overturned beds, with a story about רבא. It ends by opening a ברייתא about a traveler, which continues on the next amud.
