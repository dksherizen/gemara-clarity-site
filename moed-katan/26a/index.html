Topic

קרעין שאין מתאחין: which mourning tears are never mended, the sources for each, and how to tear and stitch them

Overview

This amud opens with a ברייתא that lists the tears of mourning that may never be properly sewn back together. They are for a parent, one's רבו who taught him תורה, the נשיא, the אב בית דין, bad news, ברכת השם, a burned ספר תורה, and the ruined ערי יהודה, מקדש and ירושלים. The גמרא then finds a פסוק for each case. אלישע's cry as אליהו went up to heaven teaches the rule for parents and for a רבו, and the extra words 'into two pieces' show the tear stays forever. דוד's mourning for שאול and יהונתן teaches the rule for the נשיא, the אב בית דין and bad news. Bad news here means only a disaster that strikes most of the nation. חזקיהו's officials teach the rule for ברכת השם, and יהויקים burning ירמיהו's scroll teaches the rule for a burned ספר תורה. רב הונא adds that a torn ספר תורה needs two tears, and שמואל limits this to destruction by force. Finally, the גמרא gives the פסוקים to say over the ruined cities, ירושלים and the מקדש, and settles when one tear is enough. It ends with a ברייתא allowing rough stitching but not real mending, and begins a statement of רב חסדא that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְאֵלּוּ קְרָעִין שֶׁאֵין מִתְאַחִין:

And these are the tears that are never mended:

הַקּוֹרֵעַ עַל אָבִיו וְעַל אִמּוֹ,

One who tears for his father or for his mother,

וְעַל רַבּוֹ שֶׁלִּימְּדוֹ תּוֹרָה,

or for his teacher who taught him Torah,

וְעַל נָשִׂיא וְעַל אָב בֵּית דִּין,

or for the Nasi, or for the head of the court,
