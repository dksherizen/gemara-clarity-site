Topic

נידוי: who may impose it, how a sinning Torah scholar is treated, how a ban is released, and how powerful a ban is

Overview

The גמרא proves from a ברייתא that a תלמיד who puts someone in נידוי for insulting him has a valid ban, and רב יוסף adds that a צורבא מרבנן may act on his own when the law is clear to him. Next comes a long story. רב יהודה bans a great scholar with a bad reputation, based on רבי יוחנן's teaching that one learns Torah only from a teacher who is like an angel of ה׳. After רב יהודה dies, the ban is not lifted, because of a קל וחומר from a ban made by רבי's maidservant. The scholar dies, and he is buried with the judges because he kept his sin private, as רבי אילעאי taught. The גמרא then tells the maidservant's story and the story of ריש לקיש, who wrongly banned a man over figs and needed the נשיא to release him. It brings different ways of handling a sinning Torah scholar: the אושא rule, ריש לקיש's view that he is never banned in public, מר זוטרא's practice, and רב פפא's use of lashes instead of a ban. Finally it explains the word שמתא, argues about whether a ban leaves a trace after it is lifted, and shows how powerful a ban is. The amud ends partway through רב יוסף's advice to a scholar who is afraid of a bully, and that advice continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תַּלְמִיד שֶׁנִּידָּה לִכְבוֹדוֹ

A student who ostracized someone for his own honor

— נִדּוּיוֹ נִידּוּי,

his ostracism is a valid ostracism

דְּתַנְיָא:

As it is taught in a ברייתא:

מְנוּדֶּה לָרַב — מְנוּדֶּה לַתַּלְמִיד,

One ostracized by the teacher is ostracized for the student,
