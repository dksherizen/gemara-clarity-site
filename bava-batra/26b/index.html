Topic

Whether tree roots follow the tree or the land, the sixteen-אמה rule, and עולא's source for it.

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a story. רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע defended cutting רב פפא's palm roots by quoting רב יהודה's rule about a public strip. Afterward he realized he could simply have said he cut beyond sixteen אמות. The גמרא then returns to the משנה: someone digging a pit may cut roots, 'and the wood is his.' יעקב הדייבא asks whose wood that is. רב חסדא tries to prove from a משנה in מעילה that roots follow the tree. The proof is challenged from the second half of that משנה. One answer says the משנה is about new growths and proves nothing. רבינא answers instead that it depends on whether the roots are within or beyond sixteen אמות. Finally, עולא rules that a tree within sixteen אמות of a border is a גזלן and cannot be used for ביכורים. The גמרא searches for his source in the משנה of שביעית, and that search continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

עַד דַּאֲמַרִי לֵיהּ

until I told him

הָא דְּאָמַר רַב יְהוּדָה:

that which Rav Yehuda said:

מֶצֶר שֶׁהֶחֱזִיקוּ בּוֹ רַבִּים –

a border strip that the public has acquired a right to use

אָסוּר לְקַלְקְלוֹ.

it is forbidden to ruin it.
