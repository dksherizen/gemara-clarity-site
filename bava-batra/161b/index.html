Topic

Protecting documents from forgery: cut-off signatures in a מקושר, and רבי יוחנן's rules on erasures and the last line of a שטר

Overview

Earlier on the page, the גמרא worried about a forger who cuts off the last line of a מקושר (a tied document), taking the first part of a witness's signature with it. If the witness signed 'ראובן בן יעקב עד', what is left reads 'יעקב עד' and looks like a full signature. The amud opens with the end of the answer: the court can tell this is not the real signature of anyone named יעקב. The גמרא then asks whether the witness might have signed with his father's name, or used it as a personal mark, the way some חכמים signed with drawings or single letters. Both ideas are rejected, because people do not treat their father's name that way. מר זוטרא then gives a simpler solution: in a מקושר all the signatures must end on the same line, so cutting a line would damage every signature and expose the forgery. Next, רבי יוחנן gives two rules for scribes. Every erasure in a שטר must be confirmed at the end with 'וְדֵין קִיּוּמֵיהוֹן', and the last line must repeat some of the document's content. The amud ends with the גמרא asking the reason for this second rule, and the answer comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

לָאו דְּיַעֲקֹב הוּא.

It is not Yaakov's.

וְדִלְמָא בִּשְׁמֵיהּ דַּאֲבוּהּ חֲתַם!

But perhaps he signed with his father's name!

לָא שָׁבֵיק אִינִישׁ שְׁמֵיהּ דִּידֵיהּ,

A person does not leave out his own name,

וְחָתֵים בִּשְׁמֵיהּ דַּאֲבוּהּ.

and sign with his father's name.
