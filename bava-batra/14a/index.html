Topic

Scroll layout and margins, the proper size of a ספר תורה, and where the ספר תורה was kept in or beside the ארון

Overview

The גמרא first explains the line 'if he wants to cut, he may cut': the books are laid out so that the scroll can be cut neatly between them. It then sets this ברייתא against another one that seems to give the same blank margin at both ends of a scroll. רב נחמן בר יצחק says each end gets the amount it needs. רב אשי says the second ברייתא is about a ספר תורה, which has a pole at each end. Next, a ברייתא teaches that a ספר תורה's height should equal its rolled-up circumference, and רבי gives six טפחים for a scroll on גויל. Stories follow about how rarely scribes achieved this, and about two reports of 'four hundred' that are read in a smaller way. Then the גמרא brings a ברייתא about the measurements of משה's ארון. רבי מאיר places the ספר תורה inside it, and רבי יהודה places the ספר תורה on the פלשתים' chest beside it. The ברייתא's explanation of 'אין בארון רק' for רבי יהודה continues on the next עמוד.
