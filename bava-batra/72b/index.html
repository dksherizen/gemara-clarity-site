Topic

Whether רבי שמעון's view of a stingy consecrator fits a ברייתא about redeeming trees and land, and רב הונא's rulings on items with two legal statuses.

Overview

Just before this, the גמרא said a ברייתא about consecrated trees follows רבי שמעון, who holds that one who consecrates does so stingily (בעין רעה). The amud first answers a problem: רבי שמעון's reason 'since they draw from a consecrated field' sounds generous. The answer is that he was only arguing on the רבנן's terms. Next the גמרא challenges the end of that ברייתא. It brings a ברייתא about a son who bought a field from his father, which seems to show that רבי שמעון goes by the time of redemption. If so, the trees should be redeemed together with their land. רב נחמן בר יצחק answers that in general רבי יהודה and רבי שמעון do not go by the time of פדיון. Their ruling comes from the wording of the פסוק. Then רב הונא teaches that a grafted carob and a sycamore trunk have the status of both a tree and land. He also teaches that a sheaf of two סאה counts as both a sheaf and a heap for the laws of שכחה. Finally, ריש לקיש says that whether these two trees become הקדש with a field depends on the argument between רבי מנחם בר יוסי and the רבנן.

Opening lines

וְאֶלָּא קַשְׁיָא

But it is difficult:

״הוֹאִיל וְיוֹנְקִין מִשְּׂדֵה הֶקְדֵּשׁ״!

"since they draw nourishment from a consecrated field"!

אֶלָּא רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן –

was speaking to them according to the words of the Rabbis:
