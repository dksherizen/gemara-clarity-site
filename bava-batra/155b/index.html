Topic

The young heir who may not yet sell his father's land: when he counts as an adult, what proves he understands, and whether his testimony and gifts are valid

Overview

The גמרא continues asking whether a young heir may sell his late father's land from age 18 or only from age 20. It first defends the age 20 view: a משנה in נדה about a סריס applies only when he shows סריס signs. Someone without those signs stays a minor until 'most of his years,' as רבי חייא teaches. Next the גמרא asks whether the year of coming of age counts as before or after that age, and gets two reports of רב נחמן's view. It explains that רבא's version was only inferred from a case where the seller was plainly foolish. רבא then rules that a fourteen-year-old girl who understands business can buy and sell. A story about a young seller, date pits and a clever remark ends with רבא upholding the sale, because he understands when things are explained to him. Finally, רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע and מר זוטרא discuss whether such a young heir can testify, and אמימר rules that his gift is valid. רב אשי challenges אמימר, and אמימר's answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הַיְינוּ דְּקָאָתוּ וְאָמְרוּ לֵיהּ:

this is why they came and said to him:

מַהוּ לְבוֹדְקוֹ.

What is the law — may we examine him?

אֶלָּא אִי אָמְרַתְּ מִבֶּן עֶשְׂרִים,

But if you say from age twenty,

כִּי בָּדְקוּ לֵיהּ מַאי הָוֵי?

when they examine him, what would it accomplish?
