Topic

How far one hoe strike acquires an unmarked field, and שמואל's ruling that land bought from a גוי is ownerless (like a מדבר) until the Jewish buyer gets the deed, challenged from דינא דמלכותא דינא

Overview

The גמרא first finishes an earlier topic: if someone acquires an unmarked ownerless field with one hoe strike, רב פפא says he gets about one furrow's length. Then רב יהודה quotes שמואל: when a גוי sells land to a Jew for money, the land is like an ownerless desert until the Jew receives the deed, so anyone who takes possession first acquires it. The reason is that the גוי lets go once he is paid, but the Jew owns it only when the שטר reaches him. אביי challenges this from שמואל's own rule, דינא דמלכותא דינא, since the king's law says land is acquired only with a document. רב יוסף admits he cannot answer, but cites a case in דורא דרעוותא where רב יהודה gave the land to the second Jew who plowed it. אביי rejects the proof: in that village the owners did not pay the land tax, and the king had said whoever pays the tax gets the land. The גמרא then tells how רב הונא bought land from a גוי, another Jew plowed it, and רב נחמן gave it to the plower. רב הונא asks רב נחמן whether he relied on שמואל's "desert" rule, and their exchange continues on the next amud.
