Topic

Space rights in shared courtyards and alleyways: the four cubits in front of entrances, and neighbors' power to block changes to doors and alleyway use

Overview

This amud discusses the space that houses around a shared courtyard or alleyway are entitled to. It opens with the ארבע אמות (four cubits) given in front of an entrance, and the reason for them: room to unload an animal (פירוק משאו). Using that test, רב הונא rules that an אכסדרה gets no four cubits, and the גמרא settles a ברייתא against him by saying it means a Roman portico. A chicken coop and a half-roofed house also get none, since there is no unloading or one can unload inside. רב הונא then asks רבי אמי two questions. Can residents stop a man from moving his door onto their alleyway? Yes. Is the burden of housing soldiers split by people or by doors? By people. רב הונא rules that neighbors can stop someone from building in front of his own door. A ברייתא seems to contradict him, and the גמרא answers that this is a dispute between רבי and רבי שמעון בן אלעזר. Finally, רבא rules that a sealed door may be reopened only if its doorposts were never broken, and אביי begins quoting a ברייתא that supports him, which continues on the next amud.
