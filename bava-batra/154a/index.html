Topic

What kind of proof the recipients of a gift must bring when the giver claims he was a שכיב מרע: witnesses about his health, or only קיום השטר

Overview

The משנה taught a dispute about a gift given in a שטר. The giver now says he was a שכיב מרע (deathly ill) when he gave it, while the recipients say he was healthy. רבי מאיר puts the burden of proof on the giver, and the רבנן put it on the recipients. The גמרא asks what kind of proof the recipients need. רב הונא says it means witnesses that the giver was healthy, which fits the earlier argument between רבי יעקב and רבי נתן. רב חסדא and רבה בר רב הונא say confirming the signatures (קיום השטר) is enough. In their view, the משנה argues about whether a שטר its writer admits writing still needs confirmation. The גמרא asks why this argument is needed when a similar one about witnesses already exists, and answers that both cases are needed. רבה agrees with the view requiring witnesses: since the deed's wording proves nothing either way, the property stays with its owner. רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש have the same argument, and רבי יוחנן begins a challenge from a story in בני ברק that continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

רְאָיָה – בְּמַאי?

The proof: with what is it brought?

רַב הוּנָא אָמַר:

Rav Huna said:

רְאָיָה בְּעֵדִים.

The proof is with witnesses.

רַב חִסְדָּא וְרַבָּה בַּר רַב הוּנָא אָמְרִי:

Rav Chisda and Rabba bar Rav Huna say:
