Topic

Dividing an estate between adult and minor heirs, and whether a husband counts as a buyer or an heir of his wife's property

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion about produce still attached to the ground (מחוברין). עולא explains that a father wants his son to get it together with the land, but an outside buyer must pay for it. A new משנה then teaches that adult and minor heirs, whether sons or daughters, do not support themselves from each other's share; they divide equally. Wedding costs paid by the father during his lifetime stay with whoever received them. Daughters are fed from an estate that sons inherit, but not from one that other daughters inherit. In the גמרא, רבא rules that the eldest brother may keep clothing he took from the shared estate, and the משנה's ban is limited to an idle brother (שרכא). רב יהודה explains that minors may match only weddings paid from the estate after the father's death. Finally, אבוה בר גניבא asks whether a husband is a לוקח or a יורש of his wife's property. Several proofs are brought and pushed back, and אביי begins quoting a משנה about יובל that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְהָא תַּנְיָא:

But isn't it taught in a ברייתא:

שָׁמִין אֶת הַמְחוּבָּרִין לַלּוֹקֵחַ!

The court appraises the attached produce for the buyer!

אָמַר עוּלָּא: לָא קַשְׁיָא;

עולא said: It is not difficult;

כָּאן בִּבְנוֹ, כָּאן בְּאַחֵר;

here it is about his son, there it is about another person;
