Bava Basra 37b

בבא בתרא ל״ז ע״ב

Topic

Who owns the ground under sold trees: a seller who keeps the trees or the land, the dispute of רבי עקיבא and רבנן, trees planted too close together, and the buyer of a date palm.

Overview

This amud first finishes explaining why someone who sells land but keeps his trees surely kept the ground they stand on: otherwise the buyer could make him uproot them. It then turns to the reverse case, where someone sells trees and keeps the land. That case depends on the dispute between רבי עקיבא, who says a seller sells generously, and רבנן, who say he sells sparingly. The גמרא shows that each side still fits the earlier rulings of רב זביד and רב פפא, because those rulings were about two separate buyers. Next, the נהרדעי say that eating the fruit of crowded trees gives no חזקה. רבא challenges this from an alfalfa field and moves the rule to a sale: a buyer of crowded trees gets no land. רבי זירא ties this to a dispute of תנאים about a crowded vineyard. The amud closes with a new ruling of the נהרדעי: a buyer of a date palm owns from its base down to the depths.

Opening lines

דְּקָמַכְחֲשִׁי בְּאַרְעָא –

since they drain the land,

שַׁיּוֹרֵי שַׁיַּיר.

he certainly kept [the ground] for himself.

דְּאִי לָא שַׁיַּיר,

For if he had not kept it,

לֵימָא לֵיהּ:

let [the buyer] say to him:
