Bava Basra 175b

בבא בתרא קע״ה ע״ב

Topic

A late ערב in the משנה, and the גמרא's question whether a loan's lien on property (שעבוד) comes from the Torah, as argued by עולא and רבה and by רב and שמואל against רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש.

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a משנה about collecting debts. A note in the borrower's own handwriting, or a ערב added after the שטר was signed, lets the lender collect only from נכסים בני חורין. בן ננס argues that such a late ערב owes nothing at all, because the lender never lent the money trusting him. רבי ישמעאל then praises money law and tells students to learn it from בן ננס. The גמרא asks whether a loan's lien on the borrower's property (שעבוד) comes from the Torah. עולא says it does, and the חכמים removed it from oral loans to protect buyers. רבה says it does not, and the חכמים added it to written loans so people would keep lending. A ruling of רבה's about a בכור seems to contradict him, and the גמרא answers that he was only explaining בני מערבא. רב and שמואל say an oral loan cannot be collected from heirs or buyers, while רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש say it can. A ברייתא about a pit digger's heirs is used to challenge רב and שמואל, and the גמרא defends them: the digger had already been judged in court before he died.

Opening lines

הוֹצִיא עָלָיו כְּתַב יָדוֹ

עָרֵב הַיּוֹצֵא
