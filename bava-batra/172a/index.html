Topic

Accuracy and safeguards in שטרות: true dates and places, not rewriting loan notes, and the new משנה on shared inheritance and two people with the same name

Overview

The amud opens at the end of a discussion about a safeguard against a lender collecting the same debt twice. The answer is that the רבנן set up the safeguard, and anyone who skips it has only hurt himself. Next come rules for scribes: a שטר must give a true date and the true place where it was written, so it never looks like a lie. רבא then rules that בית דין will not split one hundred-זוז שטר into two fifties, or merge two fifties into one hundred, because each setup already balances benefits for the מלוה and the לוה. רב אשי adds that בית דין will not shrink a hundred-זוז שטר into a fifty, because of a possible trick with a receipt for a debt that was already paid. A new משנה follows. It covers two brothers, one rich and one poor, who inherit a bathhouse and an olive press. It also covers two men in one city both named יוסף בן שמעון, and how they should write their names so their שטרות are not confused. The גמרא then begins with a story about a שטר brought to רב הונא's בית דין that does not name the lender.

Opening lines

וְהָא הָאִידָּנָא –

But nowadays,

דְּלָא קָעָבְדִינַן הָכִי!

we do not do this!

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: רַבָּנַן תַּקּוֹנֵי תַּקִּינוּ;

He said to him: The Sages instituted an enactment;

מַאן דְּעָבֵיד – עָבֵיד,

one who does it, does it,
