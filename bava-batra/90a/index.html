Topic

Which measuring vessels a merchant may make so that people do not confuse close sizes, and שמואל's one-sixth limit on changing measures, coins and profit.

Overview

This amud finishes a ברייתא that lists the measuring vessels a merchant may keep. It ends the dry measures with the tiny עוכלא, then lists the liquid measures from a הין down to a קורטוב. The גמרא asks why a two-קב vessel is left out and works out the rule that people confuse measures a quarter apart but not a third apart. It then explains two exceptions: people know small measures well, and the third-הין and quarter-הין were allowed because they were used in the מקדש, where the כהנים are careful. Next, שמואל teaches that measures, coin values and a seller's profit may not be raised by more than one-sixth. The גמרא tries three logical reasons for the limit on measures: rising prices, cancelled sales, and protecting the merchant. Each one is rejected. Finally, רב חסדא says שמואל learned the rule from a פסוק in יחזקאל, and the explanation of that פסוק continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְעוּכְלָא.

And an ukla.

וְכַמָּה הִיא עוּכְלָא?

And how much is an ukla?

אֶחָד מֵחֲמִשָּׁה בִּרְבִיעַ.

One-fifth of a quarter-kav.

וּבְמִדַּת הַלַּח הוּא עוֹשֶׂה הִין,

And in liquid measure, he makes a hin,
