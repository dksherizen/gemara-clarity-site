Topic

Reading unclear money amounts in a שטר: the holder gets the lower amount, and 'דינרי' (gold) differs from 'דינרין' (silver) (בבא בתרא קסו.)

Overview

The גמרא is working through a ברייתא about a שטר that names a metal (gold or silver) and 'dinars' without saying exactly how much. First it asks whether a gold amount could mean tiny coins, and answers that people don't make tiny gold coins. Then it asks why 'זהב בדינרין' isn't read as the bigger amount. אביי answers that when a שטר is unclear, the holder gets the lower amount. That causes a problem in the רישא, where the ברייתא gives the bigger amount. רב אשי solves it: the רישא is where the שטר wrote 'דינרי', meaning gold dinars, and the סיפא is where it wrote 'דינרין', meaning silver dinars. The גמרא proves this from a story: bird pairs in ירושלים cost gold דינרי, and רבן שמעון בן גמליאל swore to bring the price down to plain דינרין, meaning cheap silver coins.

Opening lines

וְאֵימָא פְּרִיטֵי!

But say it means small coins!

פְּרִיטֵי דְּדַהֲבָא לָא עָבְדִי אִינָשֵׁי.

Small coins of gold, people do not make.

״זָהָב בְּדִינָרִין״ –

If it says: 'Gold in dinars'

אֵין פָּחוֹת מִבִּשְׁנֵי דִינָרִין כֶּסֶף, זָהָב.

no less than two silver dinars' worth of gold.
