Topic

How the wording of a sale decides what is sold: outer boundaries (מצרים) that are only a broad description, the clause 'I held back nothing', and what words like ארעא, ארעתא and זיהרא include.

Overview

The גמרא explains that the משנה's rule (a room behind the house is not sold with it) holds even when the seller listed boundaries outside that room. It supports this with רב נחמן in the name of רבה בר אבוה: a seller who sells one residence in a big בירה and lists the building's outer boundaries has only described the location broadly. The גמרא asks what case this covers and answers with a mixed place, where people use the words in different ways. Since the seller did not write 'I have not held back anything', he clearly kept something back. רב נחמן gives the same ruling for a field inside a large בקעה, with the same question and answer. The גמרא then explains why both rulings were needed, since each case has a feature the other lacks. It shows that אביי's advice to always write 'I have not held back anything' follows רב נחמן. Finally, through רב אשי's case of two plots called 'בי חייא' and a list of sale wordings, the גמרא shows how much each wording covers: 'land' is one plot, 'lands' is two, 'all lands' is every field but not orchards, and 'זיהרא' takes in orchards too but not houses or slaves.
