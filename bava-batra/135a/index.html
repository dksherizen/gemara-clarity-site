Topic

When must we worry that a widow still needs ייבום, and what does the case of 'this is my brother' teach about answering 'I do not know'?

Overview

The amud opens with two stories about dying men whose words might free their wives from ייבום. In the first, a dying man says his wife is 'fit for a כהן גדול.' רבא wants to believe him, based on רבי יוחנן's rule that a husband who says 'I divorced my wife' is believed. אביי points to a second version of רבי יוחנן that says the opposite, and argues that a reconciling answer is not enough to rely on in practice, so רבא tells רב נתן בר אמי to be concerned. In the second story, a man with a חזקה of having no brothers also says so himself before he dies. רב יוסף sees nothing to worry about, but אביי raises a rumor of witnesses overseas, and רבא again rules to be concerned. The גמרא then returns to the משנה of 'this is my brother' and concludes that the other brothers are saying 'we do not know.' From this רבא learns that someone who answers a money claim with 'I do not know' is פטור. אביי begins to respond, and his words continue on the next amud.

Opening lines

הָהוּא דַּהֲוָה קָא שָׁכֵיב,

There was a certain man who was dying,

אֲמַרוּ לֵיהּ: אִתְּתֵיהּ לְמַאן?

they said to him: His wife — to whom may she marry?

אֲמַר לְהוּ: חַזְיָא לְכָהֲנָא רַבָּה.

He said to them: She is fit for a High Priest.

אָמַר רָבָא: מַאי נֵיחוּשׁ לַהּ?

Rava said: What should we be worried about for her?
