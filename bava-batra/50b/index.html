Topic

The end of the slave ברייתא on יום או יומים and קנין פירות, then how רב's demand that a married woman protest fits with the משנה that a husband has no חזקה on his wife's property

Overview

The amud opens at the end of a ברייתא about a slave who was sold while the seller kept the right to use him for thirty days. Which master gets the exemption of יום או יומים? רבי יוסי says both masters get it. He is unsure whether קנין פירות counts like קנין הגוף, and a ספק נפשות is judged leniently. רבי אלעזר says neither master gets it, and רבא explains from the words 'כי כספו הוא' that the master must fully own the slave. The גמרא then turns to the משנה that a husband cannot gain a חזקה on his wife's property. It challenges this with רב's statement that a married woman must protest, which seems to mean protesting against her husband. רבא answers that רב means a husband who dug pits, ditches and caves in her land. He defends this against the rule 'אין חזקה לנזקין' in two ways. רב יוסף instead says she protests against an outsider who used the field for three years after the husband died and is believed through a מיגו. The amud ends as the גמרא goes back to study רב's statement that no one can gain a חזקה on a married woman's property, which continues on the next amud.
