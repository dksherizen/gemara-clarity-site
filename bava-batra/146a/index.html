Topic

The hard life of the poor, then the משנה and גמרא on engagement gifts (סבלונות): when a groom can take them back if the marriage does not happen

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an אגדה about the פסוק 'כל ימי עני רעים' (all the days of the poor are bad). The גמרא asks how that can be true when the poor also have שבתות and ימים טובים. It answers with שמואל's rule that a change in routine makes a person sick, and adds בן סירא's line that even the poor man's nights are bad. Next comes a new משנה about סבלונות, the gifts a groom sends to his bride's family. If the marriage falls through, he cannot take them back once he has eaten a סעודת חתן there. Big gifts meant for his own house do come back, and small gifts for her use at her father's house do not. רבא says the משנה's 'דינר' is exact, and the story of a groom who drank at the door and died proves that drinking counts like eating. The גמרא then raises questions it cannot settle: whether the groom takes back only part of the gifts, who owns their rise in value, and what happens to gifts meant to wear out that did not wear out. The amud ends with two stories from רב, one about new produce sent on עצרת and one about a man testing his bride's sense of smell. That second story continues on the next amud.
