Topic

The guarantor (עָרֵב): when a lender may collect from him, why he is obligated at all, and how he differs from a קבלן.

Overview

The משנה finishes its rules about a guarantor (עָרֵב). If the lender set a condition to collect 'from whomever I wish,' he may collect from the עָרֵב. רבן שמעון בן גמליאל says that if the borrower has property, he may not. רבן שמעון בן גמליאל also rules that a husband divorcing a wife whose כתובה has a guarantor must vow off benefit from her, so the couple cannot plot against the guarantor. The גמרא first explains the משנה through רבה and רב יוסף: the guarantor only answers for producing the borrower. רב נחמן rejects this as unreasonable, 'like a Persian court,' and explains that the lender simply may not go to the עָרֵב first. A ברייתא supports him. The גמרא then looks for a פסוק showing that a עָרֵב is obligated. It rejects the story of יהודה and בנימין as קבלנות and accepts פסוקים from משלי. אמימר ties the guarantor's obligation to the dispute over אסמכתא. רב אשי rejects this and explains that the עָרֵב truly commits because he enjoys being trusted. Finally, רבי יוחנן limits the 'whomever I wish' condition to a borrower with no property. The גמרא explains that the משנה is missing words and contrasts a regular עָרֵב with a קבלן.

Opening lines

וְאִם אָמַר לוֹ:

But if he said to him:

״עַל מְנָת שֶׁאֶפָּרַע מִמִּי שֶׁאֶרְצֶה״ –

"On condition that I will collect from whomever I wish"
