Topic

Neighbors' rights in shared and public space: sealing windows, openings and privacy in a partners' courtyard, and digging under or building over רשות הרבים.

Overview

This amud begins by finishing a rule about חזקה: if someone seals a neighbor's window in front of him and the neighbor stays silent, the right to keep it sealed is set right away. The גמרא then explains that a newly bought house may not open into a partners' courtyard because it adds traffic. רב הונא explains that the end of that משנה only allows dividing space the house already has. A new משנה forbids opening a door or window directly facing a neighbor's in a shared courtyard, or enlarging or adding openings, but allows all of this toward רשות הרבים. רבי יוחנן finds the source in בלעם praising ישראל because their tent doors did not face each other. רבא rejects רמי בר חמא's idea that the rule is about courtyard space and shows it is about privacy. Toward the public street the rule does not apply, because the neighbor already has to hide from passersby. Another משנה forbids digging under רשות הרבים or building projections out over it. In a story, רבי אמי orders a man to cut off his projection and explains that an alleyway's residents can give up their claim, but no one can give up the public's claim. A similar story about רבי ינאי and a leaning tree begins and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְלִסְתּוֹם – לְאַלְתַּר הָוֵי חֲזָקָה,

And to seal – it is a חזקה immediately,

שֶׁאֵין אָדָם עָשׂוּי

for a person is not accustomed

שֶׁסּוֹתְמִים אוֹרוֹ בְּפָנָיו וְשׁוֹתֵק.

to have his light sealed in front of him and stay silent.

לָקַח בַּיִת בְּחָצֵר אַחֶרֶת –

If he bought a house in another courtyard –
