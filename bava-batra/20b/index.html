Topic

Windows and מחיצות, then neighbors' damages: mills, ovens, storerooms and noise in a courtyard

Overview

The גמרא finishes a ברייתא about items placed in a window. Such an item makes the opening smaller only if the owner will leave it there, so a shard, a כלי חרס and a ספר תורה each count only when they are useless or set aside for good. Because salt is mentioned, the גמרא brings רב and שמואל on building a מחיצה from salt. It settles their dispute two ways: by the type of salt, or by whether a beam presses the salt down. Next it explains the משנה's distances: a mill is kept from the wall because of its noise, not its shaking, and אביי learns the shape of an oven from its measurements. A new משנה sets rules for ovens near ceilings and floors. The תנא קמא and רבי שמעון disagree on whether keeping the distances frees you from paying for damage. It also forbids putting a bakery, dye shop or cattle barn under a neighbor's storeroom, with an exception for wine. The גמרא discusses all of these, leaving several questions as תיקו. Finally, a משנה about noise in a shared courtyard begins, and אביי and רבא start explaining why a store may be blocked but noisy crafts may not. רבא's answer continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

דְּלֵית בַּהּ שִׁיעוּרָא;

Where it does not have the minimum measure;

כְּדִתְנַן: חֶרֶס –

As we learned in a Mishnah: a shard –

כְּדֵי לִיתֵּן בֵּין פַּצִּים לַחֲבֵירוֹ.

enough to place between one board and another.

כְּלִי חֶרֶס – חֲזֵי לֵיהּ!

An earthenware vessel – it is fit for him!
