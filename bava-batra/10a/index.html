Topic

The power and rewards of צדקה, and the danger of ignoring the poor

Overview

The גמרא finishes showing, from a פסוק in משלי, that someone who regularly gives צדקה merits children who are wise, wealthy, and masters of אגדה. A ברייתא then brings רבי מאיר's answer to a classic challenge: if God loves the poor, why doesn't He feed them? He lets us feed them so our צדקה saves us from גיהנום. טורנוסרופוס and רבי עקיבא debate this with competing parables about a king's slave and a king's son, and רבי עקיבא closes with a פסוק showing that charity is God's will even in hard times. רבי יהודה ברבי שלום teaches that a person's losses are fixed on ראש השנה, and the story of רבן יוחנן בן זכאי's nephews shows this in action. The story of רב פפא's near-fall leads to a ברייתא that compares ignoring the poor to עבודה זרה. A string of teachings then praises צדקה: it brings peace with ה׳, brings the גאולה closer, saves from death, earns a view of the שכינה, and counts as a loan to ה׳. The amud ends with רבי יוחנן explaining why two פסוקים say צדקה saves from death. One is about גיהנום and one is about an unusual death. The question of which צדקה saves from an unusual death is answered on the next amud.

Opening lines

״יִמְצָא חַיִּים״.

"finds life."

בַּעֲלֵי עוֹשֶׁר –

Masters of wealth —

דִּכְתִיב: ״צְדָקָה״.

as it is written: "charity."

בַּעֲלֵי אַגָּדָה – דִּכְתִיב: ״וְכָבוֹד״ –

Masters of aggadah — as it is written: "and honor."
