Topic

Reserving land in a gift, the meaning of 'כל שהוא', how wording defines movables, and whether a slave counts as land or movable property

Overview

The amud opens in the middle of a debate between אביי and רב יוסף about a שכיב מרע who gave everything away but kept 'any amount of land' (קרקע כל שהוא). אביי cites רבי אלעזר, who says movables count as a reservation in the case of a slave. רב יוסף answers that the word 'land' in the slave משנה was only borrowed from רבי עקיבא's earlier clause. אביי then shows that 'כל שהוא' has a minimum in the case of ראשית הגז. רב יוסף answers that there the חכמים used the phrase only to contrast with רבי דוסא's larger amount. Next the גמרא lists three ways of wording a gift of movables: 'my movables', 'all my movables', and 'everything that moves'. Each wording includes more items than the one before. Then the גמרא asks whether a slave counts as land or as movable property. A proof from the משנה about selling a city is pushed away, because a slave is 'property that walks' in either category. רבינא brings a second proof from רבי אלעזר's rule, and רב נחמן explains that rule by saying outright that a slave is movable property.

Opening lines

וְאָמַר רַב דִּימִי בַּר יוֹסֵף

And Rav Dimi bar Yosef said

אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר:

that Rabbi Elazar said:

עָשׂוּ מְטַלְטְלִין שִׁיּוּר אֵצֶל עֶבֶד,

They made movable property a reservation in the case of a slave,

וְלֹא עָשׂוּ מְטַלְטְלִין שִׁיּוּר אֵצֶל כְּתוּבָּה!

but they did not make movable property a reservation in the case of a כתובה!
