Topic

Who pays for city needs: exemptions for תלמידי חכמים, charges on orphans, and how long until one counts as a resident

Overview

This amud is about who must pay for city needs, and why תלמידי חכמים are often exempt. It finishes רבי יוחנן's reading of 'I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers' and explains why ריש לקיש reads it like רבא. רב נחמן בר יצחק objects to taxing the רבנן and brings proofs from the תורה, the נביאים and the כתובים. Then come rules about city costs: orphans pay for a new well (with their money returned if it fails), everyone pays for gates except the רבנן, and even the רבנן pay for wells but do not join the digging. The story of רבי's storehouses and the humble רבי יונתן בן עמרם leads to רבי's view that troubles come because of עמי הארץ, shown by the crown tax in טבריא. The גמרא then goes back to the משנה's twelve months: רבא tells apart 'members' and 'residents' of a city, and רבי יוחנן limits the twelve months to paying for the city's protection. The general rule is that even orphans pay for anything that benefits them. The amud ends with stories about charity and orphans, and אביי's answer about the 'great מצוה' continues on the next amud.
