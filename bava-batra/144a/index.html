Topic

Profit from improving an undivided estate, a son acquiring his wedding house, and three rules the רבנן fixed without a stated mechanism

Overview

The גמרא finishes a challenge about a rented-out mortar. Since even minors can watch a mortar, its rent is shared by all the heirs. It then explains the משנה's rule that heirs who openly say 'we are working for ourselves' keep the profit. A story about רב ספרא shows that a great scholar does not even need to say it. The גמרא next explains the משנה's case of a wife who improves the estate. רבי ירמיה says she is an heir herself, and two 'obvious' challenges show why the משנה spells out both halves of her case. Then רבי חנינא teaches that a son married off in a house his father set aside for the wedding acquires the house, under four conditions. The גמרא asks about a porch and an inner house and leaves those questions as תיקו. It answers a ברייתא by saying any item of the father's left in the house keeps it his, which is why מר זוטרא and רב אשי hung a sandal and a jug of oil there. Finally, מר זוטרא lists three rules the רבנן fixed as הלכה without explaining how they work: the wedding house, writing all one's property to a wife, and מעמד שלשתן.
