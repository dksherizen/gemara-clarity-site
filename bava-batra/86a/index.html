Topic

Whether a buyer's vessel acquires in the seller's domain, and when movable items are acquired by משיכה versus הגבהה

Overview

The amud opens with רבא finishing a ברייתא about goods unloaded into the buyer's house. He uses it to argue that a buyer's vessel standing in the seller's domain does not acquire for him. רב נחמן בר יצחק and then מר בר רב אשי push off that proof, saying the case may have no vessels at all, such as bundles of garlic. רבינא explains that fixing a price matters because it shows a firm decision to sell. He then tries a proof from רב and שמואל, who say 'a person's vessel acquires for him in any place.' רב אשי limits that rule to where the seller said 'go acquire.' Next the גמרא quotes a משנה: movable items are acquired by משיכה. An אמורא limits it: items people usually lift must be acquired by הגבהה, not by pulling. That rule is tested twice. First comes a ברייתא about a stolen purse, which אביי explains as a purse heavy enough to need a rope. Then comes a ברייתא about buying in the seller's domain, which רב נחמן בר יצחק reads as two separate cases.

Opening lines

פֵּירְקָן וְהִכְנִיסָן לְתוֹךְ בֵּיתוֹ;

If he unloaded them and brought them into his house;

פָּסַק עַד שֶׁלֹּא מָדַד –

if he fixed the price before he measured,

אֵין שְׁנֵיהֶן יְכוֹלִין לַחֲזוֹר בָּהֶן,

neither of them can back out;

מָדַד עַד שֶׁלֹּא פָּסַק –

if he measured before he fixed the price,
