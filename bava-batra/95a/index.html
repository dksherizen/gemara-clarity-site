Topic

Past the allowed margin, is everything returned or only the excess? Three rejected proofs, then the משנה's case of a wine cellar with souring barrels

Overview

Earlier, the גמרא brought רב הונא's rule that a buyer of produce must accept a small amount of dirt mixed in. It then asked: if there is more dirt than allowed, does the seller deduct all of it, or only the amount above the limit? The גמרא tries three proofs: the משנה about selling a בית כור of land, the laws of אונאה (overcharging), and a planter whose trees fail. Each one at first seems to show that past the limit, everything goes back. Each one is pushed away, because each case has a special reason of its own. Then the גמרא turns to the next case in the משנה, a buyer of a wine cellar who accepts ten souring barrels per hundred. It asks how the sale was worded and finds that a ברייתא fits neither option, so it adds the ten-percent rule to the ברייתא's general 'wine cellar' case. It defends this against רבי חייא's teaching about a single barrel. Then it is challenged again by רב זביד's ברייתא, and the answer comes on the next amud.

הָתָם, ״הֵן חָסֵר הֵן יָתֵיר״ אֲמַר לֵיהּ,

There, he said to him, 'whether less or more,'

מִיהוּ רְבִיעַ לָא חֲשִׁיב;
