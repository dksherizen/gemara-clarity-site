Topic

How a small estate (נכסים מועטין) is divided between daughters' sustenance and sons' inheritance: רבא's rule, what happens if the estate's value changes, and whether a widow's sustenance counts in the calculation.

Overview

The גמרא rejects a view that would hand the daughters the whole estate. It adopts רבא's rule instead: the daughters' sustenance is set aside until they become adults, and the sons get the rest. It then states that if a large estate shrinks, the sons keep it, since they already acquired it. Next it asks what happens when a small estate grows. It answers from רבי יוחנן's ruling that orphans' sale of a small estate is valid, which shows the heirs still own it. After that, רבי ירמיה asks רבי אבהו a chain of questions about which claims are subtracted when deciding whether an estate is 'small'. The chain runs from the widow's sustenance, to support pledged to a stepdaughter, to a debt owed to a creditor. Some report that he asked the chain in reverse order, starting with the creditor, and that version continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

שָׁקְלִי לְהוּ בָּנוֹת לְכוּלְּהוּ?!

Shall the daughters take all of it?!

אֶלָּא אָמַר רָבָא:

Rather, Rava said:

מוֹצִיאִין לָהֶן מְזוֹנוֹת לַבָּנוֹת

We set aside sustenance for the daughters

עַד שֶׁיִּבְגְּרוּ,

until they reach adulthood,
