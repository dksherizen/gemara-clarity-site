Topic

Things said in any language: סוטה, וידוי מעשר and קריאת שמע, and quiet תפילה

Overview

The amud opens with the last words of the long משנה listing things that may be said in any language. The גמרא learns from 'וְאָמַר הַכֹּהֵן' that פרשת סוטה may be said in any language. A ברייתא adds that the כהן tells the סוטה, in a language she understands, why she drinks, what she drinks from, and how she became impure, so that nobody will speak badly of the מים המרים. The גמרא then learns וידוי מעשר from סוטה through the shared plain word 'saying'. אביי defends this against רב זביד's comparison to the לויים. This leads to רבי שמעון בן יוחי's teaching that a person says his praise quietly and his pain loudly, so that others will pray for him. The גמרא then defends his teaching that תפילה is quiet to spare sinners shame, just as the חטאת and the עולה share one slaughtering place. Finally, רבי and the חכמים argue whether קריאת שמע may be said in any language, and each side explains the words 'שמע', 'והיו' and 'הדברים'. The amud ends as the גמרא begins to suggest what רבי holds. The suggestion is about language, and it continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְלָנוּ בִּמְקוֹמָן.

and they slept in their place.

גְּמָ׳ פָּרָשַׁת סוֹטָה מְנָלַן?

GEMARA: The section of sotah — from where do we know it?

— דִּכְתִיב: ״וְאָמַר הַכֹּהֵן לָאִשָּׁה״

As it is written: "And the priest shall say to the woman"

— בְּכׇל לָשׁוֹן שֶׁהוּא אוֹמֵר.

in any language that he speaks.
