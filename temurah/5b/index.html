Topic

Does a forbidden act take effect? אביי and רבא tested against תמורה, בכור, מעשר, תרומה, a widow and a כהן גדול, and blemished animals

Overview

אביי and רבא argue about a general rule: when the תורה forbids an act and someone does it anyway, does the act still take effect? אביי says it does, and רבא says it does not. The גמרא tests both views against a series of cases. Against רבא it brings תמורה, תרומה separated before ביכורים, a widow who marries a כהן גדול, and consecrating blemished animals for the מזבח. Each of these works even though it is forbidden. Against אביי it brings בכור and מעשר, which cannot be redeemed even though redemption is only forbidden. Each time, the side under attack answers that the case is an exception learned from a special פסוק. The other side then shows what it learns from that same פסוק. Along the way the גמרא discusses blood of offerings that got mixed, and a לוי who took his מעשר early from the pile. The amud ends as the גמרא opens a new challenge about consecrating unblemished animals for בדק הבית, which continues on the next amud.
