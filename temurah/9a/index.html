Topic

The laws of תמורה: across kinds of animals, the extra words 'טוב', swapping with others' animals, and one-for-many versus one-for-one

Overview

The amud opens with the last line of an earlier discussion: a folk saying that a small thing in hand beats a bigger thing later. A new משנה then teaches that תמורה works across every kind of animal: flock and herd, sheep and goats, males and females, and blemished and unblemished. With blemished animals, it works only if the animal was made holy before it got its blemish. The גמרא finds the source in 'בהמה בבהמה'. אביי and רבא then argue over how the extra words 'טוב' in the פסוק teach the timing rule and the מלקות for swapping good for good. A ברייתא explains why the Torah uses two verbs for swapping, and the גמרא shows how a person can swap with someone else's holy animal: its owner gave permission. A second משנה brings a dispute: the תנא קמא allows one animal for many, while רבי שמעון allows only one for one. ריש לקיש and רבי יוחנן then argue whether רבי שמעון allows swapping the same holy animal a second time, and each has a ברייתא on his side. The amud ends as רבי אבין begins a question about an אשם that was swapped, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

בּוּצִינָא טָב מִקָּרָא.

A zucchini now is better than a gourd later.

מַתְנִי׳ מְמִירִין מִן הַצֹּאן עַל הַבָּקָר,

MISHNA: One makes a substitution from the flock onto the herd,

וּמִן הַבָּקָר עַל הַצֹּאן,

and from the herd onto the flock,

וּמִן הַכְּבָשִׂים עַל הָעִזִּים,

and from the sheep onto the goats,
