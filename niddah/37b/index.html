Niddah 37b

נדה ל״ז ע״ב

Learn Niddah 37b step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Niddah 37b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Whether birth-impurity days count toward a זבה's seven clean days, and when blood seen during labor pains makes a woman a זבה

Overview

The גמרא finishes explaining why the תורה had to teach the count of seven clean days for both a זב and a זבה, since each has its own strictness. אביי then tries to prove that a woman's days of birth-impurity do not count toward a זבה's seven clean days. His first proof, from a ברייתא on "דותה תטמא", is pushed off: "clean" may just mean clean of blood. His second ברייתא compares birth days to נדה days. רבא says it follows רבי אליעזר, and the גמרא asks how one can learn a possible case from an impossible one, which is answered in two ways. The גמרא then returns to the משנה about labor pains. רב חסדא and רבי חנינא argue about a woman whose pains and bleeding both stopped before the birth, and each side defends his view against a challenge from the משנה. רב חסדא explains why רבי מאיר gives both forty and fifty days for early labor pains. Finally, רבי לוי and רב argue about which days the birth keeps her from becoming a זבה, and רב אדא בר אהבה begins a point that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

בְּזָב — מִשּׁוּם דְּלָא מְטַמֵּא בְּאוֹנֶס,

[If it had been written only] with a zav — because he is not made impure through circumstances beyond his control,

אֲבָל זָבָה — דִּמְטַמְּיָא בְּאוֹנֶס

but a zava — who is made impure even through circumstances beyond her control,

אֵימָא לָא, צְרִיכָא.

I might say no; therefore it was necessary.

וְאִי כְּתַב רַחֲמָנָא בְּזָבָה —

And if the Merciful One had written it with a zava —
