Topic

Young children and the laws of נדה and זיבה, which discharges make a man or a woman impure, and the bedding of a בועל נדה compared to a זב.

Overview

The גמרא explains that the extra word "ואשה" in the נדה verse teaches that a man is not impure through a red discharge. It shows that a ten-day-old girl's זיבה needs its own verse, and that this verse already tells us a baby girl can be a נדה, since there is no זבה without נדה. That leaves the נדה verse free to exclude a man from red; a second exclusion covers blood as well as red semen. Next, a ברייתא teaches that even a one-day-old boy can be a זב, either from "איש איש" (רבי יהודה) or from "לזכר ולנקבה" (רבי ישמעאל בנו של רבי יוחנן בן ברוקה). Another ברייתא includes a boy in the laws of semen only from nine years and one day. רבא settles the clash: the nine-year rule is a tradition from סיני, the baby rule comes from the פסוק, and the extra word teaches that a white discharge does not make a woman a זבה. A צריכותא explains why the baby rule is written for both boys and girls. Finally, the גמרא explains the משנה about כותים: bedding under a בועל נדה is like bedding above a זב, making only food and drink impure. It then starts to find the source in the פסוק "תחתיו", and the answer continues on the next amud.
