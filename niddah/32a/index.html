Topic

רבי מאיר's concern for a minority, why only the girls of the כותים are נדות from the cradle, and the דרשה of "וְאִשָּׁה" for young girls

Overview

The גמרא shows that רבי מאיר is the תנא who worries about a small minority. In a ברייתא he forbids minors from doing יבום, in case the boy turns out to be a סריס or the girl an אילונית, while the חכמים simply follow the majority. The גמרא objects that this may only be a common minority. It answers that baby girls seeing blood is also common, citing three incidents where a baby girl was immersed before her mother. A side question asks why the baby in פומבדיתא needed טבילה outside ארץ ישראל. מר זוטרא answers that it was so she could be smeared with תרומה oil, since smearing counts like drinking. The גמרא then explains that only the girls of the כותים were made נדות from the cradle, because we learn from "וְאִשָּׁה" to separate even a baby girl who sees blood, and they do not. Two ברייתות seem to use "וְאִשָּׁה" for different ages. רבא answers that these are traditions only leaned on verses. The amud ends by asking why a verse is needed for a law that is a tradition; the answer comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

רַבִּי מֵאִיר הִיא, דְּתַנְיָא:

It is Rabbi Meir, as it is taught:

קָטָן וּקְטַנָּה

A minor boy and a minor girl

לֹא חוֹלְצִין וְלֹא מְיַבְּמִין,

do not perform chalitza and do not perform yibum,

דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי מֵאִיר.

these are the words of Rabbi Meir.
