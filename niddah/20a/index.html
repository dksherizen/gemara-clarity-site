Topic

Identifying and checking the five colors of impure blood: red, black, crocus, water of earth, and diluted wine

Overview

The גמרא finishes defending its definition of the red color (אדום) of impure blood. אמימר then shows his colleagues what that red looks like, and רב אשי admits that someone who cannot tell such shades apart should not rule on blood. Next, the משנה's 'black like חרת' is explained as the darkest part of dry ink, and several אמוראים give familiar samples of the black shade, such as wax, grapes, a סיואה garment, and the bath attendants' cloths. שמואל and רב יצחק בר אבודימי argue about whether black blood is checked on a white cloth or a red one. עולא, רבי אמי בר אבא, רמי בר אבא, and בר קפרא then give different views on whether shades darker or lighter than the משנה's measures are טמא or טהור. For the crocus color, the standard is moist saffron, the middle leaf of the middle layer, still in its clumps of earth, and this settles four seemingly conflicting ברייתות. Finally, the גמרא explains how to prepare the 'water of earth' sample. It ends a question about stirring by hand with תיקו and reports practical rulings on where and how the earth check may be done.

Opening lines

בְּקִילוֹר,

to an eye salve,

וְרַבִּי תָּלָה בִּשְׂרַף שִׁקְמָה.

and רבי attributed a stain to the sap of a sycamore.

מַאי לַָאו אַאָדוֹם?

What, is it not about the red color?

לָא, אַשְּׁאָר דָּמִים.

No, it is about the other bloods.
