Topic

Whether the blood and fluids of non-Jews make impure, and the idea of a היכרא that shows an impurity is only rabbinic

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier story. The כהן גדול was not worried about the spit of a plain עם הארץ, because it was a רגל. On a רגל the רבנן treated every Jew as a חבר, based on a פסוק in שופטים. A new משנה then brings two disputes between בית שמאי and בית הלל. The first is about the blood of a נכרית and the דם טהרה of a מצורעת. The second is about the blood of a יולדת who has not yet immersed. It also gives a case where everyone agrees, a יולדת בזוב. The גמרא asks how בית שמאי can call a נכרית's blood pure when חז"ל decreed that גוים are like זבין. It explains that any decree on her blood would confuse people about the Torah rule, so her pure blood serves as a היכרא. רבא then rules that a נכרי man's זוב is impure even for בית שמאי, and his קרי is pure even for בית הלל, with the pure קרי acting as the sign. The גמרא tries to support רבא, first from a משנה, which is rejected, and then from a ברייתא that continues on the next amud.
