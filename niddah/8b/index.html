Topic

Where הבדלה is said, balsam sap as fruit, the 'virgin of blood', and when a pregnancy counts as known

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an explanation of why רבי אלעזר rules like רבי אליעזר that הבדלה is said in הודאה. רבי אליעזר could not have been quoting רבי חנינא בן גמליאל, who was much younger, so the answer is that רבי חנינא בן גמליאל agreed with him. A ברייתא about the prayers of יום הכפורים seems to show otherwise. רב נחמן בר יצחק answers that there רבי חנינא בן גמליאל was only reporting his forefathers' view. Next, the גמרא returns to whether רבי אליעזר is the תנא who treats balsam sap as fruit, and it shows that even the רבנן may agree in some cases. Then the גמרא turns to the משנה's בתולה, which here means a woman who never saw menstrual blood. It asks why רב כהנא's list of three kinds of בתולה leaves this one out, and several אמוראים give reasons. After that, the גמרא sets three months as the time a pregnancy becomes known. It rules that a pregnant woman who miscarries air keeps her pregnant status. The amud ends with a second ברייתא that seems to contradict that ruling, and the question continues on the next amud.
