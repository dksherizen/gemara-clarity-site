Niddah 56a

נדה נ״ו ע״א

Learn Niddah 56a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof colour-coded.

Open Niddah 56a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

The זב's fluids, a dried or burnt שרץ, טומאה only when moist, and retroactive טומאה from a שרץ or a כתם

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא: a זב's urine is טמא because of the word "וזאת", since logic fails when blood from the same place is טהור. The word "הוא" teaches that the blood is טהור. To explain why urine is included and blood excluded, רבי יוחנן in the name of רבי שמעון בן יוחי says the impure fluids are like רוק: they gather, come out, and can be reabsorbed. This rule also excludes a woman's milk. רבא explains that זוב itself cannot serve as a model, because it is unique. The גמרא then turns to the שרץ. ריש לקיש rules that a dried or burnt שרץ with an intact skeleton is still טמא. רבי זירא defends him by splitting whole from partial, backed by a ברייתא on the words "בהם" and "מהם". The גמרא then gives sources for why the items in the משנה are טמא only when moist, discusses soaking dried items in lukewarm water, and has שמואל explain that dried corpse flesh still carries טומאת רקב. A new משנה teaches that a שרץ found in an alleyway or a כתם found on a garment makes things טמא back to the last check or cleaning. The גמרא begins asking why sweeping counts as a cutoff.

Opening lines

מִמָּקוֹם טָמֵא —

From a place of impurity,

אֵינוֹ דִּין שֶׁיְּהוּ טְמֵאִין?

is it not logical that they should be impure?

דָּם הַיּוֹצֵא מִפִּי הָאַמָּה יוֹכִיחַ,

Blood that comes out of the opening of the penis will prove otherwise,

שֶׁבָּא מִמָּקוֹם טָמֵא וְטָהוֹר.

for it comes from an impure place, and yet it is pure.
