Niddah 23b

נדה כ״ג ע״ב

Open Niddah 23b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

When a miscarried fetus counts as a human birth: animal shapes, the human face, and defects

Overview

The גמרא first tries to prove from a משנה in בכורות that an animal-shaped fetus born to a woman cannot survive. רבא rejects the proof, because firstborn status for inheritance follows its own פסוק, ראשית אונו: the בכור must be a child the father would mourn. Next, רב אדא בר אהבה asks אביי the reverse case according to רבי מאיר. If a human-shaped fetus is found inside a slaughtered animal, may it be eaten? A proof from רבי יוחנן's pigeon case is pushed aside, since a human foot is at least like a hoof. The גמרא then returns to the משנה's argument over how much human form a miscarried fetus needs for טומאת לידה. רב says the face decides. There are two versions of what רבי מאיר and חכמים hold, and רבי יוחנן (the whole face) and חסא (half the face) each follow one of them. אביי answers a ברייתא that seems to say one feature is enough. רבא then rules on a fetus with one eye or one thigh and on a sealed וושט. A ברייתא defines a גוף אטום, and the question of how much must be missing continues onto the next amud.

Opening lines

אַף אֲנַן נָמֵי תְּנֵינָא:

We, too, have learned in a משנה:

הַמַּפֶּלֶת כְּמִין בְּהֵמָה חַיָּה וְעוֹף —

A woman who miscarries something like a domestic animal, a wild animal, or a bird —

וָלָד מְעַלְּיָא הוּא, דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי מֵאִיר.

it is a full-fledged offspring; these are the words of רבי מאיר.

וַחֲכָמִים אוֹמְרִים:
