Topic

Warming, cooking, and bathing on שבת: oil, water, כלי שני, and bathhouse conduct

Overview

This amud opens by tying an earlier harsh statement about bathhouse transgressors to a known תנא. A ברייתא then allows strolling through big-city bathhouses on שבת, and רבא limits this to cities, because small village bathhouses are too hot. Next comes a series of ברייתות about warmth on שבת: warming by a fire before rinsing, a warm towel versus a kettle, and taking the chill off water or oil near a fire. The גמרא asks how the תנא קמא rules on oil, and רבה and רב יוסף read the ברייתא one way while רב נחמן בר יצחק reads it another way. שמואל sets the practical rule that for both oil and water the line is יד סולדת בו, which רחבא defines as heat that would burn a baby's belly. A story about רבי in the bathhouse teaches that oil can be cooked, that warming oil counts as cooking it, and that a כלי שני does not cook. The גמרא explains that רבי could teach there because stopping someone from an איסור is different, and רבינא and רב חסדא are reconciled about cooking in the hot springs of טבריה. The amud ends with רבי זירא's report of רבי אבהו floating in a bath, and the answer to the challenge against it continues on the next amud.
