Shabbos 83b

שבת פ״ג ע״ב

Shabbat 83b

Topic

The טומאה of עבודה זרה (היסט, broken pieces, minimum size, rabbinic leniency), and why a ship cannot become טמא, with lessons on devotion to Torah study

Overview

This amud first finishes the discussion of how עבודה זרה makes things טמא. The גמרא shows that a ברייתא about the היסט of a זב can still fit רבי עקיבא, because 'זב' includes everything like it. רב חמא בר גוריא asks whether broken pieces of an idol carry טומאה, and the question stays unresolved (תיקו). רב אחדבוי בר אמי asks about an idol smaller than a כזית. After רב יוסף shows that tiny idols are clearly forbidden, the question is narrowed to טומאה, and a ברייתא rules that less than a כזית has no טומאה, like a מת. According to רבנן, the טומאה of idols is only דרבנן, so its comparisons to שרץ, נדה, and מת teach only leniencies. Next comes a new משנה: a ship cannot become טמא, learned from 'דרך אניה בלב ים'. חנניה learns the same law from a שק instead, and the גמרא shows the two sources differ about an earthenware ship and a Jordan River boat. The amud ends with lessons about never leaving the בית המדרש and devoting oneself fully to Torah, and רבא's statement begins at the very end and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

כָּרְעוּ הֵן טְהוֹרִין.

If they tipped (the scale), they are pure.

כְּמַאן אָזְלָא הָא דְּתַנְיָא

According to whom is that which was taught (in a baraita):

כׇּל הַטֻּמְאוֹת הַמְּסִיטוֹת טְהוֹרוֹת,

All impure things that move (objects) — (the objects remain) pure,

חוּץ מֵהֶיסֵּטוֹ שֶׁל זָב

except for the moving done by a zav,

  1. StatementEnd of the scale case: the food stays pure
  2. QuestionWhose view is the ברייתא that only a זב makes things impure by moving them?

  4. QuestionDo broken pieces of an idol still carry impurity?

  6. QuestionSecond version: the doubt is only when an ordinary person can reassemble it

  8. QuestionWhat is the law for an idol smaller than an olive?

  13. QuestionWhat does each comparison teach according to רבנן?
