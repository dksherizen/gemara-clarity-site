Topic

The soldiers' atonement for gazing; the משנה on hair and ornaments a woman may wear on שבת; רב on the courtyard and on מראית העין

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a lesson about the soldiers who fought מדין: they needed a כפרה because they gazed at forbidden sights. רב ששת adds that gazing at even a woman's little finger for pleasure is as serious as gazing at her private area. A new משנה then lists what a woman may wear outside on שבת: hair strands, sewn head ornaments, a מוך, and items in her mouth. A כבול and a פאה נכרית are allowed only in the חצר, and רבי and the חכמים disagree about a false tooth. The גמרא explains why all three sources of hair had to be listed, and a ברייתא forbids a mismatch between a young girl's hair and an old woman's hair. רב rules that whatever is forbidden in the street is also forbidden in the חצר, except the כבול and the פאה נכרית. עולא explains this exception: the חכמים did not want her to become unattractive to her husband, a concern רבי עקיבא also shows. Finally, רב rules that what is forbidden because of מראית העין is forbidden even in private. A plugged bell is used to challenge this, and the גמרא answers that it is a dispute between תנאים, with the proof coming on the next amud.
