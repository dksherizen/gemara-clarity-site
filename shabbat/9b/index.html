Topic

Activities not to begin close to מנחה, which מנחה is meant, and when an activity counts as begun

Overview

The משנה says a person should not start certain activities close to מנחה before he davens: a haircut, the bathhouse, the tannery, a meal, or judging a case. If he already started, he does not stop for תפלה, but he does stop for קריאת שמע. The גמרא first suggests the משנה means close to מנחה קטנה. That would clash with רבי יהושע בן לוי, who forbids even tasting food once מנחה time arrives. So the גמרא says the משנה means מנחה גדולה, and each activity is a long version, like בן אלעשה's elaborate haircut or a big meal. רב אחא בר יעקב offers another approach: even short versions are forbidden, because each one might unexpectedly drag on, for example if the scissors break or he faints in the bathhouse. The גמרא then defines when each activity counts as 'started.' For a meal, רב says it starts with washing hands and רבי חנינא says with loosening the belt, and the two fit different local customs. Finally, אביי applies the belt rule to ערבית, and the גמרא challenges the idea that a required ערבית would make him stop his meal.

The steps (17)

  1. StatementActivities you may not start close to מנחה
  2. StatementIf you already started, you do not stop

  5. ChallengeDoes this contradict רבי יהושע בן לוי?

  7. ResolutionThe long version of each activity

  10. ResolutionEating and judging: even a small meal or the end of a trial is forbidden

  12. QuestionWhen does a bath count as begun?

  14. StatementWhen does a meal count as begun? Two opinions

  16. Statementאביי: Babylonian scholars who loosened their belts need not stop for an optional ערבית
