Topic

Shabbat 113a: Temporary knots on a bucket, moving a weaver's loom parts, folding clothes on שבת, and honoring שבת through dress, walking and speech.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a point about temporary knots. רב יוסף then rules like רבי אליעזר בן יעקב, and אביי insists on knowing whether חכמים disagree, because Torah is not a song to repeat without understanding. A new משנה allows tying a bucket with a belt but not a rope, while רבי יהודה permits the rope. The גמרא explains that the rope is a weaver's rope, and it settles an apparent contradiction from a ברייתא: רבנן decree only where confusion is likely, and רבי יהודה holds a bow is itself a knot. Next, the גמרא discusses which loom parts may be moved on שבת. Weavers' tools and beams may be moved according to שמואל, but the posts may not, because a person might fill in the holes in his house; רבי יהודה בר ליואי forbids moving the heavy beams. A second משנה deals with folding clothes, making beds, and burning sacrificial fats when שבת and יום הכיפורים fall next to each other. The גמרא limits folding to one person folding a new, white garment when he has nothing else to wear. It then teaches how to honor שבת with clothing, walking and speech, based on a פסוק in ישעיה, and that explanation continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

חֲדָא מִינַּיְיהוּ בַּטּוֹלֵי מְבַטֵּיל,

[since] one of them he voids,

קָא מַשְׁמַע לַן.

it teaches us [that both are temporary knots].

אָמַר רַב יוֹסֵף אָמַר רַב יְהוּדָה אָמַר שְׁמוּאֵל:

Rav Yosef said that Rav Yehuda said that Shmuel said:

הֲלָכָה כְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב.

The halacha is in accordance with Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov.

The steps (44)

  1. ResolutionEven when one knot is undone, both still count as temporary
  2. StatementThe הלכה follows רבי אליעזר בן יעקב

  5. ResponseTorah is not a song to repeat without understanding

  7. Statementרבי יהודה's rule: no liability for a temporary knot
  8. QuestionWhat kind of rope does the משנה mean?
  9. ChallengeA regular rope would make a permanent knot
  10. ResolutionIt means a weaver's rope
  11. QuestionSo the dispute is about making a rabbinic decree
  12. ChallengeA ברייתא shows the opinions reversed
  13. ChallengeStating the double contradiction
  14. ResolutionAnswering for רבנן: a rope is confused with a rope, but a bow is not confused with a knot
  15. ResolutionAnswering for רבי יהודה: to him a bow is itself a knot
  16. Statementרב: one may bring a rope from home and tie it between a cow and a trough
  17. ChallengeA ברייתא forbids bringing a rope from home
  18. ResolutionThe ברייתא means an ordinary rope; רב means a weaver's rope
  19. Statementשמואל: weavers' tools may be moved on שבת
  20. QuestionAsking about the upper and lower beams of the loom
  21. Responseרב יהודה is unsure
  22. Conclusionרב נחמן's report: even the beams may be moved, but not the posts
  23. Challengeרבא asks why the posts are forbidden
  24. ProofProof from turnips buried under a vine
  25. ResolutionA field is different from a house
  26. Questionרבי יוחנן asks about the heavy loom beams
  27. ResponseThe beams may not be moved
  28. QuestionWhat is the reason?
  29. ResponseThey are never moved anyway
  30. StatementNew משנה: folding garments and making beds
  31. Statementרבי ישמעאל: from יום הכיפורים to שבת
  32. Statementרבי עקיבא: neither direction
  33. StatementFolding is allowed only by one person
  34. StatementOnly new garments
  35. StatementOnly white garments
  36. ConclusionOnly if he has nothing else to wear
  37. Proofרבן גמליאל's household did not fold their white clothes
  38. Statementרב הונא: change clothes, or let your clothes hang loose
  39. Challengeרב ספרא: doesn't this look like arrogance?
  40. ResolutionIt is clearly for שבת, so it is not arrogance
  41. Statement'And you shall honor it': שבת clothes should differ from weekday clothes
  42. Statement'Not doing your ways': שבת walking should differ from weekday walking
  43. Statement'From pursuing your affairs': your business is forbidden, Heaven's affairs are permitted
  44. Statement'And speaking a word': setting up the next explanation
