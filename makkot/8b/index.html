Topic

Whether doing a מצוה affects liability (impurity from a מת מצוה, the עומר harvest, a father who strikes his son), and who goes into גלות for killing whom.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion between רבא and רבינא. A person made impure by a מת מצוה is still liable for entering the מקדש, and the source is the extra word 'עוד' in the פסוק. A second version places the discussion on a ברייתא about 'בחריש ובקציר תשבת'. There, רבי ישמעאל teaches that only optional harvesting is forbidden on שבת, so harvesting the עומר, which is a מצוה, is allowed. רבא first proves that plowing is never a מצוה, defends this against a challenge from a father who disciplines his son, and then switches to a stronger proof from comparing harvesting to plowing. A new משנה follows, explaining who goes into גלות for accidentally killing whom: father and son, Jews and others, and a גר תושב. The גמרא narrows the case of a father who is exiled until it reaches a son learning carpentry who already knows another trade. For a son who kills his father, רב כהנא says the sources follow רבי שמעון and the רבנן, while רבא says the ברייתא excludes only accidental wounding. Finally, the גמרא asks why a Jew would get lashes because of a כותי or an עבד. After the answer about an עד זומם fails, רב אחא בריה דרב איקא begins to explain it as a case of striking, and this continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

״טָמֵא יִהְיֶה״ – מִכׇּל מָקוֹם.

"He shall be impure" – in any case.

הָהוּא מִיבְּעֵי לֵיהּ לְכִדְתַנְיָא:

He needs that for what was taught in a ברייתא:

״טָמֵא יִהְיֶה״ – לְרַבּוֹת טְבוּל יוֹם,

"He shall be impure" – to include one who immersed that day;

״טוּמְאָתוֹ בוֹ״ – לְרַבּוֹת מְחוּסַּר כִּיפּוּרִים!

"His impurity is upon him" – to include one lacking atonement!
