Topic

יש אם למקרא and כח כחו; the משנה of the stone-thrower: when גלות applies, ״וּמָצָא״, and אבא שאול's rule that exile is only for an optional activity

Overview

The amud opens by defending רבי's ruling about a flying splinter. His spelling-based proof seems to clash with his own view, יש אם למקרא, and the answer is that his word 'ועוד' adds a second proof that does not depend on the spelling. רב פפא then compares dates knocked off a palm tree to the splinter case and shows what a true כח כחו case would be. A new משנה follows. A person who throws a stone into רשות הרבים and kills goes to גלות, unless, according to רבי אליעזר בן יעקב, the victim stuck his head out after the stone was thrown. Throwing into one's own courtyard brings גלות only if the victim had permission to be there, which is learned from the forest פסוק. אבא שאול learns from the same פסוק that גלות applies only to an optional activity, so a father, a teacher and a בית דין messenger are exempt. The גמרא explains that the stone-thrower is a שוגג, neither פושע nor אונס, using רב פפא's scrap-heap case, and it explains the word ״וּמָצָא״ from the context of its פסוק. Finally רבא defends אבא שאול's reading that the wood-cutting is רשות. When רב אדא בר אהבה challenges his proof from the word אשר, רבא begins an answer that continues on the next amud.

and Beit Shammai, and Rabbi Shimon, and Rabbi Akiva,
