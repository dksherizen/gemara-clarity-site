Topic

Who goes to גלות: the downward-motion rule, the slipping ladder rung, the butcher, and the ax that slips

Overview

The משנה ends with a rule: an accidental killer goes to גלות only if the killing came through a downward motion. שמואל finds the source in the פסוק 'וַיַּפֵּל עָלָיו'. A ברייתא then reads the פסוקים word by word. רבא and רבה explain that its 'מזיד' and 'מתכוין' mean an אומר מותר and someone who aimed at a different target. Another ברייתא lists cases left out of גלות (a corner, an enemy, a wrong throw) and cases brought in (a shove, or a downward motion done in order to go up). רבי יוחנן uses that last idea to rule that a climber whose ladder rung slips and kills goes to גלות. He defends this by showing that the משנה's זה הכלל comes to add a butcher, whose four ברייתות all follow the direction of the swing. The גמרא then rejects the idea that two ladder ברייתות argue about up or down, and settles them in three other ways. A new משנה brings a מחלוקת between רבי and חכמים about an ax head slipping off its handle versus a chip flying off the wood. רב explains that they argue whether we learn from the spelling (יש אם למסורת) or the reading (יש אם למקרא). The גמרא begins to question whether רבי really holds יש אם למסורת, and this continues on the next amud.
