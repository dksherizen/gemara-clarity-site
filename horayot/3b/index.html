Topic

How much of the court must agree to a mistaken ruling, the judges' shared responsibility, and the laws of acting on a reversed ruling or a ruling that uproots a whole מצוה.

Overview

This amud opens by closing a question from the previous amud with תיקו. Next, רבי יונתן teaches that a court brings its offering only if every judge agreed to the mistaken ruling, and רב הונא בריה דרב הושעיא supports this from the word 'כל'. The גמרא defends רבי יונתן against two משניות by saying the judge who disagreed nodded along. רב משרשיא then refutes him from the פסוק 'הגוי כלו', which follows the majority, so 'כל עדת' is reinterpreted to mean that the whole court must be present. רבי יהושע teaches that every judge, even a student before his teacher, shares the blame, and רב הונא and רב אשי brought others in to share that weight. A new משנה discusses someone who acted on a ruling the court had already reversed, and it teaches that uprooting a whole מצוה is פטור while cancelling one detail is חייב. The גמרא explains רבי שמעון's exemption, answers a challenge about collecting money for the offerings, and ends with רבי זירא comparing רבי אלעזר's view to eating fat that might be חלב.
