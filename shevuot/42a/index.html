Topic

Weakened documents, a borrower's promise to trust the lender, and oaths involving a minor or an heir

Overview

This amud covers several cases about debts, documents and oaths. First it finishes the gallnuts story: witnesses prove the borrower named a false market price, and רבא rules he is a כפרן, because people do remember the set market price. Next, a lender with a שטר admits he was paid but says the money was for other debts. רב נחמן says the שטר is weakened, and רב פפא says it is not. The final הלכה is that the שטר is weakened, but only when the payment was made in front of witnesses. In a private payment the lender is believed because of a מיגו. Then the גמרא discusses a borrower who promised to believe the lender about non-payment. It asks whether that promise beats witnesses, and whether 'like two witnesses' covers three. Here רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע teaches that in testimony two are like a hundred, unlike counting opinions. Finally, the גמרא explains why no oath is taken on a minor's claim. It shows that the משנה's 'we swear to a קטן' means an adult heir claiming his father's debt, following רבי אליעזר בן יעקב.

Opening lines

עַפְצֵי דְּקָיְימִי בְּשִׁיתָּא שִׁיתָּא?״

...gallnuts that were selling at six, six [dinars per measure]?

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: ״לָאו בְּאַרְבְּעָה אַרְבָּעָה הֲווֹ קָיְימִי?״

He said to him: Weren't they selling at four, four?

אֲתוֹ תְּרֵי סָהֲדִי וַאֲמַרוּ:

Two witnesses came and said:

״אִין, בְּאַרְבְּעָה אַרְבָּעָה הֲווֹ קָיְימִי״.

Yes, they were selling at four, four.
