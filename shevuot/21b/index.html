Topic

Which opinion the oath משנה follows, and whether רבי עקיבא holds that eating any amount brings liability everywhere or only with oaths

Overview

The גמרא first looks at the words "זו היא" ("this is") at the end of the משנה about שבועת שוא and asks what they come to exclude. After going back and forth, it concludes that the whole משנה follows רבי עקיבא, because a line about future oaths should exclude a future case. Next it turns to רבי עקיבא's ruling that someone who swore not to eat is liable for eating any amount. It asks whether רבי עקיבא holds like רבי שמעון, that a tiny amount brings liability everywhere, or only rules this way for oaths. One proof from the משנה is pushed off, but proofs from a נזיר who soaked bread in wine and from ריש לקיש's explanation of an oath on forbidden foods show that, in general, רבי עקיבא holds like the רבנן. Finally the גמרא tests the רבנן's question "where do we find liability for any amount?" with an ant, הקדש, and a clearly stated oath, and answers each one. The case of dirt cannot be answered that way, so the גמרא begins to reword the רבנן's question, and this continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֵימָא סֵיפָא:

Say the end of the משנה:

זוֹ הִיא שְׁבוּעַת שָׁוְא

This is a vain oath,

שֶׁחַיָּיבִין עַל זְדוֹנָהּ מַכּוֹת

for which one is liable to lashes if done on purpose,

וְעַל שִׁגְגָתָהּ פָּטוּר.

and if done by mistake, he is exempt.
