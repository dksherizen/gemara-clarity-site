Topic

A warned, deliberate false deposit oath: מלקות, קרבן, or both, and רבה's rule that denying a debt that has witnesses brings no קרבן

Overview

רב כהנא asks about someone who deliberately swears falsely about a deposit (שבועת הפקדון) after witnesses warned him. Does he bring the usual אשם, get only מלקות, or get both? A first proof from a ברייתא is pushed aside in two ways. רבא בר איתי says it follows only רבי שמעון. רב כהנא says the correct wording of the ברייתא never mentions מלקות. Further proofs fall away because they might be about someone who was never warned. A ברייתא comparing a נזיר טמא to a deposit oath is also deflected: 'not flogged' may simply mean that lashes alone do not finish his case. רבה then objects that when witnesses exist, a false denial is only empty words. This shows he holds that denying a debt that has עדים brings no קרבן. רבה himself turns down a ברייתא offered in his support, and the גמרא begins a list of proofs against his rule, starting with a משנה about two sets of witnesses.

Opening lines

לְהוּ: הֵזִיד בִּשְׁבוּעַת הַפִּקָּדוֹן

to them: If one intentionally swore falsely about a deposit

וְהִתְרוּ בּוֹ, מַהוּ?

and they warned him, what is the law?

כֵּיוָן דְּחִידּוּשׁ הוּא –

Since it is a novelty,

דִּבְכׇל הַתּוֹרָה לָא אַשְׁכְּחַן

for in the entire Torah we do not find

  21. ConclusionThe whole ברייתא is about denying details only
  22. ProofProof from the משנה that an intentional false oath brings an אשם

  24. ProofProof from two sets of witnesses who both denied
  25. QuestionWhy is the first set liable if the second set was still there?
