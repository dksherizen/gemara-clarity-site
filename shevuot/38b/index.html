Topic

The end of פרק שבועת הפקדון, and the opening of שבועת הדיינין: when one who admits part of a claim must swear, and how the court administers the oath

Overview

The גמרא finishes asking whether a man who swore to one claimant and then said 'and not yours either' to the others is liable for each claim separately. From רבי חייא's count of twenty offerings it concludes that such a denial counts as specific. It then explains why רבי שמעון exempts a false oath about rape or seduction. אביי knocks down רבא's grain parable, and רבין's version of רבי יוחנן says that according to רבי שמעון the father claims only the קנס. That ends פרק שבועת הפקדון. A new משנה opens the laws of שבועת הדיינין, the oath of one who admits part of a claim: the minimum amounts, full denial versus partial admission, and when the admission counts as the same kind as the claim. It also covers vessels and land, and whose claims can lead to an oath. The גמרא then teaches that the person swearing must hold a holy object, and the הלכה follows רבא and not רב פפא. It adds rules for standing or sitting, and says the oath may be said in any language. The judges' warning before the oath begins here and continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אוֹ דִלְמָא אַכׇּל חֲדָא וַחֲדָא מִיחַיַּיב?

Or perhaps he is liable for each and every one?

תָּא שְׁמַע, דְּתָנֵי רַבִּי חִיָּיא:

Come and hear, for Rabbi Chiyya taught:

הֲרֵי כָּאן עֶשְׂרִים חַטָּאוֹת.

Here there are twenty sin-offerings.

הֵיכִי דָמֵי?

What is the case?
