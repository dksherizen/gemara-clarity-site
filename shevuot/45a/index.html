Topic

Suspect litigants, the oaths of those who swear and collect, and why the רבנן let a hired worker swear and collect his wages.

Overview

This amud finishes the משנה about oaths and begins the גמרא. A person who swore falsely is suspect about oaths. So are people who take money unfairly, like dice players and lenders with interest. When one side is suspect, the other side swears and collects. If both are suspect, רבי יוסי and רבי מאיר disagree about what happens. The משנה then explains the storekeeper's ledger case, where בן ננס objects to letting two people swear opposite things. Next come disputes between a customer and a storekeeper or money changer, where רבי יהודה disagrees with the first opinion. The משנה then lists cases where a woman collects her כתובה, or orphans collect a debt, only with an oath. It ends with people who must swear even without a specific claim. In the גמרא, a פסוק shows that Torah oaths only free a person from paying. The גמרא then works hard to explain why the רבנן let a hired worker swear and collect. Its final answer is that the busy employer probably forgot to pay, and the oath puts his mind at ease.
