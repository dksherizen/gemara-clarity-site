Topic

שבועת העדות: when witnesses are exempt, and which oath wordings and names of God make them liable

Overview

This amud opens by finishing an earlier point: since שבועת העדות leaves out the word "ונעלם," even a witness who swears falsely on purpose (מזיד) brings a קרבן. Then a משנה lists five cases where witnesses who falsely deny knowing testimony are exempt. They are exempt when the testimony is not about a real money claim, when the oath came before they knew anything, when the oath was called out to a general crowd, when they are unfit to testify, and when the demand did not come from the תובע himself. The גמרא brings a ברייתא with a פסוק as the source for each case, and רבי אלעזר reads "אם לוא יגיד" as "to him," meaning the plaintiff. A second משנה teaches which wordings count as an oath, and that God's names and descriptions count while heaven and earth do not. It also covers cursing with these names, where רבי מאיר and the חכמים disagree. In the גמרא, רב יהודה and then אביי explain how "משביעני עליכם" works without God's name. The amud ends with a contradiction: a ברייתא says חנון ורחום may be erased. אביי begins to answer that the משנה means "by the One Who is gracious."
