Topic

Can a son collect a debt by oath when his father would have needed an oath? The challenge to רב ושמואל's rule (אין אדם מוריש שבועה לבניו), רב יוסף's answer, and רב נחמן's decision to limit the rule.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא that seems to contradict רב ושמואל. Their rule is that a father cannot pass down to his son an oath he would have had to take. רב יוסף answers that the ברייתא follows בית שמאי. רב נחמן refuses to overturn רב ושמואל, but he rules that their rule should not be extended. Two attempts to treat a case as outside the rule are rejected: a guarantor, and a brother as heir. רב חמא says the הלכה was never decided between רב ושמואל and רבי אלעזר, so a judge who follows either one has ruled properly. רב פפא therefore says an orphans' document is neither torn up nor used to collect. The גמרא then explains the משנה: partners, sharecroppers and a household manager swear even on a 'maybe' claim, because they tend to allow themselves to take. That oath requires a denied claim worth two silver מעה, like רב. Finally, a ברייתא about שביעית proves that other claims can be rolled onto a rabbinic oath. The amud ends as רב הונא begins a new statement.
