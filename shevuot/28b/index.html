Topic

Conditional oaths on two loaves, the proof that a condition fulfilled by mistake does not count, and אבימי's test of עיפא on when a repeated oath takes effect.

Overview

The גמרא finishes its cases of a שבועה with a condition, like 'I will not eat this loaf if I eat that loaf.' When both loaves are eaten on purpose, the order matters. If he ate the forbidden loaf first, the warning he got was only a התראת ספק, and that depends on the dispute between רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש. In a two-way oath, where each loaf is the condition for the other, he is liable only if he knowingly fulfilled the condition that made the other loaf forbidden. רב מרי supports this from the משנה in נדרים about mistaken vows, together with a ברייתא saying mistaken oaths are released too. Then the גמרא tells how אבימי tested his brother עיפא with three questions about repeated oaths, and corrected all three answers. An oath about the past is a new lie each time. A second oath only takes effect if it forbids something new. At the end, אביי starts showing that עיפא's 'ten then nine' answer can sometimes be right, using a case of רבה about figs and grapes that continues on the next amud.
