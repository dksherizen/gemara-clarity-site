Topic

When a false oath counts as a mistake that brings a קרבן, and whether an oath needs spoken words (שמואל).

Overview

רבא asks רב נחמן how a false oath about the past can ever be a mistake that brings a קרבן. If the person knew the truth, he lied on purpose, and if he did not know, he could not help it. רב נחמן answers that the person knew the oath was forbidden but did not know it requires a קרבן. Even the רבנן, who usually disagree with מונבז on this point, agree here, because a קרבן for a plain לאו with no כרת is unusual. Next, רבינא asks about a hungry man who forgot his oath and ate the loaf he swore off. רבא answers from a ברייתא that a קרבן comes only if knowing would have stopped him. Then שמואל rules that an oath decided in the heart must be said out loud. The גמרא defends this rule against two ברייתות. רב ששת rereads the first one, and the second, about the gifts to the משכן ('כל נדיב לב'), is shown to be a holy matter that cannot teach the rule for everyday oaths.
