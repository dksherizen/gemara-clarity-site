Topic

Testimony about a husband's death: conflicting witnesses, why a צרה cannot testify for her rival, and the argument of רבי טרפון and רבי עקיבא about a rival or mother-in-law eating תרומה.

Overview

The amud opens with the last line of a משנה: when witnesses contradict each other about whether a husband died, his wife may not remarry. The גמרא then looks back at the earlier case of two co-wives, where the one who says 'he did not die' may not remarry. It explains that the words 'he did not die' do not mean a silent צרה could rely on her rival. They rule out the idea that she is lying just to spite her rival. Next the גמרא asks why רבי מאיר does not also disagree in that earlier clause. רבי אלעזר says he does. רבי יוחנן says even רבי מאיר agrees there, but a line of the משנה is a difficulty for רבי יוחנן, and it stays unanswered. A new משנה teaches that a wife who says her husband died may remarry, but her צרה or her חמות may not. רבי טרפון lets that other woman keep eating תרומה, while רבי עקיבא forbids her both. The גמרא explains why both cases were needed, rules like רבי טרפון, and אביי supports this from a later משנה showing that a rival wife's words are ignored.

Opening lines

אִשָּׁה אוֹמֶרֶת: ״מֵת״,

A woman says: 'He died,'

וְאִשָּׁה אוֹמֶרֶת: ״לֹא מֵת״ —

and a woman says: 'He did not die' —

הֲרֵי זוֹ לֹא תִּנָּשֵׂא.

this one may not marry.

גְּמָ׳
