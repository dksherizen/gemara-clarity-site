Topic

The sources for the scope of אחות אשתו and אשת אח, the extra punishments written for specific עריות, and when הַעֲרָאָה counts as a forbidden act.

Overview

The גמרא notes that our משנה forbids a wife's sister whether she shares a father or a mother with the wife. After weighing a comparison to אחותו against one to דודתו, it learns the rule from אשת אח, which matches on both points. A ברייתא then proves that אשת אח itself applies to a brother from either parent: two logical comparisons cancel out, so the extra words ״עֶרְוַת אָחִיךָ הִיא״ decide it. The גמרא defends that reading against attempts to use those words for other cases. It also learns from the word ״הִיא״ that a maternal brother's wife stays forbidden even after her husband dies. Next it explains why the תורה spells out כרת for a sister, and ״עֲרִירִים״ for an aunt. Last, it asks where we learn that הַעֲרָאָה, the first stage of relations, counts for ordinary לאוין and for the לאוין of כהונה. The source for prohibitions that come from an עשה is asked at the end and answered on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

אַלְמָא אֲחוֹת אִשְׁתּוֹ,

Apparently, his wife's sister,

בֵּין מִן הָאָב בֵּין מִן הָאֵם — אֲסוּרוֹת.

whether from the father or from the mother, is forbidden.

מְנָלַן?

From where do we know this?

יָלֵיף מֵאֲחוֹתוֹ.

He derives it from his sister.

The steps (38)

  1. StatementThe משנה implies a wife's sister is forbidden from either parent

  6. ChallengeOn the contrary: the aunt matches through marriage

  10. QuestionDoes a brother's wife include a brother from the mother?

  12. ChallengeOr compare to his aunt instead

  17. ResolutionThe childless case during the husband's life is already known from רב הונא

  21. RejectionMaybe the widow with children is forbidden only by a weak prohibition
  22. ResolutionAnother verse already teaches the full prohibition
  23. ChallengeShould a maternal brother's wife be permitted after his death?

  29. ChallengeWhy does the תורה write 'childless' about an aunt?

  31. ResolutionWhy both childlessness phrases are needed

  37. ResponseLearned from the word 'taking' in both places
