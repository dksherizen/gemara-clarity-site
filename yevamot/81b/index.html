Yevamos 81b

יבמות פ״א ע״ב

Topic

Items too important to be canceled in a mixture: רבי מאיר's 'usually counted' rule, the את/כל wording dispute, and the ברייתא about a single impure holy piece

Overview

The גמרא quotes a משנה from מסכת ערלה about a forbidden item that got mixed into permitted food. רבי מאיר says anything usually sold by count is too important to be canceled (בטל), so the whole mixture must be burned. חכמים say only six specific items are that important, and רבי עקיבא adds a seventh: a homeowner's loaves. Next, רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש argue about the exact wording of רבי מאיר's rule. 'את' would mean only things that are always counted, while 'כל' would include things that are sometimes counted. The גמרא then brings a ברייתא about an impure piece of a חטאת or of לחם הפנים mixed into a hundred pure pieces. The תנא קמא says it is canceled and רבי יהודה says it is not, but everyone agrees that a pure holy piece mixed into plain pieces is not canceled. The גמרא challenges ריש לקיש from the first half, and רבי חייא בריה דרב הונא answers that the piece was crushed. The גמרא then asks what רבי יהודה's reason could be, and the answer comes on the next amud.

Opening lines

כּוּלָּן יִדָּלְקוּ, דִּבְרֵי רַבִּי מֵאִיר.

They must all be burned; these are the words of Rabbi Meir.

וַחֲכָמִים אוֹמְרִים:

And the Sages say:

יַעֲלוּ בְּאֶחָד וּמָאתַיִם.

They are nullified in one to two hundred.

שֶׁהָיָה רַבִּי מֵאִיר אוֹמֵר:

For Rabbi Meir would say:
