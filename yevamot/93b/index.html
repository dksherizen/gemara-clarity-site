Topic

Who holds one can act on a דבר שלא בא לעולם; and whether one witness about a death is believed in cases of יבום

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a list of sages who hold that a person can act on a דבר שלא בא לעולם, meaning something that does not yet exist or is not yet his. It first closes the story of רבי ינאי tithing fruit before it arrived. רבי ינאי dreamed of a 'bruised reed', and רבי חייא reassured him that the dream pointed to a comforting פסוק. Next the גמרא brings a source for each remaining name: רבי, from a ברייתא about a slave bought on condition to free him; רבי מאיר, from קידושין that take effect 'after' a later event; רבי אליעזר בן יעקב, from separating תרומה with fruit that is not yet ripe or picked; and רבי עקיבא, from a wife's קונם vow on work she has not yet done. Then a new topic begins: is one witness believed that a husband died, so that his widow may do יבום? רב ששת proves from a משנה about reversed testimony that one witness is believed. A second version says that question is obvious, since even the wife herself is believed. In that version the real question is whether one witness who says the יבם died frees the יבמה to marry anyone, and this question continues onto the next amud.

Opening lines

אֶלָּא לָאו, כִּי הַאי גַוְונָא.

Rather, is it not a case like this?

אֲמַר לֵיהּ:

He said to him:

וְהָא אַקְרְיוּן בְּחֶלְמָא ״קָנֶה רָצוּץ״,

But they read to me in a dream: "bruised reed";

מַאי לָאו, הָכִי קָאָמְרִי לִי:

Is it not that they were saying this to me:
