Topic

Laws of the חליצה shoe: intention, material, the meaning of חלץ, and the status of an אנפיליא

Overview

The גמרא first confirms that people really do wear one shoe over another, since רב יהודה once went out in five layers. Next, רב rules that a young יבמה who grew up among the brothers may marry one of them, and the גמרא gives two readings of whether a sandal pulled off without intention matters. רב then teaches that a חליצה shoe must be leather, learned from תחש, though any leather works because the word נעל is repeated. A leather shoe with hair straps is fine, but an all-hair one is only a slipper. The גמרא proves from the words 'מעל רגלו' that חליצה means pulling the shoe off, even though the root חלץ can also mean strengthening. In a side story, רבן גמליאל answers a heretic who claimed ה׳ did חליצה from Israel. Finally, the גמרא resolves conflicting sources about the אנפיליא: a leather one counts as a shoe and a cloth one does not. A ברייתא supporting this begins at the end of the amud, and its ruling continues on the next amud.
