Topic

Confirming a husband's death: certain-death cases, miracles in the great pit, and trusting overheard talk and a גוי's casual report

Overview

This amud continues the discussion of when we may accept that a missing husband has died, so his wife can remarry. It first finishes a disagreement about a man who fell among snakes and scorpions. Then it brings a ברייתא about a man who fell into a furnace or a boiling cauldron. Next it discusses רבי מאיר's story of a man who survived three days in the great pit. The חכמים answer that we do not learn from miracles, and the גמרא explains that staying awake for three days is the miracle. A related story tells how the daughter of נחוניא חופר שיחין was saved from the same pit, and how ה׳ judges the righteous very strictly. A new משנה then allows even overheard talk of women or children to count, and it trusts a גוי only when he speaks casually (מסיח לפי תומו). Through stories of reports about חיואי, a horseman, and חסא who drowned, the גמרא teaches that a wife whose husband fell into מים שאין להם סוף may not remarry from the start, but stays married if she already did. A final story about a גוי's boast continues on the next amud.
