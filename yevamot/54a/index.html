Topic

How much intent is needed for יבום to take effect, and the Torah source that העראה counts as ביאה for all עריות

Overview

The משנה taught that a יבם acquires his יבמה through ביאה whether by mistake or on purpose, by force or willingly. The גמרא looks for a real case of this. A sleeping man (רב יהודה) does not acquire her, and neither does one who fell from a roof (רבה). The case is a man who meant to be with his wife and his יבמה grabbed him. The source is the פסוק 'יבמה יבא עליה'. The גמרא shows that the other lessons drawn from that פסוק, the מצוה and כדרכה, come from other פסוקים. Then it studies רב יהודה's rule about a sleeping man, and explains that a dozing man (מתנמנם) does acquire. It also studies רבה's rule about the man who fell, and brings רבא's rule that intending any act of intercourse is enough. Finally, עולא learns from the פסוק about נדה that העראה counts by Torah law. The גמרא then searches for a source that covers the other עריות, testing pairs of sources. The discussion of רב אחא's objection continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

יָשֵׁן לֹא קָנָה בִּיבִמְתּוֹ.

A sleeping man has not acquired his yevama.

אֶלָּא בְּנִתְקָע —

Rather, it refers to one who was inserted by accident.

וְהָא אָמַר רַבָּה:

But didn't Rabba say:

נָפַל מִן הַגָּג וְנִתְקַע —

One who fell from a roof and was inserted

The steps (38)

  1. ChallengeA sleeping man does not acquire his יבמה
  2. ResolutionMaybe the case is a man who was accidentally inserted
  3. Challengeרבה says one who fell from a roof does not acquire

  5. QuestionWhat is the case where both were forced?
  6. ResponseNon-Jews pushed them together
  7. QuestionWhere do we learn this from?
  8. ResponseThe ברייתא learns it from 'יבמה יבא עליה'
  9. ChallengeBut that פסוק was used for the מצוה
  10. ResolutionThe מצוה comes from a different פסוק
  11. StatementA second ברייתא reads each word of the יבום פסוק
  12. ChallengeThat phrase was already used for 'the typical way'
  13. Resolution'The typical way' comes from a different פסוק
  14. Statementרב יהודה: a sleeping יבם does not acquire
  15. ChallengeA ברייתא says awake or asleep both work
  16. ResolutionRead it as talking about her
  17. ChallengeAnother ברייתא names both him and her
  18. ResolutionThe ברייתא means a man who is dozing
  19. QuestionWhat counts as dozing?
  20. Responseרב אשי defines dozing
  21. StatementFalling from a roof: four payments, no acquisition
  22. StatementIntending a wall versus intending an animal
  23. StatementSource in the תורה that העראה counts
  24. QuestionWhat about the other forbidden relations?
  25. ChallengeWhy we cannot simply learn from נדה
  26. ResponseTrying to learn from a brother's wife
  27. ChallengeA brother's wife is special: the number of forbidden women can grow
  28. ResponseTrying to learn from a father's sister and a mother's sister
  29. ChallengeAunts are special: their prohibition comes on its own
  30. ResponseNew plan: learn from two sources together
  31. QuestionWhich two sources should we combine?
  32. ResponseTrying the pair: brother's wife plus the aunts
  33. ChallengeThat pair fails: both are forbidden as family
  34. ResponseTrying a new pair: the נדה and the aunts
  35. ChallengeRejected: both are forbidden on their own
  36. ResponseA pair with no weakness: נדה and אשת אח
  37. Challengeרב אחא's objection: אשת איש can become permitted
  38. Rejectionרב אחא מדפתי pushes back: are they permitted afterward?
