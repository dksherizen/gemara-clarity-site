Beitzah 39a

ביצה ל״ט ע״א

Learn Beitzah 39a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Beitzah 39a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Borrowed ingredients and ביטול, coal versus flame, and the תחום of water drawn from cisterns

Overview

The amud opens with רב אשי's reason why borrowed spices, water and salt are not cancelled out in someone else's food: they are a דבר שיש לו מתירין. Two ברייתות about רבי יהודה seem to contradict the משנה. Each one is solved by splitting the cases: coarse salt versus fine salt, and a thick dish versus a thin, saucy one. Next, a new משנה and ברייתא teach that a coal is a real object and a flame is not, and they show how this matters for תחומין, הקדש, עבודה זרה, carrying on שבת, and vows. The גמרא explains that a הקדש flame is forbidden only by a rabbinic גזירה, because people do not naturally keep away from הקדש. רב ששת and אביי explain when carrying a flame does make one חייב: when it sits on a chip or shard too small to count by itself. Then the גמרא finds the case where one is פטור: a flame fanned outside by hand. A third משנה ties water drawn from a בור to the walking limits of the בור's owners. רב נחמן and רב ששת then argue whether a public cistern is ownerless or owned by partners, and רבא begins a challenge to רב נחמן that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְרַב אָשֵׁי אָמַר:

And Rav Ashi said:

מִשּׁוּם דְּהָוֵי לֵיהּ

Because it is

דָּבָר שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מַתִּירִין,

a thing that will become permitted,

וְכׇל דָּבָר שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ מַתִּירִין

and anything that will become permitted
