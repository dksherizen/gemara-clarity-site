Megillah 9a

מגילה ט׳ ע״א

Open Megillah 9a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Which languages holy books may be written in, and why Greek is allowed for a ספר תורה (the story of תלמי המלך).

Overview

The גמרא is working on a contradiction. Our משנה says holy books may be written in any language, but a ברייתא says a verse written in translation is not holy, and a scroll only counts if it is written in אשורית. רבא first answers that the משנה means a foreign language written in Hebrew letters, but אביי knocks this down. The next two answers also fall. The first says the sources follow different תנאים, but that fails because רבן שמעון בן גמליאל allows Greek. The second says the ברייתא is about תפילין and מזוזות, which must stay 'as they are' because of 'והיו', but it fails because they contain no Aramaic. The גמרא then says the ברייתא is about the מגילה, which must be written 'ככתבם וכלשונם', and it does have Aramaic words like 'פתגם' and 'יקר'. רב אשי gives another answer: the ברייתא is about the other books of תנ״ך and follows רבי יהודה, who allowed Greek only for a ספר תורה. The amud ends with the reason Greek was allowed: the story of תלמי המלך, whose seventy-two separated זקנים all made the same changes in their translations.

Opening lines

כָּאן בְּגּוֹפָן שֶׁלָּנוּ,

Here, it is in our script,

כָּאן בְּגּוֹפָן שֶׁלָּהֶן.

there, it is in their script.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ אַבָּיֵי:

Abaye said to him:

בְּמַאי אוֹקֵימְתָּא לְהַהִיא?

How did you explain that one?
