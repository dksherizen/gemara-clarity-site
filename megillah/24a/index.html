Megillah 24a

מגילה כ״ד ע״א

Topic

Rules for the מתורגמן and skipping in the reading, and why the מפטיר leads the prayers

Overview

This amud finishes the משנה's rules for reading with a מתורגמן. In נביא the reader may give three verses at a time, unless each verse is its own paragraph. Skipping is allowed in נביא but not in the Torah. The גמרא explains that the three-verse minimum for each Torah reader matches תורה, נביאים, and כתובים (רב אסי), and it gives ישעיה 52:3-5 as an example of verses that are separate paragraphs. A contradiction from the כהן גדול's יום כיפור reading leads אביי to rule that in the Torah one may skip within one topic, and a ברייתא confirms this. A second ברייתא forbids skipping from one book of נביא to another, though skipping forward within תרי עשר is allowed. A new משנה then gives the מפטיר the honor of leading the prayers and lists what a קטן, a פוחח, and a סומא may and may not do. The גמרא asks why the מפטיר leads. רב פפא says it is for honor, and רבה בר שימי says it is to prevent quarrels. The case of a child מפטיר is answered with the honor of his father and teacher.

Opening lines

וּבַנָּבִיא שְׁלֹשָׁה.

And in the Prophets, three [verses at a time].

הָיוּ שְׁלָשְׁתָּן שָׁלֹשׁ פָּרָשִׁיּוֹת —

If the three of them were three separate paragraphs,

קוֹרִין אֶחָד אֶחָד.

they read them one by one.

מְדַלְּגִין בַּנָּבִיא

One may skip in the Prophets
