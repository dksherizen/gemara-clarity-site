Megillah 24b

מגילה כ״ד ע״ב

Open Megillah 24b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Who may lead prayers, read from the תורה, or do נשיאת כפים, and improper practices with תפילין

Overview

The עמוד opens by finishing an earlier discussion. Even according to רבה בר שימי, who explains the rule as a way to avoid quarrels, the משנה's case of a קטן works, because his father or teacher could be drawn into a quarrel. Next, אביי rules that a קטן פוחח may not read from the תורה, because the reason is כבוד צבור. The חכמים and רבי יהודה then argue whether a סומא may lead פורס על שמע. רבי יוסי's story of the blind man with a torch shows that even a blind person benefits from light. A new משנה follows, barring a כהן with blemished or dyed hands from נשיאת כפים, and the גמרא adds more cases: spotted hands, mixing up letters, runny eyes and blindness in one eye. A כהן who is דש בעירו, or whose dyed hands are common in his town, may still bless, since people will not stare. Another משנה bars a prayer leader who insists on white clothes or bare feet, because he may be influenced by מינות. It also lists improper ways of making and wearing תפילין, and רב פפא explains that 'round' there means ball-shaped. A new משנה begins at the end and continues on the next עמוד.

Opening lines

הָכָא נָמֵי,

Here too,

אִיכָּא נִצּוּיֵי אָבִיו

there is the quarreling of his father

וְנִצּוּיֵי רַבּוֹ.

and the quarreling of his teacher.

פּוֹחֵחַ פּוֹרֵס עַל שְׁמַע וְכוּ׳.

One whose limbs are exposed may lead the blessings of Shema, etc.
