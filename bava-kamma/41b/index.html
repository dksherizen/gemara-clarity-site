Topic

The laws learned from ״לא יאכל את בשרו״ and ״בעל השור נקי״: the bans on a slaughtered שור הנסקל and its hide, and רבי אליעזר's exemption from half the כופר.

Overview

The גמרא finishes defending רבי אבהו's view that ״לא יאכל את בשרו״ forbids a שור הנסקל even if it was properly slaughtered. It rejects the idea that this applies only to slaughter with a flint, because a flint is as valid as a knife for שחיטה. Then it explains that ״בעל השור נקי״ adds a ban on benefit from the hide. תנאים who use that phrase for something else learn the hide ban from the extra word ״אֶת״ in ״אֶת בְּשָׂרוֹ״. The תנא who does not learn from ״אֶת״ is backed by the story of שמעון העמסוני, who stopped expounding ״אֶת״ until רבי עקיבא explained it. Next, רבי אליעזר reads ״בעל השור נקי״ as freeing the owner of a שור תם from half the כופר. When רבי עקיבא says this is obvious, רבי אליעזר answers with cases where the ox is not stoned: proof by one witness or by the owner's admission (since כופר is כפרה, not קנס), or an ox that meant to kill something else. The amud ends as רב כהנא and רב טביומי argue which answer came first, and a fisherman משל that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הֵיכָא דְּבָדַק צוּר

Where he checked a flint

וְשָׁחַט בּוֹ –

and slaughtered with it,

דְּעַבְדֵיהּ כְּעֵין סְקִילָה,

since he did to it something like stoning,

אֲבָל הֵיכָא דְּשַׁחְטֵיהּ בְּסַכִּין – לָא!

but where he slaughtered it with a knife – not!
