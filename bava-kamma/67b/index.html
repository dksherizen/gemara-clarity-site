Topic

Does יאוש make a thief the owner? Plus why the four- and five-times payment applies only to an ox and a sheep.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a proof that יאוש (the owner giving up hope) does not make a thief the owner. A stolen animal, like a blemished one, can never be fixed for a קרבן. רבא brings another proof from the word קרבנו, 'his offering.' Since before יאוש this would be obvious, the verse must mean after יאוש, which shows that יאוש does not acquire. The גמרא settles a contradiction in רבא's words: either he changed his mind, or רב פפא said one of the statements. Next, the גמרא asks why the four- and five-times payment applies only to a שור or שה, and not to a חיה or עוף through a word-link to שבת. רבא answers that the פסוק names 'ox and sheep' twice. After testing several options, the גמרא finds that the 'שור' at the end and the 'שה' at the beginning are the extra words. Finally, רב rules that after יאוש a second thief pays double to the first thief. רב ששת rejects this and begins a proof from רבי עקיבא, which continues on the next amud.

אַף גָּזוּל דְּלֵית לֵיהּ תַּקַּנְתָּא;

לָא שְׁנָא לִפְנֵי יֵאוּשׁ,

וְלָא שְׁנָא אַחַר יֵאוּשׁ.
