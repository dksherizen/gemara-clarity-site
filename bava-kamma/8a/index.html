Topic

Which grade of land creditors collect from: how land is graded, עולא's תקנה for lenders, and collecting from buyers of the debtor's fields.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא that lists which grade of land each claimant collects from. The גמרא uses its middle clause to challenge the view that land is graded against the owner's own fields (בשלו הן שמין). It answers that the case is one where the borrower once owned עידית and sold it, and רב חסדא says the same. A second ברייתא seems to contradict the first, and the גמרא settles the clash in four ways. These are: רב חסדא's case, whether his בינונית matches the world's עידית, a dispute between בשלו and בשל עולם, and רבינא's view that the תנאים disagree about עולא's teaching. עולא teaches that by Torah law a בעל חוב collects from זיבורית, but the Sages gave him בינונית so that people would keep lending. Then a new ברייתא covers a debtor who sold his fields. Buyers who bought from him take his place, but when there were several buyers one after the other, creditors collect from the last buyer first. The גמרא explains why a single buyer is different. He bought the עידית last, and he can threaten to return the זיבורית to the seller. It then begins a new challenge that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

עִידִּית וְזִיבּוּרִית –

If he has top-quality and low-quality land,

נִזָּקִין בְּעִידִּית,

damages are paid from the top-quality land,

וּבַעַל חוֹב וּכְתוּבַּת אִשָּׁה בְּזִיבּוּרִית.

and a creditor and a woman's כתובה are paid from the low-quality land.

קָתָנֵי מִיהָא מְצִיעָא:

In any case, it teaches in the middle clause:
