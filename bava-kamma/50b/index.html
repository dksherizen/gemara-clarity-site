Topic

נזקי בור: the ten-טפחים rule, the dispute between רב and שמואל over הבל and חבטה, and רב נחמן's ruling on an ox that fell into a shallow channel.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a short aggadic point: the plural ארך אפים shows that God is patient with both צדיקים and רשעים. A ברייתא then forbids clearing stones from your field into רשות הרבים. It tells of a man who mocked a חסיד's warning and later tripped on his own stones after he sold his field. A new משנה follows: anyone who digs a pit, ditch, cave, trenches or channels in public is liable, and the word בור teaches that a hole must be ten טפחים deep to count as deadly. Below ten טפחים, the digger is פטור if the animal dies but חייב if it is only injured. In the גמרא, רב says a בור makes the digger liable only for its deadly air (הבל), while שמואל says it also makes him liable for the impact (חבטה). Their practical difference is a mound in public, and each reads the word ״וְנָפַל״ his own way. The גמרא defends רב against challenges from the משנה. It explains why every kind of hole is listed, and it concludes that a shallow hole has enough air to injure but not to kill. Finally, רב נחמן declares an ox that fell into a shallow channel a טריפה, and רבא challenges him from the same משנה.
