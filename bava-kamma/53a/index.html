Bava Kamma 53a

בבא קמא נ״ג ע״א

Topic

Liability of a בור for an ox that falls in (רב and שמואל on הבל and חבטה), and רבי נתן's rule for splitting payment between a pit owner and another cause of the damage

Overview

The משנה taught that if an ox fell forward into a בור because of the noise of digging, the owner is liable, but if it fell backward, he is exempt. רב says both falls are into the pit, and the backward fall is exempt because the Torah makes a בור liable only for its bad air (הבל), not for the impact of the fall (חבטה). שמואל holds the owner liable for חבטה too. For him, the משנה's exempt case is an ox that tripped and landed behind the pit, outside it. A ברייתא that holds the owner liable even for a backward fall seems to refute רב, and רב חסדא, רבה and רב יוסף each give a different answer for him. רב חנניה then brings a ברייתא that supports רב. The גמרא asks why the pit owner is liable if the digging noise caused the fall, and answers that this follows רבי נתן: the pit owner pays whatever cannot be collected from the other cause. This leads into רבי נתן's ruling about an ox pushed into a pit, where the split is half and half for a מועד ox and a quarter and three-quarters for a תם ox, with two explanations of that split. Finally רבא applies the same dispute to a stone left at the opening of a pit, and a new teaching begins with איתמר.

Opening lines

וְזֶה וָזֶה בְּבוֹר.

And both this and that are in the pit.

רַב לְטַעְמֵיהּ –

Rav follows his own reasoning –

דְּאָמַר רַב:

as Rav said:

בּוֹר שֶׁחִיְּיבָה עָלָיו תּוֹרָה –

the pit that the Torah made one liable for –
