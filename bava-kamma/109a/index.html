Bava Kamma 109a

בבא קמא ק״ט ע״א

Topic

Whether a son who robbed his father can forgive the theft debt to himself once he inherits, and three ways to explain the sources

Overview

The amud opens with the end of the משנה, which finishes the case of a father who vowed that his son may not benefit from his property, even after the father's death. The גמרא then goes back to the משנה's case of a son who robbed his father. רב יוסף says the son may even give the payment to charity, and רב פפא adds that he must say 'This is what was stolen from my father.' The גמרא asks why the son, who is now the heir, cannot just forgive the debt to himself, since another משנה shows that a theft debt can be forgiven. רבי יוחנן answers that the two משניות follow two תנאים, רבי יוסי הגלילי and רבי עקיבא. He bases this on a ברייתא about a man who robbed a גר, which ends in a disagreement between them. רב ששת objects to this reading and says instead that both משניות follow רבי יוסי הגלילי: forgiving works 'to others' but not 'to himself'. רבא gives the reverse answer and says both משניות follow רבי עקיבא, using the same split.

Opening lines

בְּחַיָּיו וּבְמוֹתוֹ,

[If the father vowed his son may not benefit from his property] in his life and in his death,

אִם מֵת – לֹא יִירָשֶׁנּוּ,

if he dies, he does not inherit him,

וְיַחְזִיר לְבָנָיו אוֹ לְאֶחָיו.

and he gives it back to his sons or to his brothers.

וְאִם אֵין לוֹ –

And if he does not have [enough to live on],
