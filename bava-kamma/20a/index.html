Topic

Animal eating damage (שן) versus unusual behavior, the limits of the exemption in רשות הרבים, paying for benefit, and the squatter's rent

Overview

The גמרא opens with a goat that climbed to eat a turnip and broke a barrel. רבא makes the owner pay full damages for both, because climbing to reach food is normal for a goat. אילפא rules that an animal in רשות הרבים that eats off another animal's back is liable, since that back counts like חצר הניזק. A proof for אילפא is pushed away: the case may involve a jumping animal, which רבא elsewhere treats as unusual behavior (קרן). רבי זירא asks about food that rolls from a private area into רשות הרבים, and the attempted proof from רבי חייא is rejected. Next, רב and ריש לקיש say the exemption in רשות הרבים covers even garments and vessels, because the victim acted unusually first. שמואל and רבי יוחנן limit the exemption to fruit and vegetables, since putting down a cloak is normal. The גמרא then discusses how to measure payment for the animal's benefit: רבה says the price of straw, and רבא says cheap barley. Finally, רב חסדא asks whether a squatter must pay rent when he gains but the owner loses nothing, and the two sides of that question continue on the next amud.
