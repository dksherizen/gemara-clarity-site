Topic

A שליח who changes his instructions, whether a seller must know about the sender, and buying a field in someone else's name

Overview

Someone gave money to a שליח to buy wheat, and the שליח bought barley instead (or the reverse). Two ברייתות disagree about who gets the profit, though both put any loss on the שליח. רבי יוחנן says one ברייתא follows רבי מאיר (שינוי קונה) and the other follows רבי יהודה (שינוי אינו קונה). רבי אלעזר disagrees and says both follow רבי מאיר: one is about grain bought to eat, the other about grain bought to sell. The scholars of ארץ ישראל mock רבי יוחנן's version of רבי יהודה, since the seller never knew about the sender. רבי אבהו explains that a שליח who keeps his mission counts as the sender himself, and he tries to prove it from a משנה in ערכין about a wife's clothes. רבי אבא rejects that proof and, after challenges from רבי זירא and רב אושעיא, settles on a new reason: the family's clothes count as already given to them. Finally the גמרא brings a ברייתא about buying a field in someone else's name. רב ששת's reading, using the name of the ריש גלותא, is rejected, and אביי begins a new reading that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

לִיקַּח לוֹ חִטִּין –

[If he gave money to his agent] to buy wheat for him,

It is taught in one [ברייתא]:
