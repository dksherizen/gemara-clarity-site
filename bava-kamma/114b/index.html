Topic

Whether a thief acquires through the owner's יאוש, offhand remarks (מסיח לפי תומו) about bees and other cases, court conditions from יהושע, and recovering stolen items from a buyer.

Overview

The amud first settles a question about רבי's statement that 'a גנב is like a גזלן.' Did he mean a גזלן as the רבנן see him, so the thief does not acquire, or as רבי שמעון sees him, so the thief does acquire? Two proofs, from the משנה of the customs collectors and from a ברייתא about הקדש, are pushed aside by reading the 'thief' as an armed bandit. רב אשי then proves from רבי's teaching to his son about returning stolen animals 'for their father's honor' that רבי meant רבי שמעון's view. Next the גמרא explains the משנה about a swarm of bees: the finder may keep it only if the owners gave up hope, and a woman or child is believed when speaking offhandedly. רב אשי limits offhand remarks to עדות אשה, and each challenge to this is answered by showing that the case is only rabbinic. A ברייתא teaches court conditions from the time of יהושע that let a person save his friend's property and be paid back for his loss. Finally, a new משנה about finding your stolen items with a buyer leads to a careful definition of when a report of theft is trusted. The amud ends with רבא beginning to limit that rule to an owner who usually sells his vessels, and his ruling continues on the next amud.

וְקַיְמָא לַן כְּגַזְלָן לְרַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן.
