Topic

A craftsman who strays from the owner's order (the carpenter case), and whether dye counts as something separate from the wool

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier definition: 'ugly dye' means leftover dye scraped from the pot. Then a ברייתא discusses a carpenter who made a bench when he was asked for a chair, or the reverse. רבי מאיר says the carpenter keeps the piece and pays for the wood. רבי יהודה says the owner keeps it and pays the carpenter the lower of his costs or the value he added. רבי מאיר agrees with that payment rule when the carpenter made the right item but made it badly. Next the גמרא asks whether dye on wool still counts as a real, separate thing. It tries several cases to find where this question would matter, and rejects some of them. It keeps a case where a thief returns dyed wool after the dye's price fell, and also רבינא's case of a monkey that dyed one person's wool with another person's dyes. Finally the גמרא tries to settle the question with proofs from garments dyed with ערלה and שביעית peels. Both proofs are rejected, because each of those laws has its own special rule from a verse.
