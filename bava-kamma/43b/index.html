Bava Kamma 43b

בבא קמא מ״ג ע״ב

Topic

An ox that kills a free man or a slave: admission, accidental killing, כופר and the thirty שקלים, and liability for killing minors

Overview

רבה explains why an owner who admits that his ox killed a free man pays the victim's value (דמים), while an owner who admits that his ox killed a slave pays nothing. For a free man, admission can obligate כופר, but the thirty שקלים for a slave are a קנס, and a קנס is never paid through one's own admission. רב שמואל בר רב יצחק challenges this from a ברייתא. The ברייתא is then reread so that it only covers cases proven by witnesses. רבא asks whether a fire that kills someone by accident should also pay דמים. The גמרא tests two possible sources for his view, rejects both, and leaves the question as תיקו. רבי יוחנן learns from the extra word 'אם' that כופר is paid even for an accidental killing, and also that the thirty שקלים are paid for a slave killed by accident. ריש לקיש exempts the accidental slave case but agrees about כופר, because only the 'אם' in the payment clause is extra. Finally, a ברייתא learns that an ox's owner is liable for killing children just as for adults, and asks whether logic could have taught this. Its קל וחומר continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מִיהוּ, בֶּן חוֹרִין

However, with a free man,

דִּמְשַׁלֵּם כּוֹפֶר עַל פִּי עַצְמוֹ –

where one pays ransom based on his own admission –

וְהֵיכִי דָּמֵי?

and what is the case?

דְּאִי אֲתוֹ סָהֲדֵי וְאַסְהִידוּ בֵּיהּ דִּקְטַל,

if witnesses came and testified about it that it killed,
