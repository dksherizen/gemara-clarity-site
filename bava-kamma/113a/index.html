Bava Kamma 113a

בבא קמא קי״ג ע״א

Topic

Court summonses and נידוי, heirs returning stolen items, and money from customs collectors under דינא דמלכותא דינא

Overview

The amud opens by limiting when a court may place someone in נידוי for ignoring a summons sent through a woman or a neighbor. If he was in town, passed by the courthouse, or did not come home that same day, the messenger may never have told him. רבא then teaches when a פתיחא is torn up, and רב חסדא rules that a court gives three dates, Monday, Thursday and Monday, before writing one. רב כהנא explains that a woman who is always in town and still does not come may get a פתיחא right away. רב יהודה and רב נחמן list times when no court date is set: ניסן, תשרי, ערב שבת and ערב יום טוב, and during Torah gatherings. Nowadays, because of tricksters, the court does summon people even during those gatherings. The גמרא then returns to the משנה: heirs must return even a well-known stolen animal, or a bed or table, for their father's honor. A new משנה forbids taking change or charity from the funds of customs and tax collectors. Each time the גמרא challenges a source with שמואל's rule דינא דמלכותא דינא, it answers that the collector had no fixed rate or appointed himself, and רב אשי adds that the collector was a non-Jew. That leads to a challenge, which continues on the next amud: רבי עקיבא himself teaches from a פסוק that גזל גוי is forbidden.

Opening lines

אֲבָל אִיתֵיהּ בְּמָתָא – לָא;

But if he is in the city – no;

דְּאָמְרִינַן: אֵימַר לָא אֲמַרוּ לֵיהּ,

for we say: perhaps they did not tell him,

דְּאָמְרִי: (אַשְׁכְּחִינְהוּ) [אַשְׁכְּחֵיהּ]

because they say: found him

שְׁלִיחָא דְּבֵית דִּין, וַאֲמַר לֵיהּ.

the court's messenger, and told him.
