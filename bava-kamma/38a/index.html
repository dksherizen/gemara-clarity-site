Topic

A Jew's ox goring a gentile's ox: the sources, the reward of בני נח, the Roman officers, and עולא's words of comfort

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion about why ״רֵעֵהוּ״ was written by a תם. Then the גמרא quotes the משנה: a Jew's ox that gores a gentile's ox is exempt, but a gentile's ox that gores a Jew's ox pays in full. It asks a ממה נפשך: whichever way you read ״רֵעֵהוּ״, both directions should come out the same. רבי אבהו answers from a פסוק in חבקוק, and רבי יוחנן answers from ״הוֹפִיעַ מֵהַר פָּארָן״. A ברייתא quotes both פסוקים, and the גמרא explains that the second is needed because the חבקוק פסוק may be used for the teachings of רב מתנה (exile) and רב יוסף (the nations' מצוות were 'released'). The גמרא concludes that the nations still get reward for keeping their מצוות, but only like one who is not commanded, since גדול המצווה ועושה. Next comes the story of Roman officers who accepted the whole תורה except this law of the ox. The amud ends with עולא comforting רב שמואל בר יהודה, whose lesson about משה and מואב continues on the next amud.
