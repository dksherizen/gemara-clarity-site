Bava Kamma 32b

בבא קמא ל״ב ע״ב

Learn Bava Kamma 32b step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof colour-coded.

Open Bava Kamma 32b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Welcoming שבת, a wood-chopper's liability for flying chips, and when an accidental killer is exempt from גלות because his act was שוגג קרוב למזיד.

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a short אגדה about welcoming שבת like a bride and a queen, including רבי ינאי's custom of saying 'Come, bride.' Then a new משנה rules that someone chopping wood is liable when a chip causes damage, in every mix of public and private areas. The גמרא explains why all three cases had to be listed. A ברייתא follows about a carpenter whose flying chip kills a visitor. The carpenter is exempt if the visitor came in without permission and liable if he came in with permission. רבי יוסי בר חנינא says the carpenter pays four injury payments but is exempt from גלות. רבא first rejects the reason given for this and then explains it: the killing is שוגג קרוב למזיד, too careless for גלות. The גמרא tests this idea against the lasher who adds a lash and the man who throws a stone into a public area, and narrows each case until it is a plain accident. A second version places רבי יוסי בר חנינא's ruling on the visitor without permission. That leads to a challenge from a smith's sparks, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

״בּוֹאוּ וְנֵצֵא לִקְרַאת כַּלָּה מַלְכְּתָא״.

"Come and let us go out to greet the bride, the queen."

וְאָמְרִי לַהּ:

And some say it this way:

״לִקְרַאת שַׁבָּת – כַּלָּה מַלְכְּתָא״.

"To greet שבת, the bride, the queen."

רַבִּי יַנַּאי מִתְעַטֵּף וְקָאֵי,

Rabbi Yannai would wrap himself and stand,
