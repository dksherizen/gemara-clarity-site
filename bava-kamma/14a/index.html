Topic

Liability for damage in a shared courtyard: רבי אלעזר, רב יוסף's ברייתא, and רבי שמעון בן אלעזר's four rules

Overview

This amud finishes explaining a ברייתא about a borrowed ox. In the first case, the borrower never took on the job of stopping the ox from causing damage. The second half of that ברייתא is a different case, where he did take on that job. The גמרא then challenges רבי אלעזר, who exempts partners for שן and רגל in a shared courtyard, using a ברייתא of רב יוסף that makes them liable. רבי אלעזר answers that the ברייתות disagree, and he quotes רבי שמעון בן אלעזר's four rules, which exempt partners in a shared courtyard. The גמרא settles the clash: one courtyard is set aside for both produce and oxen, while the other is set aside only for produce. רבי זירא and אביי then argue whether a produce-only courtyard counts as 'another's field'. Next the גמרא studies the four rules closely. It reads 'חייב בכל' as full damage, following רבי טרפון, and asks whether the last rule switches to the רבנן. רבינא offers a reading where the whole ברייתא follows רבי טרפון. The גמרא objects that this leaves only three rules, and רב נחמן בר יצחק's answer begins at the very end of the amud and continues on the next one.

Opening lines

וְלֹא קִבֵּל עָלָיו שְׁמִירַת נְזָקָיו.

but he did not take upon himself the guarding of its damages.

אִי הָכִי, אֵימָא סֵיפָא:

If so, say the latter clause:

נִפְרְצָה בַּלַּיְלָה אוֹ שֶׁפְּרָצוּהָ לִסְטִים,

if it broke open at night, or robbers broke it open,

וְיָצְתָה וְהִזִּיקָה – פָּטוּר.

and it went out and caused damage, he is exempt.
