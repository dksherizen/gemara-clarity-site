Topic

Coin loans after a coin change, and רבה's rulings exempting damage that takes nothing away (coin in the sea, effaced coin, slit ear, burned שטר)

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a discussion about repaying a coin loan after the coin was changed. Repayment follows the real metal value, using the rate set by רב פפא and רב הונא בריה דרב יהושע: ten old coins for eight new ones. Then רבה gives a series of rulings where someone harms another person's property without really taking anything away, and each time he is פטור. Throwing a coin into the ים הגדול is exempt, because the coin is still there to pick up, but only in clear water and only if he never lifted it. Wiping the image off a coin by hammering it flat is exempt, but filing metal off makes him liable. Slitting the ear of someone's cow is exempt, since the cow keeps all its ordinary uses and most cattle never go on the מזבח anyway. רבא challenges each ruling, using sources about מעשר שני, a slave who is deafened, and work done with מי חטאת, and each challenge is answered. Finally רבה rules that burning someone's שטר is exempt, because he only burned paper, and רמי בר חמא begins an objection that continues on the next amud.
