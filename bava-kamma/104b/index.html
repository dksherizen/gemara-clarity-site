Topic

Collecting a debt through a messenger, and whether a robber's heirs must pay the חומש

Overview

The amud opens with שמואל's ruling that a debtor may not pay a messenger who only shows the creditor's personal sign (דיוקני), even if witnesses signed it. רבי יוחנן allows it when witnesses are signed. Following שמואל, the story of רבי אבא and רבא shows that a receipt (התקבלתי) is not enough, because the creditor might die and the debt would pass to his heirs. The real fix is to transfer the debt to the messenger through land (קנין אגב קרקע), as רב פפא did. The גמרא then returns to the משנה and proves from several sources that חומש is ממון, so יורשין must pay it. A ברייתא seems to say the opposite: the heir is free of חומש because he did not rob, though he does pay the קרן. רב נחמן answers that the sources differ over whether the robber admitted. After pushback, the גמרא explains the case as one where the father did not admit but the son did, the stolen item is gone, and the father left land. The amud ends with a new problem: a debt like this is an oral loan (מלוה על פה), which cannot be collected from heirs. The answer begins on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֵין מְשַׁלְּחִין מָעוֹת בִּדְיוֹקְנֵי,

One may not send money on the basis of a sign-figure,

וַאֲפִילּוּ עֵדִים חֲתוּמִים עָלֶיהָ.

and even if witnesses are signed on it.

וְרַבִּי יוֹחָנָן אָמַר:

And רבי יוחנן says:

אִם עֵדִים חֲתוּמִים עָלֶיהָ – מְשַׁלְּחִין.

If witnesses are signed on it, one may send.
