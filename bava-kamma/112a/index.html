Topic

What heirs owe for property their father took: stolen goods, רבית money, and a borrowed cow.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an answer that defends רמי בר חמא's rule that an heir is like a buyer: heirs must return a stolen item only before the owner gave up hope (לפני יאוש). Then רב אדא בר אהבה gives another version, in which רמי בר חמא learned his rule from a ברייתא saying heirs need not return רבית money. רבא rejects that proof, because the Torah commands only the lender himself to return רבית, not his son. Next, two ברייתות discuss a thief's children who eat or inherit stolen food. רבא and רב פפא each reread one of them so it makes sense. רבא then rules on heirs left a borrowed cow, and the גמרא connects his ruling to רב פפא's view about when a borrower becomes liable. A third ברייתא learns from 'אשר גזל' that the actual stolen item must be returned, and it brings סומכוס's view that minors are exempt. The amud ends with a story: רבי ירמיה's brother-in-law shuts him out of a house, and רבי אבין's response continues on the next amud.
