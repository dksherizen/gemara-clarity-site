Topic

When does a שומר who lies about a פקדון pay תשלומי כפל (double payment)? The גמרא finishes a teaching about a found אבידה, then quotes a משנה from later in the מסכת and draws two lessons from it: only a טענת גנב, and only with a שבועה, brings כפל.

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a teaching: someone guarding a lost object he found pays כפל, from the פסוק ״על כל אבדה״. Then the גמרא quotes a משנה about a שומר who swears falsely about a פקדון. If he claimed ״it got lost,″ he pays only the קרן. If he claimed ״it was stolen,″ he pays כפל. From this the גמרא learns that only a טענת גנב, and only one made with a שבועה, brings כפל. Two ברייתות argue over whether ״אם ימצא הגנב״ is about the real thief or the lying שומר. Both agree that ״אם לא ימצא הגנב״ is about the lying שומר, and רבא explains how the words show this. A גזירה שוה on ״שליחות יד″ shows that the שומר must have sworn. The גמרא then works out how each ברייתא gets its sources: excluding a claim of loss, the four-or-five payment of רבי חייא בר אבא, and a real thief's own כפל. The answer to that last question, a teaching of תנא דבי חזקיה, begins at the end of the amud and continues on the next one.

Opening lines

בַּאֲבֵידָה, מְשַׁלֵּם תַּשְׁלוּמֵי כֶפֶל;

With a lost object, he pays double payment;

שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״עַל כׇּל אֲבֵדָה אֲשֶׁר יֹאמַר״.

as it is stated: "For any lost thing about which one shall say."

תְּנַן הָתָם: ״הֵיכָן פִּקְדוֹנִי?״

We learned there: "Where is my deposit?"

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: ״אָבַד״,

He said to him: "It got lost."
