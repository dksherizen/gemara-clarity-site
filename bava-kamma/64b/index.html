Topic

The sources and scope of כפל for a thief: which items are included, how ״אם המצא תמצא״ is read, and the rule that one who admits to a קנס is exempt

Overview

The גמרא explains why the תורה needs the words ״אם המצא תמצא״ to include all stolen items in כפל (double payment). The word ״חיים״ would otherwise limit the law to animals. It first reads the פסוק as כלל ופרט וכלל, using רבינא's rule to place each detail between the two generalizations. That method leaves ״שה״ with no job, so the גמרא switches to ריבה ומיעט וריבה, following the school of רבי ישמעאל. Under that reading everything is included except land, slaves and documents, and ״גניבה״ and ״חיים״ teach רב's rule that the thief pays the value at the time of theft. The גמרא then turns to the view that one פסוק is about a real thief. For that view, ״אם המצא תמצא״ teaches רבא בר אהילאי's source for רב: one who admits to a קנס stays exempt even if witnesses come later. The two views assign ״אם המצא תמצא״ and ״אשר ירשיען אלהים״ differently, and they disagree on whether later witnesses make the admitter liable. Finally, אביי uses a קל וחומר to prove that a thief pays double even without an oath. A new challenge, starting from the word ״ידו״, continues on the next amud.
