Bava Kamma 119b

בבא קמא קי״ט ע״ב

Topic

What a craftsman or worker may keep from a customer's materials, and what may be bought from him, closing מסכת בבא קמא.

Overview

This amud finishes the last פרק of בבא קמא, which deals with what a craftsman may keep from a customer's materials. The גמרא first explains a ברייתא about a carder and a launderer. It settles apparent contradictions by splitting the cases: thick versus thin threads, an ordinary garment versus a fine cape, and a garment versus a belt. רבי ירמיה's question about how to count stitches ends in תיקו. Next come ברייתות about buying from a carder, a weaver, and a dyer. Raw leftovers may not be bought, but items the craftsman changed may be bought, because he acquired them through שינוי. The גמרא then works through the rest of the משנה: the launderer's short threads, the tailor's leftover thread (proved to mean two needle-lengths), and a carpenter's chips (where רבא explains that the word מעצד meant different tools in different places). It ends with workers' scraps and wild plants, where it all depends on whether the owner, or the local people, care. The מסכת closes with the הדרן.

Opening lines

וְלֹא יָטִיל בּוֹ

And he should not place in it

יוֹתֵר מִשְּׁלֹשָׁה חוּבִּין,

more than three stitches,

וְלֹא יִסְרוֹק הַבֶּגֶד לְשִׁתְיוֹ

and he should not card the garment along its warp

אֶלָּא לְעֶרְבּוֹ.

but rather along its weft.
