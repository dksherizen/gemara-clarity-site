Bava Kamma 54b

בבא קמא נ״ד ע״ב

Open Bava Kamma 54b in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Which oxen make a pit owner liable, and how the תורה's 'ox and donkey' laws extend to all animals

Overview

The amud opens by asking whether the משנה implies that a smart, normal ox falling into a pit leaves the owner exempt. רבי ירמיה says no: the משנה only lists the weak oxen because they are the surprising case. רב אחא challenges this with ברייתות about a 'בר דעת' falling in, and in the end רבא sets רבי ירמיה's reading aside. He rules that only a deaf, foolish, young, blind or night-walking ox makes the owner liable, because a normal ox should watch where it goes. A new משנה then teaches that many laws written about an 'ox' or 'donkey' apply to every animal, wild animal and bird. The גמרא finds a source for each law, and many of them are linked to שבת through a גזירה שוה. It proves from the extra words 'וכל בהמתך' in the second עשרת הדברות that שבת covers all creatures, working through כלל ופרט and ריבוי along the way. It also explains why people, who appear in the same שבת verse, may still plow alongside an animal. The amud ends as רבי חנינא בן עגיל begins asking why the word 'good' appears only in the second version of the עשרת הדברות, and his question continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הָא שׁוֹר שֶׁהוּא פִּקֵּחַ – פָּטוּר?

But then, an ox that is smart, would he be exempt?

אָמַר רַבִּי יִרְמְיָה:

Rabbi Yirmeya said:

לָא מִיבַּעְיָא קָאָמַר;

It is speaking in a 'there is no need to say' style;

לָא מִבַּעְיָא שׁוֹר שֶׁהוּא פִּקֵּחַ – דְּחַיָּיב;

there is no need to say that for a smart ox he is liable;
