Topic

Whether a לסטים מזוין is treated like a גנב (אביי and רב יוסף, and a rejected proof from a renter), then רב on an animal that fell into a garden.

Overview

רב יוסף holds that a לסטים מזוין (armed bandit) counts as a גנב (thief). אביי challenges him twice from ברייתות. First, a ברייתא says a שומר שכר does not pay כפל, but under רב יוסף he would pay כפל for a false armed-bandit claim. רב יוסף answers that a שומר שכר pays כפל only in that one case, unlike a שומר חנם. Second, a ברייתא calls its קל וחומר from a שומר שכר to a שואל unbreakable. רב יוסף answers that paying the קרן with no oath is stricter than paying כפל after an oath. The גמרא then tries to prove רב יוסף's view from a ברייתא about a renter. It rejects that proof three ways: through רבי מאיר, through רבה בר אבוה's reversed version, and through רבי זירא's unarmed-bandit case. Finally, the גמרא turns to the משנה about an animal that fell into a garden. רב says the owner pays for the benefit of a cushioned fall. The גמרא rejects linking this to רב's rule 'הָיָה לַהּ שֶׁלֹּא תֹּאכַל', and its new explanation continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

לֹא; אִם אָמַרְתָּ בְּשׁוֹמֵר חִנָּם –

No; if you said so regarding an unpaid guard,

שֶׁכֵּן מְשַׁלֵּם תַּשְׁלוּמֵי כֶפֶל,

that is because he pays a double payment,

תֹּאמַר בְּשׁוֹמֵר שָׂכָר –

will you say the same about a paid guard,

שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְשַׁלֵּם תַּשְׁלוּמֵי כֶפֶל?

who does not pay a double payment?
