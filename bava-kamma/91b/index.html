Topic

Whether a person may injure himself, payment for taking away someone's מצוה, and when fruit trees may be cut down.

Overview

The גמרא first finishes a question on רבי עקיבא's words. It explains that a person may shame himself but may not injure himself, and others who do either to him must pay. Next, a ברייתא about a שבועה to harm oneself seems to show that self-injury is allowed. שמואל says that oath means an oath to fast, and the גמרא defends this by saying you can make others fast by locking them up. A second ברייתא shows the oath is about real injury, so the גמרא concludes that the question is a מחלוקת תנאים. It then searches for the תנא who forbids self-injury. It rejects רבי אלעזר's teachings about one's own blood and about tearing clothes over the dead, and settles on רבי אלעזר הקפר, who calls a נזיר a sinner for giving up wine. Next, רב reads a ברייתא that frees a damager who says 'you told me to' as being about an ox or tree that was going to be destroyed anyway. In that case the owner's claim is that he lost the chance to do the מצוה himself, as רבן גמליאל ruled about כיסוי הדם. The amud ends with rules about cutting down fruit trees: how much fruit protects a tree, when the wood's value permits cutting it, and that barren trees should be cut first.

Opening lines

וְקָתָנֵי: הַחוֹבֵל בְּעַצְמוֹ,

And it teaches: one who injures himself,

אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵינוֹ רַשַּׁאי – פָּטוּר!

even though he is not permitted, is exempt!
