Topic

Which creatures and objects a pit owner pays for, the search for the right פסוק, and רבי יוחנן's reading of 'שור חרש שוטה וקטן'

Overview

The גמרא keeps reading the פסוק about a בור (שמות 21:33-34) to work out which creatures a pit owner must pay for. First it explains that the רבנן need the word או to teach that one animal alone is enough. רבי יהודה learns this from the singular word ונפל instead. The גמרא then tries a כלל ופרט וכלל to include all living creatures, but that reading could leave out birds, and the attempt to save it fails. Next it tries ״וְהַמֵּת יִהְיֶה לוֹ״ (anything that can die) and defends it on the question of vessels. That source is dropped because רבא needs the same פסוק for a different law. The final source is ״כֶּסֶף יָשִׁיב לִבְעָלָיו״ (anything with an owner), with ״שׁוֹר״ leaving out people and ״חֲמוֹר״ leaving out vessels. רבא admits that for רבי יהודה the word ״חֲמוֹר״ stays unexplained, just like ״שֶׂה״ in אבידה. Finally, רבי יוחנן explains that the משנה's 'חרש, שוטה, וקטן' ox describes the ox itself, not its owner.

״אוֹ״, מִיבְּעֵי לֵיהּ לְחַלֵּק.

'Or' is needed to separate.

וְרַבִּי יְהוּדָה – לְחַלֵּק מִ״וְּנָפַל״ נָפְקָא.

And Rabbi Yehuda: 'to separate' comes out from 'and it falls'.

וְרַבָּנַן – ״וְנָפַל״, טוּבָא מַשְׁמַע.

And the Rabbis: 'and it falls' implies many.
