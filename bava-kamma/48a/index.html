Bava Kamma 48a

בבא קמא מ״ח ע״א

Learn Bava Kamma 48a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof colour-coded.

Open Bava Kamma 48a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Responsibility for damage in someone else's courtyard: how permission, guarding and intruding decide who pays.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a question from the previous amud: does a בעל חצר who gives permission to bring things into his courtyard take responsibility for them? Two ברייתות that seemed to prove this are explained as talking about the owner of the damaging ox instead. For the eating case, permission decides whether it counts as שן in the victim's domain or in the public domain. For the goring case, the ברייתא follows רבי טרפון, and permission decides between full and half damage. Next, רבא makes a woman who came in to bake pay for the homeowner's goat that died from eating her dough. The גמרא shows this fits with רב: she came with permission, and because baking needs privacy, the owners leave and the guarding falls on her. The amud then brings several rulings of רבא about entering a courtyard without permission: an ox that digs pits, an ox that injures the homeowner or drops dung (which counts as a בור), and a person who intrudes. רב פפא adds that a homeowner who knows the intruder is there may throw him out but may not hurt him. The amud ends by introducing another statement of רבא or רב פפא, which is quoted on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַאי בִּרְשׁוּת

What is there in 'with permission'

וּמַאי שֶׁלֹּא בִּרְשׁוּת אִיכָּא?

and what is there in 'without permission'?

אָמְרִי: בִּרְשׁוּת –

They said: With permission –

הָוְיָא לַהּ שֵׁן בִּרְשׁוּת הַנִּיזָּק,

it is a case of Eating in the domain of the injured party,

  8. ChallengeDoes רבא disagree with רב's rule that the animal should not have eaten?
