Bava Kamma 29a

בבא קמא כ״ט ע״א

Learn Bava Kamma 29a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof colour-coded.

Open Bava Kamma 29a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

אביי's two-part explanation of the broken-jug מחלוקת (stumbling during the fall, giving up the pieces after it), applied to the משנה, the ברייתא's camel, and רבי אלעזר's statement

Overview

The amud opens with the end of a ברייתא: the חכמים say the man who broke his jug is exempt in human court but liable before Heaven. The ברייתא adds that everyone agrees a usual wind makes a person liable for things on his roof. Everyone also agrees an unusual wind makes him exempt. This shows that even רבי מאיר does not make a person pay for a true accident (אונס), so אביי drops רבה's explanation of our משנה. אביי says רבי מאיר and רבי יהודה argue about two moments. During the fall, they argue whether one who stumbles (נתקל) is careless (פושע). After the fall, they argue whether one who gives up his damaging things (מפקיר נזקיו) must pay. The גמרא proves this from the משנה's double wording and applies it to the ברייתא's camel case: the owner stumbled and the camel stumbled over him. רב יוסף and רב אשי explain that רבי יהודה's 'intent' means the owner wants to keep the shards. Finally, רבי אלעזר's statement that the argument is 'at the time of the fall' is read as 'even at the time of the fall,' just like אביי.

Opening lines

פָּטוּר מִדִּינֵי אָדָם,

He is exempt according to human laws,

וְחַיָּיב בְּדִינֵי שָׁמַיִם.

but liable according to the laws of Heaven.

וּמוֹדִים חֲכָמִים לְרַבִּי מֵאִיר,

And the Rabbis agree with Rabbi Meir

בְּאַבְנוֹ סַכִּינוֹ וּמַשָּׂאוֹ

about his stone, his knife, or his load
