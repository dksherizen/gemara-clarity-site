Topic

Whether an animal's mouth counts like the owner's courtyard, fencing against grazing animals, and how an ox becomes a מועד

Overview

The גמרא learns from the case of a dog that ate bread that an animal's mouth counts like the injured party's courtyard. This matters because there was an open question: is a cow's mouth (פי פרה) like the injured party's courtyard or like the owner's? It attacks the 'owner's courtyard' side by asking when anyone would ever pay for שן. רב מרי answers with damage done for pleasure without the mouth. רבינא and רב אשי defend him against מר זוטרא's demand for total destruction. Proofs from a dog or snake set on a person are pushed off, because the argument 'what were you doing in my area?' does not apply where someone was killed. A ברייתא about an ox that kills a trespasser shows this. Then a story about goats that damaged רב יוסף's property teaches that a landowner is expected to fence his field. A public ruling adds that owners of damaging market goats are warned and then made to slaughter them. Finally a new משנה gives the מחלוקת between רבי יהודה and רבי מאיר on how an ox becomes a מועד. The גמרא brings the פסוק-based sources of אביי and רבא for רבי יהודה, and begins quoting רבי מאיר's source, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

כַּחֲצַר הַנִּיזָּק דָּמֵי;

is like the courtyard of the injured party;

דְּאִי כַּחֲצַר הַמַּזִּיק דָּמֵי,

for if it were like the courtyard of the one who caused the damage,

לֵימָא לֵיהּ:

let him say to him:

מַאי בָּעֵי רִפְתָּךְ בְּפוּמָּא דְּכַלְבַּאי?

What is your bread doing in my dog's mouth?
