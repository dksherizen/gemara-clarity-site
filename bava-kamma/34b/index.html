Bava Kamma 34b

בבא קמא ל״ד ע״ב

Topic

How רבי יהודה divides the dead ox without a תם paying more than a מועד, and a new משנה on an owner's liability for his ox versus himself

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a challenge to רבא's reading of רבי יהודה: on that reading, a תם would come out stricter than a מועד. A משנה about guarding is offered as support, but the גמרא rejects it, because guarding (שמירה) is not payment (תשלומין). A ברייתא then shows, through a קל וחומר, that on payment רבי יהודה never lets a תם pay more than a מועד. So רבי יוחנן gives a new explanation: רבי מאיר and רבי יהודה argue about who gets the carcass's increase in value, and רבי יהודה lets the מזיק share in it. Even so, a מזיק may never profit, and the פסוק ״שַׁלֵּם יְשַׁלֵּם״ covers even the case where the ניזק loses nothing. Next, רב אחא בר תחליפא asks whether a תם would then pay more than half. The answer is that only the loss caused by the death is split, which the extra word ״וְגַם״ teaches. A new משנה then lists cases where an owner is liable for his ox's act but not his own, or the reverse. The death-penalty cases are among them. The גמרא discusses destructive acts on שבת and begins a challenge to רבי יוחנן from the haystack case, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תָּם חָמוּר מִמּוּעָד!

An innocuous ox is stricter than a forewarned ox!

וְכִי תֵּימָא הָכִי נָמֵי –

And if you would say: indeed so,

כְּדִתְנַן, רַבִּי יְהוּדָה אוֹמֵר:

as we learned in a mishna: Rabbi Yehuda says:

תָּם חַיָּיב, וּמוּעָד פָּטוּר;

for an innocuous ox he is liable, and for a forewarned ox he is exempt;
