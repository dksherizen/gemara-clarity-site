Topic

Valuing theft payments, the חומש and אשם where there is כפל, and whether a stolen animal that grows up counts as changed.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a ברייתא about a שומר who falsely claimed a deposit was stolen and now owes כפל. רבי יעקב says his חומש is 'covered by' the כפל. The חכמים say there is no חומש where there is כפל, and רבי שמעון בן יוחאי says there is no אשם either. The גמרא first tries to prove רב's rule from רבי יעקב: the קרן follows the value at the theft, while כפל follows the value at the trial. רבא rejects the proof by explaining the case as four false oaths, which create four חומשים. The גמרא then explains why the חכמים still require an אשם, based on how they read 'ואת אשמו' in the פסוק, and how רבי שמעון בן יוחאי reads it differently. Next, רבי אילעא rules that a stolen lamb that grows into a ram has changed, so the thief owns it. רבי חנינא challenges him from a ברייתא, and רבי זירא raises a point about a change of name, which רבא answers. Finally, רב ששת explains that the ברייתא follows בית שמאי, who hold that a change does not transfer ownership, as seen from their view on a changed אתנן. The source for בית שמאי's view continues on the next amud.
