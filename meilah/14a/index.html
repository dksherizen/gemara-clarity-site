Topic

An idol that broke by itself and the nest in an אשירה tree; then wood bought by the גזברים and שמואל's rule that the בית המקדש is built with חולין.

Overview

The גמרא opens with a מחלוקת about an idol that broke by itself. רבי יוחנן says it is still forbidden because no non-Jew cancelled it. ריש לקיש says it is permitted because the owner gives up on an idol that could not even save itself. ריש לקיש challenges רבי יוחנן from our משנה, which lets a person knock down a nest in an אשירה tree with a pole, as if branches that broke off the idol are permitted. The first answer, that the bird brought wood from elsewhere, is attacked from the הקדש half of the משנה. So the גמרא reads the case as branches that grew after the tree was consecrated, and it backs this up from the pole. רבי אבהו, quoting רבי יוחנן, keeps the outside-wood reading and says the pole is for knocking down the chicks. The גמרא then rules that chicks are permitted and eggs are forbidden, and רב אשי adds that chicks still needing their mother count like eggs. A new משנה teaches that there is מעילה on wood the גזברים bought, but not on its sawdust or leaves. שמואל explains that building is done with חולין: the donated money's holiness is moved onto the finished building, and then the money is paid to the workers.

Opening lines

גְּמָ׳ אִיתְּמַר,

GEMARA: It was stated:

עֲבוֹדָה זָרָה שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּבְּרָה מֵאֵלֶיהָ.

An idol that broke by itself:

רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן אָמַר: אֲסוּרָה.

Rabbi Yoḥanan says: It is forbidden.

וְרֵישׁ לָקִישׁ אָמַר: מוּתֶּרֶת.

And Reish Lakish says: It is permitted.
