Topic

רב יהודה's three 'lengthenings,' further lists of three, teachings about בצלאל and wisdom, and the nature of dreams.

Overview

The גמרא finishes explaining רב יהודה's three kinds of 'lengthening' that bring long life. A long תפלה is good as long as the person does not expect an answer as his due; he lengthens it by adding pleas for mercy. A long meal is good because a poor person may come and be fed, and today a person's שלחן atones like the מזבח once did. Staying long in the בית הכסא is healthy only without straining, as the story of רבי יהודה ברבי אלעאי shows. Next come more lists of three: things that shorten life, things that need prayer (a good king, a good year, a good dream), and things ה׳ announces Himself. The mention of בצלאל as a good leader leads to several teachings. A leader must be approved by the community. בצלאל was named for his wisdom ('בצל אל'), and he knew the letters of creation. ה׳ gives wisdom only to the wise. The amud ends with a series of teachings about dreams from רב חסדא and others: every dream has some nonsense in it, and no dream comes true completely. The proof from יוסף's dream is explained on the next amud.

Opening lines

כׇּל הַמַּאֲרִיךְ בִּתְפִלָּתוֹ

Anyone who prolongs his prayer

וּמְעַיֵּין בָּהּ —

and expects it to be answered —

סוֹף בָּא לִידֵי כְּאֵב לֵב,

will ultimately come to heartache,

שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״תּוֹחֶלֶת מְמֻשָּׁכָה מַחֲלָה לֵב״.

as it is said: 'Hope deferred makes the heart sick.'
