Topic

Mistakes in קריאת שמע, workers' obligations in קריאת שמע, תפילה and ברכות, and the exemption of a חתן and one busy with a מצוה.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a דרשה: just as streams raise a person from טומאה to טהרה, tents of Torah raise a person from guilt to merit. Next comes a story about רבי אלעזר, who left his own wedding preparations to hear a ברייתא before רבי יוחנן. The ברייתא says where to go back to if you lose your place in קריאת שמע, and רבי יוחנן adds a limit to it. A new משנה then teaches that workers may say קריאת שמע at the top of a tree or wall but not the עמידה. It also teaches that a חתן is exempt from קריאת שמע, though רבן גמליאל chose to say it anyway. The גמרא asks whether reciting up a tree means no כוונה is needed. It answers that workers stop working for the first paragraph and may keep working during the second. It then shows that hired workers may shorten their davening and ברכת המזון, depending on whether they are paid a wage or only a meal. Finally, the גמרא learns from the words ״בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ״ that someone busy with a מצוה, and a חתן, is exempt from קריאת שמע.
