Topic

כבוד הבריות vs. Torah law: the consolers' row, כלאים in public, and the sources that seem to let honor override prohibitions

Overview

The גמרא first explains the משנה's rule about consolers standing in a row (שורה), who are exempt from קריאת שמע. A ברייתא gives two tests. The תנא קמא asks whether the row can see the mourners, and רבי יהודה asks whether the people came for the mourner's sake. The גמרא then asks a wider question: does כבוד הבריות push aside Torah law? רב rules that someone who finds כלאים on his clothes must take them off even in the market, because 'there is no wisdom against ה׳'. Wherever there is חילול השם, we do not give honor even to a רב. The גמרא tests this rule against three sources. Walking a טמא path for a mourner is explained as a בית הפרס, which is only rabbinic. כהנים jumping over coffins to greet kings broke only a rabbinic decree. The ברייתא saying dignity overrides a 'Torah prohibition' really means rabbinic laws, which lean on לא תסור. The lost-object case is a real Torah exception, but it is about money, so it cannot teach rules about איסור. Finally, the גמרא begins a ברייתא about a נזיר and a מת מצוה, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הָעוֹמְדִים בַּשּׁוּרָה וְכוּ׳.

'Those standing in the row,' etc.

תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן:

The Rabbis taught:

שׁוּרָה הָרוֹאָה פְּנִימָה — פְּטוּרָה,

A row that sees inside is exempt,

וְשֶׁאֵינָהּ רוֹאָה פְּנִימָה — חַיֶּיבֶת.

and one that does not see inside is obligated.
