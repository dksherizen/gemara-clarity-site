Topic

ברכות on seeing crowds, sages and kings, with stories about בן זומא, רב ששת and רבי שילא on gratitude and on earthly kingship as a reflection of heavenly kingship.

Overview

The amud opens with רבי ירמיה בן אלעזר: ruined בבל harmed its neighbors, but ruined שומרון became vineyards that helped them. רב המנונא and a ברייתא then teach that one who sees 600,000 Jews says 'ברוך חכם הרזים', because ה׳ knows every distinct mind and face. בן זומא, seeing such a crowd on הר הבית, also thanked ה׳ for all the people whose work brings him ready-made bread and clothing, and he contrasts a grateful guest with an ungrateful one. After defining an אוכלוסא as at least 600,000 people, the גמרא lists ברכות for seeing sages and kings, both Jewish and non-Jewish. רבי יוחנן urges people to go greet kings. The blind רב ששת shows a skeptic that the king arrives in silence, like ה׳'s revelation to אליהו. רבי שילא, reported for judging without royal permission, is saved and given authority to judge. He then explains the verse 'לך ה' הגדולה' phrase by phrase, ending with the idea that even a minor leader is appointed from heaven. A ברייתא gives רבי עקיבא's different reading of the same verse.

אָמַר רַבִּי יִרְמְיָה בֶּן אֶלְעָזָר:

When Babylonia was cursed, its neighbors were cursed.

When Samaria was cursed, its neighbors were blessed.
