Topic

Dreams follow their interpretation: the story of בַּר הֶדְיָא, אַבָּיֵי and רָבָא

Overview

The amud opens with two rulers who ask Jewish sages to predict their dreams. Each ruler thinks all day about the frightening image he was given, and he dreams it that night. The גמרא then tells about בַּר הֶדְיָא, a dream interpreter who gave good readings to clients who paid and bad readings to those who did not. אַבָּיֵי paid him and רָבָא did not, so the very same dreams were read as success for אַבָּיֵי and as disaster for רָבָא. Several of רָבָא's bad readings come true, including his arrest and two blows from a club. Once רָבָא pays, his readings turn good. רָבָא then finds a book that says all dreams follow the interpretation, so he holds בַּר הֶדְיָא responsible and curses him. בַּר הֶדְיָא flees into exile in Rome, where his habit of demanding a זוז gets him blamed for ruining the king's silks; the story continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

אֲמַר לֵיהּ קֵיסָר לְרַבִּי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בְּרַבִּי חֲנַנְיָא:

The emperor said to Rabbi Yehoshua son of Rabbi Ḥananya:

אָמְרִיתוּ דְּחָכְמִיתוּ טוּבָא,

You say that you are very wise,

אֵימָא לִי מַאי חָזֵינָא בְּחֶלְמַאי.

tell me what I will see in my dream.

אֲמַר לֵיהּ: חָזֵית דִּמְשַׁחֲרִי לָךְ פָּרְסָאֵי

He said to him: You will see Persians capture you
