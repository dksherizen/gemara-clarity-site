Topic

Why blood is excluded from מעילה, נותר and טומאה; the rule that a finished מצוה has no מעילה and its exceptions

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier point: the פסוק calls נותר and טומאה two kinds of 'profaning,' so they work alike. Then the גמרא asks for the source of the משנה's words 'except for the blood.' First it brings three teachings from ויקרא 17:11 that blood has no מעילה: עולא ('to you'), דבי רבי ישמעאל ('to atone'), and רבי יוחנן ('it is,' so blood before כפרה is like blood after כפרה). The גמרא defends רבי יוחנן with the rule that once a מצוה is done, there is no מעילה. It then deals with the exceptions to that rule: תרומת הדשן, the יום הכפורים garments, and the עגלה ערופה. These are two cases that come as one, and each also has a limiting word in its פסוק, so they teach nothing for other cases. Finally, the גמרא explains that the three words about blood exclude it from מעילה, נותר and טומאה. פיגול needs no פסוק, because blood is itself the מתיר. The amud ends as רבי יוחנן asks why the תורה mentions כרת three times for eating שלמים while impure.

Opening lines

בִּשְׁנֵי חִילּוּלִין הַכָּתוּב מְדַבֵּר;

The verse is speaking of two profanations;

אֶחָד פְּסוּל נוֹתָר,

one is the disqualification of notar,

וְאֶחָד פְּסוּל טוּמְאָה.

and one is the disqualification of ritual impurity.

חוּץ מִן הַדָּם כּוּ׳.

Except for the blood, etc.
