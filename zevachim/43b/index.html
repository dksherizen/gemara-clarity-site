Topic

Altar items whose איסור lapses once the fire takes hold, and the ברייתא about ״וְטֻמְאָתוֹ עָלָיו״ and lenient and stringent impurities

Overview

The amud opens by explaining why עולא had to teach that a קומץ caught by the altar fire counts as one unit, just like an אבר. רב אחאי draws a practical rule from this: a פיגול קומץ that is half on the מזבח and burning is brought up entirely. רבי יוחנן then rules that פיגול, נותר and טמא brought onto the מזבח lose their איסור. רב חסדא objects that the מזבח is not a מקוה, and רבי זירא limits the rule to where the fire has taken hold. רבי יצחק בר ביסנא challenges this from a ברייתא that says meat's טומאה can never leave it. רבא, רב פפא and רבינא each try to answer him. The גמרא then studies that ברייתא's proofs that ״וְטֻמְאָתוֹ עָלָיו״ means a person's impurity, and explains רבי's proof from ״וְאָכַל״ through רב יצחק בר אבודימי. Finally it quotes a hard ברייתא about 'lenient' and 'stringent' cases and begins testing what they could mean. That discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַהוּ דְּתֵימָא:

What might you have said?

הָנֵי מִילֵּי אֵבֶר – דִּמְחַבַּר;

This applies only to a limb, which is connected;

אֲבָל קוֹמֶץ, דְּמִיפְּרַת – אֵימָא לָא;

but a handful, which is in separate pieces, say it does not;

קָא מַשְׁמַע לַן.

so he teaches us that it does.
