Topic

Why an offering slaughtered with no stated intent (סתמא) is valid but a גט written סתמא is not, and the sources for each rule

Overview

The גמרא finishes reading the פסוק about vows. A נדר that was not offered properly becomes a נדבה, and you may not change the intent of a נדבה, so the remaining steps must still be done properly. Then רבינא tells רב פפא about a contradiction רבא raised. From our משנה it seems that an offering slaughtered with no stated intent (סתמא) counts as לשמן, but a גט written סתמא is פסול. רבא answers that an offering already stands for its own purpose, while a wife does not stand ready for divorce. The גמרא then looks for the source that סתמא is valid for offerings. Proofs from the wording of our משנה and from both clauses of the משנה about פסח and שלמים are each pushed off. The working proof comes from רבי יוסי: בית דין told people not to state their intent, and the court would never set up a practice that ruins a קרבן. Finally, the גמרא looks for the source that a סתמא גט is פסול. The case of scribes in the market is rejected using רב פפא, and a new proof from the next part of that משנה continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

נֶדֶר הוּא!

It is a vow offering!

אֶלָּא אִם כְּמָה שֶׁנָּדַרְתָּ עָשִׂיתָ –

Rather, if you did as you vowed,

יְהֵא נֶדֶר,

it shall be a vow offering,

וְאִם לֹא – נְדָבָה יְהֵא;

and if not, it shall be a gift offering;
