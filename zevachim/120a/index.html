Topic

Rules of a private altar (במה קטנה) compared with the public altar: the partition's hold, slaughter at night, flaying and cutting, and the time limit

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a question about an offering set aside for a private altar (במה קטנה). The offering was brought inside the partition of the public altar and then taken back out: does the partition's hold on it stay? The גמרא suggests this is the known dispute between רבה and רב יוסף about disqualified offerings that came down from the מזבח. It then shows the question can be asked according to each of them, and it ends in תיקו. A related question from רבי ינאי, about limbs of a private altar's עולה that came down from the מזבח once the fire had caught them, also ends in תיקו. Next, רב and שמואל disagree whether slaughtering at night on a private altar is valid, based on two ways of resolving רבי אלעזר's contradiction between פסוקים about שאול. רב and רבי יוחנן then disagree whether an עולה on a private altar needs הפשט וניתוח, and a ברייתא listing what is different and what is the same between the two altars supports רבי יוחנן. Finally, the גמרא returns to the משנה: נותר, זמן and טמא apply on both altars. A ברייתא learns the time rule for a private altar by comparing it to פיגול, faces a rival comparison to יוצא, and begins answering with a קל וחומר from birds that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

שֶׁהִכְנִיסָהּ לִפְנִים וְהוֹצִיאָהּ לַחוּץ – מַהוּ?

that one brought inside and took outside – what is the law?

מִי אָמְרִינַן:

Do we say:

כֵּיוָן דַּעֲיַילָא – קָלְטָה לַהּ מְחִיצְתָּא;

since it entered, the partition has grabbed it;

אוֹ דִלְמָא, כֵּיוָן דַּהֲדַר – הֲדַר?

or perhaps, since it went back out, it went back?
