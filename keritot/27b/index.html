Topic

A חטאת must belong to its owner and match its exact sin, yet in the קרבן עולה ויורד set-aside money may move between levels as the owner's wealth changes.

Overview

The amud has two משניות. The first teaches that a חטאת is tied to its owner and to its exact sin. If the owner dies, his son may not bring it. The owner himself may not move it to another sin, even from one eating of חלב to another. A long ברייתא learns this step by step from 'קרבנו' (his own offering) and 'על חטאתו' (for that very sin): no father's animal, no father's money, no switching one's own animal, and no switching one's own money. רב שמואל בר שימי explains why money needed its own teaching: switched money can lose its holiness through מעילה, so one might think switching is allowed from the start. The second משנה teaches that in the קרבן עולה ויורד, set-aside money follows the owner's wealth, going down from lamb to bird to עשירית האיפה, or back up. The גמרא shows that the phrases ״מחטאתו״ and ״על חטאתו״ are each needed, because flour is not a blood offering. The amud ends as רבי אלעזר begins a case of a rich מטמא מקדש who set aside birds, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מַתְנִי׳

MISHNA:

הַמַּפְרִישׁ חַטָּאתוֹ וָמֵת –

One who designates his sin offering and dies –

לֹא יְבִיאֶנּוּ בְּנוֹ תַּחְתָּיו.

his son may not bring it in his place.

לֹא יְבִיאֶנּוּ מֵחֵטְא אֶל חֵטְא.

One may not bring it from one sin to another sin.
