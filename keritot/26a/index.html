Topic

The limits of יום הכפורים's atonement: known sins, מלקות, doubtful cases of טומאה, and sins discovered after יום הכפורים

Overview

The גמרא gives a simple source for why a known sinner still brings his חטאת after יום הכפורים: יום הכפורים atones only for a sin that only the מקום knows about. רבא then rules that someone who owes מלקות is still lashed after יום הכפורים. A משנה seems to say otherwise, and the גמרא answers that the משנה speaks of someone who was not warned. Next the גמרא tests the principle on a series of doubtful cases: the יולדת, the מצורע, the נזיר, the סוטה and the עגלה ערופה. Each time it explains why יום הכפורים does not exempt them. Some קרבנות come for טומאה or to permit eating קדשים, not for atonement. In other cases someone does know about the sin, or a פסוק demands a special atonement. Finally, the גמרא asks why a person who finds out about his sin after יום הכפורים must still bring a חטאת. After a long back-and-forth about the word 'knowing' written for the יחיד, the נשיא and the צבור, it learns this from the extra word by the נשיא. אביי then begins to challenge that proof, and his argument continues on the next amud.

a sin that no one is aware of except the Omnipresent —
