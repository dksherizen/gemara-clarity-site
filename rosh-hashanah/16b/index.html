Topic

Why the שופר is blown twice, רבי יצחק's teachings on judgment and the רגל, and the three books opened on ראש השנה.

Overview

The גמרא finishes explaining why we blow the שופר both sitting and standing: it confuses the שטן. רבי יצחק then gives a long series of teachings. A year with no שופר at its start meets trouble at its end. A year that starts poor ends rich. A person is judged only by what he is doing right now. Three things bring up a person's sins, and four things (צדקה, crying out, a new name, better deeds) can tear up a bad decree. Some add moving to a new place, but רבי יצחק credits ארץ ישראל in the story of אברם. He also teaches that one must greet his רבי on the רגל and must be טהור for the רגל, which a ברייתא supports. The גמרא then returns to ראש השנה: three books are opened, for צדיקים, רשעים and בינוניים, with two פסוקים as sources. A ברייתא of בית שמאי describes three groups at the future יום הדין, and its account of the בינוניים continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וְתוֹקְעִין וּמְרִיעִין כְּשֶׁהֵן עוֹמְדִין

and then sound a tekia and a terua again while they are standing?

כְּדֵי לְעַרְבֵּב הַשָּׂטָן

In order to confuse the Satan.

וְאָמַר רַבִּי יִצְחָק

And Rabbi Yitzḥak said:

כׇּל שָׁנָה שֶׁאֵין תּוֹקְעִין לָהּ בִּתְחִלָּתָהּ

Any year during which the shofar was not sounded at its beginning
