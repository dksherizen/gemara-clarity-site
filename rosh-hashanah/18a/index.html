Rosh Hashanah 18a

ראש השנה י״ח ע״א

Learn Rosh Hashanah 18a step by step: Aramaic beside English, every question, answer and proof color-coded.

Open Rosh Hashanah 18a in Gemara Clarity

Topic

Tearing up a heavenly גזר דין: individual versus community, the ten days of תשובה, judgment on ראש השנה, and the months when messengers go out

Overview

The גמרא explains that the rule 'no forgiveness after the גזר דין' applies only to a יחיד, while a ציבור can be forgiven even after its sentence. It then shows that the individual's case is a disagreement. רבי מאיר says a complete prayer decides, רבי אלעזר says prayer helps only before the sentence, and רבי יצחק says crying out helps both before and after. Next the גמרא asks whether even a community's decree can be torn up. It answers that only a decree sealed with a שבועה cannot be undone, as with ה׳'s oath against בית עלי. רבא and אביי say that תורה, and also גמילות חסדים, can still atone for that family, and stories about רבה, אביי and a family in ירושלים show it. רב teaches that a community's sealed decree can still be torn up. An individual, though, has a special time to reach ה׳: the ten days from ראש השנה to יום הכפורים. The גמרא then explains the משנה's phrase 'כבני מרון' and teaches that ה׳ sees everyone in one look. Finally a new משנה lists the months when messengers announce ראש חודש, and the גמרא asks why תמוז and טבת are not included.

Opening lines

כָּאן קוֹדֶם גְּזַר דִּין,

Here, it is before the sentence;

כָּאן לְאַחַר גְּזַר דִּין!

there, it is after the sentence!

הָכָא נָמֵי בְּיָחִיד.

Here too, it refers to an individual.

וּגְזַר דִּין דְּיָחִיד תַּנָּאֵי הִיא.

And the sentence of an individual is a dispute of תנאים.
