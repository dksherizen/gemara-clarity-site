Topic

Which תנא holds זה וזה גורם אסור; רבי אליעזר's view; אביי on ovens, pots and pottery; שמואל's reversed version; and bread baked over coals of forbidden wood.

Overview

Something can come about through two causes at once, one forbidden and one permitted. This is called זה וזה גורם. The גמרא is searching for a תנא who agrees with רבי that such a result is forbidden, as in bread baked in an oven heated with forbidden fuel. A first proof from רבי אליעזר, about forbidden and permitted starter in dough, is rejected, because he may simply follow the last ingredient. A second proof comes from the משנה about אשירה wood. רבי אליעזר argues only about a mixture of the bread, and a ברייתא confirms that he was strict in all Torah prohibitions. אביי then explains the dispute between רבי and חכמים: pottery fired with forbidden wood is forbidden by everyone, while an oven or pot depends on the זה וזה גורם question. שמואל teaches the ברייתא with the names reversed, and the גמרא explains why. Finally, bread cooked on coals is permitted, two אמוראים argue about glowing coals, and רב פפא says רבי forbids the bread when a flame burns directly opposite it.
