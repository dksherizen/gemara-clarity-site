Topic

The end of the dispute about a half-slave, half-free man (חציו עבד וחציו בן חורין), and a new משנה about a slave sent to slaughter his master's קרבן פסח who does not know or forgets which animal to use.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing the משנה about a man who is half slave and half free. בית שמאי argue that he cannot marry anyone, and the world was created for פריה ורביה. So for תיקון העולם his master is forced to free him, and בית הלל change their ruling to agree. A new משנה follows about a slave sent to slaughter his master's קרבן פסח. If the master named no animal, whatever the slave brings is fine, and if he brings two, the master eats from the first. If the slave forgot a specific instruction, he slaughters both animals with a condition. If the master also forgot, both are burned, but they are exempt from פסח שני. The גמרא explains that eating from the first fits people like a king and queen, who are not particular. It supports this with a ברייתא about רבן גמליאל, which also tells a story about a lizard found in the royal kitchen. אביי shows how a slave can own an animal, and he limits the exemption from פסח שני to forgetting after זריקה. Some place this limit on a ברייתא about five mixed-up hides instead. The גמרא then asks why all five owners are exempt, rejects several possible fixes, and begins rejecting a conditional פסח/שלמים offering, an answer that continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תִּקַּנְתֶּם אֶת רַבּוֹ,

You have fixed things for his master,

וְאֶת עַצְמוֹ לֹא תִּקַּנְתֶּם.

but for himself you have not fixed things.

לִישָּׂא שִׁפְחָה אֵינוֹ יָכוֹל —

To marry a maidservant he cannot —

שֶׁכְּבָר חֶצְיוֹ בֶּן חוֹרִין,

because half of him is already free,
