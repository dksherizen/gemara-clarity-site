Topic

Selling deposited חמץ, and רבי יהודה's two תודה loaves on the Temple colonnade: where they sat, why they were called פסולות, and how they signaled the end of eating חמץ on ערב פסח.

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier answer. Even the חכמים agree that a deposit facing unusual loss is sold through בית דין, and this is all the more true for חמץ, which becomes worthless after פסח. The גמרא then turns to רבי יהודה's two תודה loaves, which served as a public signal on ערב פסח. רב יהודה corrects a ברייתא to say the loaves sat 'על גג האיצטבא', on the roof of the colonnade, where everyone could see them. רחבא and a ברייתא add that הר הבית had a double colonnade. Next the גמרא asks why the loaves were called פסולות. רבי חנינא says so many תודות were brought on the thirteenth that leftovers became פסול through לינה, since no תודה is brought on the fourteenth or during פסח. רבי ינאי says the loaves were really כשרות but could not be eaten. After rejecting the lost-animal case, the גמרא settles on a case where the animal was slaughtered but its blood spilled, which works according to רבי or, in a narrower case, according to רבי אלעזר ברבי שמעון. Finally, a תוספתא in the name of רבי אלעזר says the loaves were fit and describes the signal: eat, wait, then burn. אבא שאול's teaching begins at the end and continues on the next amud.
