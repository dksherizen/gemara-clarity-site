Topic

Which camps a בעל קרי must leave and the פסוק behind it; the meaning of 'מיחוי קרביו'; and אגדה about the future, צדיקים reviving the dead, and a Torah source for תחיית המתים.

Overview

Background: In the desert there were three camps: מחנה שכינה (innermost), מחנה לויה, and מחנה ישראל. A מצורע leaves all three, a זב leaves two, and a טמא מת leaves only מחנה שכינה. The גמרא first shows that the משנה's line 'בועל נדה is like a טמא מת' is about camps. It then refuses to conclude that the line about בעל קרי is about camps too. A ברייתא seems to group בעל קרי with טמא מת, so he could stay in מחנה לויה. The גמרא rereads it: he is grouped with the זב, whom he resembles, so he leaves two camps. A ברייתא learns this from the פסוק in דברים. רב יצחק בר אבדימי corrects which camp matches which phrase, and רבינא's challenge is answered from the extra word ״המחנה״. The גמרא then defines 'מיחוי קרביו' from the משנה, where רב הונא and חייא בר רב disagree. A פסוק in ישעיה brought to support חייא בר רב leads into אגדה: the promise that צדיקים will revive the dead, verses that seem to contradict each other about death and about the sun and moon in the future, and a Torah source for תחיית המתים from 'אני אמית ואחיה, מחצתי ואני ארפא'.

Opening lines

בּוֹעֵל נִדָּה כִּטְמֵא מֵת.

One who had relations with a menstruating woman is like one impure from a corpse.

לְמַאי?

With regard to what?

אִילֵימָא לְטוּמְאָתָם,

If you say: regarding their impurity,

הַאי טוּמְאַת שִׁבְעָה כְּתִיב בֵּיהּ,

seven-day impurity is written about this one,
