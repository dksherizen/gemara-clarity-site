Pesachim 25b

פסחים כ״ה ע״ב

Topic

The three sins one may not commit even to save a life, healing with forbidden items, and the אביי–רבא dispute about forbidden benefit that comes against one's will.

Overview

This amud finishes רבי יוחנן's rule that one may heal with anything forbidden except עבודה זרה, גילוי עריות and שפיכות דמים. The גמרא then gives a source for each exception. For עבודה זרה it points back to 'בכל נפשך'. For עריות, a ברייתא of רבי compares a נערה המאורסה to a רוצח. The comparison teaches that a רודף may be killed and that one must die rather than commit עריות. The rule for murder itself comes from סברא, shown by רבא's ruling: 'Who says your blood is redder?' Next comes a story: רבינא treated his daughter with ערלה olives, and he explained it either as a case of danger or as an unusual way of benefiting. The amud ends with a dispute between אביי and רבא about forbidden benefit that comes to a person against his will. The גמרא gives two versions of exactly which case they argue about, based on whether the benefit was avoidable and whether he intended it, and links each version to רבי יהודה and רבי שמעון.
