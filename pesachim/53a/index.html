Topic

Cutting trees and the deadlines for eating שביעית produce, signs of land types, and local customs about selling animals and roasted meat on פסח night

Overview

This amud opens at the end of a ברייתא about when it becomes forbidden to cut down fruit trees during שביעית. רב אסי says that בוסר and גירוע are the same stage, reached when the grape is the size of a white bean. The גמרא then rejects its earlier explanation of how רבי אילעאי could cut down a palm tree, and concludes that the tree's dates never ripen on the tree. Next, a ברייתא lists how long one may keep eating each kind of שביעית fruit: until that fruit is gone from late-ripening places like אוכל, תקוע, בית הִינֵי and צוער. Another source gives holiday deadlines instead. The גמרא answers that both describe the same time, and that the real test is whether the fruit is still found in the field. רבן שמעון בן גמליאל then lists plants that mark each kind of land, and the גמרא explains their uses for בכורים, עגלה ערופה, and buying and selling. A new משנה follows about local customs: selling animals to non-Jews and eating roasted meat on פסח night. The גמרא brings רב's rule against saying 'this meat is for פסח,' and challenges it from the story of תודוס of Rome. The discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

מִשֶּׁיְּגָרֵיעוּ,

from when [the grapes] form kernels;

וְהַזֵּיתִים מִשֶּׁיָּנֵיצוּ,

and olive trees, from when they blossom;

וּשְׁאָר כׇּל הָאִילָנוֹת מִשֶּׁיּוֹצִיאוּ.

and all other trees, from when they produce fruit.
