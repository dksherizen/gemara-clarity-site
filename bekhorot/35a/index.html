Topic

Blemishes on a בכור: on purpose versus by accident, and who is believed about them

Overview

The amud opens by finishing an earlier ruling. If someone made another person's טהרות impure and then died, his son does not pay, because hidden damage is only a rabbinic קנס aimed at the wrongdoer himself. Next comes a new משנה with two stories, a Roman official and children at play. In each one, a בכור got a blemish and the Sages permitted it the first time, but they forbade it when the act was repeated on purpose. The rule is that a blemish made on purpose is forbidden and an accidental one is permitted. The גמרא explains why both stories are needed and debates whether the bystanders' wording can count as encouraging the official. It also explains what the general rule adds: an indirect cause (גרמא) is forbidden, and a blemish that followed offhand talk is permitted. Another משנה permits a בכור that was kicked while chasing its owner, and רב פפא gives two versions about a kick after the chase. The גמרא then discusses making a blemish before the animal is born and a mouth blemish seen only when the animal cries out. Finally, a משנה rules on which shepherds are believed that a blemish was not made on purpose, and רבי יוחנן and רבי אלעזר begin explaining it in two different ways.

Opening lines

טִימֵּא טְהָרוֹת וָמֵת —

If he made pure items impure and died —

לֹא קָנְסוּ בְּנוֹ אַחֲרָיו.

they did not penalize his son after him.

מַאי טַעְמָא?

What is the reason?

הֶיזֵּק שֶׁאֵינוֹ נִיכָּר

Damage that is not visible
