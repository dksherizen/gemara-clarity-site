Topic

Blemishes (מומין) of a בכור: jaw, ears, tails, warts in the eye, and uneven body parts

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a story: the חכמים ruled that a בכור whose lower jaw sticks out has a מום. רב פפא explains this is because the jawbone itself sticks out, unlike a person's protruding lip, which is only a מום for a כהן. A new משנה follows. A doubled ear is a מום only if its two parts share one bone (cartilage), and a goat's tail that is pig-like, or a tail with fewer than three joints, is a מום. The גמרא explains that a pig-like tail means a round one. It also shows that רב הונא misread the three-joint rule: that rule is about a lamb, so a goat needs only two joints. Another משנה lists more blemishes, including a wart in the eye and visibly uneven eyes or ears. After several attempts, the גמרא settles a contradiction about warts. A wart in the black of the eye is a full מום. In the white, a wart with a hair disqualifies the animal from the מזבח and one without a hair does not. The amud ends with a new משנה about a calf's short tail and which leg joint it is measured against.

Opening lines

וּמַעֲשֶׂה נָמֵי

And there was also an incident

שֶׁלְּחִי הַתַּחְתּוֹן עוֹדֵף עַל הָעֶלְיוֹן,

where the lower jaw stuck out beyond the upper one,

שָׁאַל רַבָּן שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן גַּמְלִיאֵל לַחֲכָמִים

Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel asked the Sages

וְאָמְרוּ: הֲרֵי זֶה מוּם.

and they said: That is a blemish.
