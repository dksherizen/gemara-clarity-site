Topic

קנסות for blemishing a בכור and cutting off a נגע: how long they last, and whether they pass to a son

Overview

The amud opens by finishing a challenge against רבי אליעזר. He gives an endless קנס to someone who blemishes a בכור, but someone who cuts off his own בהרת only waits until a new נגע appears. רבה and רב יוסף answer that רבי אליעזר is harsh about property, which people are tempted to damage, but not about one's own body. רבא adds that the חכמים also seem inconsistent, and explains that they always penalize the exact act the person did. רב פפא then asks whether רבי אליעזר said 'יטהר' or 'ויטהר', which matters for a חתן who gets a new נגע during his wedding week, and the question ends in תיקו. Next, רבי ירמיה asks whether the קנס on someone who slit a בכור's ear passes to his son after he dies. Two comparisons are set aside. רבי זירא then proves from a field improved during שביעית that the רבנן penalized only the father, not the son. The amud ends as אביי begins a tradition of his own, which continues on the next amud.

וְנִקְצְצָה — טָהוֹר,

If he cut it off on purpose,
