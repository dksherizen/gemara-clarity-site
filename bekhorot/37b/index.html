Topic

Which blemishes allow a בכור to be slaughtered: why the blemish must be visible and permanent, and the exact measures for ear blemishes (split, notch, hole, dried-out ear)

Overview

The גמרא first explains why a בכור may be slaughtered only for a blemish that is both visible and permanent. 'מום רע' rules out a hidden blemish. A temporary blemish is ruled out by a קל וחומר from פדיון, which a ברייתא on ויקרא 27 supports, or else by the words 'lame and blind.' Next it goes through the ear blemishes in the משנה. A split counts at any size, a notch counts if a fingernail catches in it, and a dried-out ear is defined in two ways. For the size of a hole, the ת״ק says a כרשינה and רבי יוסי ברבי יהודה says a lentil, and the גמרא notes that these two are nearly the same. A contradiction from piercing a slave's ear is answered: a lentil-size hole is needed to permit slaughter, but a tiny hole already disqualifies the animal from the מזבח. The גמרא then asks whether a 'full כרשינה' means a hole the bean passes through. רב הונא רבה answers from a similar case, the משנה about a damaged skull. This leads to a side discussion about whether בית הלל's measure is a סלע, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

נֶעְקְרוּ אִין,

If they were pulled out, yes (it counts as a blemish),

נִפְגְּמוּ וְנִגְמְמוּ לָא?

but if they were damaged or scratched, no?

בָּעֵינָא ״מוּם רָע״, וְלֵיכָּא!

I require a 'bad blemish,' and there is none!

אִי הָכִי, מוּם עוֹבֵר נָמֵי!

If so, a temporary blemish too (should count)!
