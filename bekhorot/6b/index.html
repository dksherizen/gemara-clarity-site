Topic

An animal born unlike its mother, the prohibition of non-kosher milk, and the sources that kosher milk is permitted

Overview

The גמרא first explains the ברייתא: the verse "גמל... טמא הוא" teaches that a non-kosher-looking animal born from a kosher mother is permitted. רבי שמעון disagrees, learning from the double "גמל" that even a camel born from a cow is forbidden. The רבנן use the double "גמל" to forbid camel's milk. רבי שמעון learns the milk from "את", which the רבנן do not expound, as the story of שמעון העמסוני and רבי עקיבא illustrates. The גמרא then explains why a special verse is needed to forbid non-kosher milk at all: kosher milk is itself a surprising permission, so one might have extended it. Next it searches for the source that kosher milk is permitted, rejecting some proofs and accepting others, such as ישי's cheeses and "a land flowing with milk and honey". It also defends expounding the doubled "גמל", even though the list of animals was repeated to add the שסועה. Finally, a ברייתא about a sheep born looking like a goat leads to a question about רבי שמעון's view, which continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

הָכִי קָאָמַר:

This is what it is saying:

דָּבָר הַבָּא מִמַּעֲלֵי הַגֵּרָה

A being that comes from those that chew the cud

וּמִמַּפְרִיסֵי הַפַּרְסָה לָא תֵּיכוּל,

and from those with split hooves, do not eat it (so one might think)

תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר: ״גָּמָל... טָמֵא הוּא״

Therefore the verse states: 'The camel... it is impure'
