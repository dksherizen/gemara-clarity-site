Topic

Which animals have בכור status, mixed-species births, and following the mother's status

Overview

The גמרא proves that among non-kosher animals, only a donkey has בכור status, not a horse or a camel. The first proof comes from the Torah writing ״פטר חמור״ twice. רב אחאי challenges it, and the גמרא defends it from the repeated word ״פטר״. Our משנה's תנא uses that repetition for something else, so רב פפא gives him a different source: a כלל ופרט in the verse. רבי יוסי הגלילי and the רבנן then argue over whether the donkey belongs in that list, based on the words ״ופטר״ and the letter ו׳. Next, the גמרא asks a ladder of questions about a mother that gives birth to an animal that looks like another species but has some of its mother's features. Two ברייתות show that a cow's donkey-like calf with some of the mother's features is a בכור, but the horse cases are not proven. Finally, the גמרא explains that for eating, an animal's status follows its mother, not its own looks. It begins bringing a verse for this rule, and the discussion continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר:

The verse states:

״פֶּטֶר חֲמוֹר״ ״פֶּטֶר חֲמוֹר״ שְׁנֵי פְּעָמִים —

"Firstborn of a donkey," "firstborn of a donkey" — two times

פִּטְרֵי חֲמוֹרִים אָמַרְתִּי לְךָ,

I told you about firstborn donkeys,

וְלֹא פִּטְרֵי סוּסִים וּגְמַלִּים.

and not about firstborn horses and camels.
