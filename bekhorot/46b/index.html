Topic

What counts as birth when only the head or forehead emerges: שמואל's rule for a נפל is refuted, and ריש לקיש and רבי יוחנן disagree about the forehead and the firstborn's inheritance.

Overview

The גמרא opens with שמואל: a נפל's head coming out does not count as its birth, because only a creature with 'breath of life in its nostrils' has a head that matters. The גמרא challenges him from our משנה, which uses the word 'ראשו' for a נפל. It first answers that the word means 'רובו' and was written only to match the next clause about a nine-month baby. That defense collapses, because the rule for a nine-month baby's head is already taught in חולין for animals and in נדה for humans. So the גמרא declares שמואל conclusively refuted. Next, ריש לקיש rules that a forehead coming out counts as birth everywhere except for the firstborn's inheritance, because the Torah says 'יכיר'. רבי יוחנן says it counts even for inheritance. The גמרא challenges רבי יוחנן with sources that require seeing the nose to recognize a face. It answers by reading 'up to the nose,' and by explaining that testimony letting a woman remarry has a special rabbinic strictness about identifying the body. The עמוד ends as the גמרא begins a second answer, which continues on the next עמוד.
