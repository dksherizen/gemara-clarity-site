Topic

The exemption of bought or gifted animals from מעשר בהמה: its source in the היקש to 'your son,' and its limit to animals bought when fit to be offered (purchased fetuses, אתנן bought back)

Overview

The גמרא finishes a היקש: just as פדיון הבן applies only to your own son, מעשר בהמה applies only to your own animals, not ones bought or received as a gift. It then explains why the verse, which is about בכור, can teach this. The word 'כן תעשה' has no use for a בכור, which is holy from birth, so it is moved to מעשר בהמה. The גמרא tests other possible targets for the phrase: חטאת and אשם, then עולה and שלמים, then עולת ראייה. Each is ruled out because only מעשר בהמה is like 'your son' in every way: it is not for a sin, it is not a נדר or נדבה, and it has no fixed time. Next the גמרא asks why רבי אסי rules that fetuses bought in the womb are tithed. רבא and רבי אלעזר answer that 'תעשה' limits the exemption to animals bought when they are already fit to be offered. A ברייתא that seems to say otherwise is either rejected or assigned to רבי שמעון בן יהודה. Finally, an אתנן that was bought back can be tithed, because it was bought back while still a fetus.

Opening lines

מָה בָּנֶיךָ — אֵין בְּלָקוּחַ וּבְמַתָּנָה,

Just as 'your sons' does not apply to one bought or received as a gift,

אַף צֹאנְךָ וּבְקָרְךָ — אֵינוֹ בְּלָקוּחַ וּבְמַתָּנָה.

so too 'your flock and your herd' does not apply to animals bought or received as a gift.
