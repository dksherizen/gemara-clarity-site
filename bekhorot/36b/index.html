Topic

Who is believed about blemishes on a בכור and on מעשר, and when three ordinary people may permit a blemished בכור.

Overview

This amud deals with who is believed about blemishes on holy animals. First the גמרא finishes testing רב's rule that a כהן is believed when he says a ישראל gave him a בכור that was already blemished. It tries to prove the rule from a משנה about a שליח buying produce, but the proof is turned away. רבי ירמיה בר אבא then says the tradition got changed along the way: really it is the ישראל who is believed. The הלכה follows even the first, more lenient version, and the story of רפרם shows that one brazen כהן does not make all כהנים suspect. רב אשי rules that a בכור born with an obvious blemish may be examined even without a כהן present. A new משנה says that anyone, even the owner, is believed about blemishes on a מעשר animal, because he could have blemished his whole flock beforehand. Another משנה lets three ordinary people permit a בכור with an obvious blemish, while רבי יוסי requires an expert. The גמרא limits this to obvious blemishes and to a place with no expert, and begins explaining רב חייא בר עמרם's ruling, which pairs the בכור with undoing a נדר. That discussion continues on the next amud.

דְּאִית לֵיהּ לְאִשְׁתְּמוֹטֵי.

since he has a way to slip out of it.

סֵיפָא וַדַּאי מְסַיְּיעָא לֵיהּ:

The latter clause certainly supports him:

מֵאִישׁ פְּלוֹנִי — הֲרֵי זֶה נֶאֱמָן.

'From so-and-so' — this one is believed.

הָתָם, כֵּיוָן דְּאִית לֵיהּ תּוֹבֵעַ, מִירְתַת.

There, since he has a claimant, he is afraid.
