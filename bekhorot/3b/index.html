Topic

Two rulings of רבי יוחנן: a גוי's share in an animal makes it exempt from בכורה even if that share is only a מום קל, and a mixed-looking firstborn (a goat-like lamb) counts as having a מום קבוע. The גמרא accepts the first ruling and asks what new point the second one teaches.

Overview

This amud opens with two rulings of רבי יוחנן. First, a גוי's share in an animal cancels its בכורה even if removing that share would cause only a מום קל. Second, a firstborn that looks like a different species but has some of its mother's features has a מום קבוע and may be slaughtered. The גמרא accepts that the first ruling sides with רב הונא, but asks what the second one adds, since משניות already count a pig-like mouth and uneven eyes as blemishes. It answers that an eye as big as a calf's is a מום because the animal is a שרוע. It tries to support this from the person-only blemishes of a כהן, but rejects that proof: one big eye and one small eye could also be a plain שינוי, since health or weakness would affect both eyes equally. The גמרא then turns to partnership with a גוי. רבא tells a convert that nobody follows רבי יהודה, who says such a partnership is obligated in בכורה. רב מרי בר רחל sold the ears of his firstborns to a גוי, and his flock died. The גמרא gives two reasons: he removed even the כהן's holiness, which goes further than blemishing a fetus; or others might copy him without a proper sale. A new משנה then teaches that כהנים and לוים are exempt from פטר חמור, by a קל וחומר from the מדבר.

Opening lines

הָכִי אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן:

This is what Rabbi Yoḥanan said:

אֲפִילּוּ מוּם קַל.

Even a slight blemish.

וְהָדִתְנַן:

And that which we learned in a mishnah:

רָחֵל שֶׁיָּלְדָה מִין עֵז

A ewe that gave birth to a goat of sorts
