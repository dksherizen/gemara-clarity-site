Topic

How we know the לויים keep their exemption from בכורה for all generations, and how we know the wilderness redemption of firstborn donkeys was done with a שה (lamb).

Overview

The גמרא first learns from the word ״וְהָיוּ״ that the לויים stay exempt from בכורה in every generation. Next it asks how we know the firstborn donkeys of ישראל in the מדבר were redeemed with the לויים's lambs. A comparison to כסף is rejected, and a פסוק that joins בכור אדם with בכור בהמה טמאה proves it instead. רבי חנינא teaches that one לוי's lamb exempted several donkeys. אביי and a careful reading of ״בֶּהֱמַת הַלְוִיִּם תַּחַת בְּהֶמְתָּם״ prove this, and רבא shows it is the reason behind the משנה that lets one lamb be reused. Then רבי יוחנן and ריש לקיש argue whether firstborn born in the מדבר were holy. They trade proofs from a משנה about the firstborn doing the עבודה, a ברייתא about the day the משכן was set up, and a ברייתא about three places of holiness. רב פפא's question about the counting of the firstborn in the מדבר forces a new version of the dispute: both agree they became holy, and the question is whether that holiness stopped. ריש לקיש's side of this new version continues on the next amud.

Opening lines

וּלְדוֹרוֹת מְנָלַן?

And for later generations — from where do we know?

אָמַר קְרָא: ״וְהָיוּ לִי הַלְוִיִּם״,

The verse says: "And the Levites shall be Mine,"

״וְהָיוּ״ — בַּהֲוָויָיתָן יְהוּ.

"Shall be" — they shall remain in their state.

וּמִמַּאי דִּבְשֶׂה?

And from where do we know that it was with a lamb?
